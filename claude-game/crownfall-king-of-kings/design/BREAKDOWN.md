# Crownfall: King of Kings: storyboard breakdown

Sources (the author's own files, both by Osita Miles, dated 24 November 2026):

- Screenplay, fifth draft, 11 PDF pages: rendered to `design/pages/page-01.png` to `page-11.png` (110 dpi), text in `design/screenplay.txt`.
- Visual bible, version 1, 18 PDF pages: rendered to `design/bible/page-01.png` to `page-18.png` (90 dpi), text in `design/bible.txt`.

Every page was read as an image. The text files were used only to copy dialogue exactly. All dialogue below and in `scenes.json` is copied character for character from the screenplay. A script checks it against `screenplay.txt`.

Conventions used here:

- **Script page** is the number printed on the script page. Script page 1 is PDF page 3 (it carries no number). Script pages 2 to 9 are PDF pages 4 to 11, so add 2.
- **Bible p.N** is the bible's printed page number, which is also its PDF page number.
- **Shot numbers** (for example 3.4) are the bible's, taken from the author's timed shot list. That list is cited in the bible but was not supplied, so only shots the bible names are tied to beats.
- The bible tags its notes **SCRIPT** (in the fifth draft), **ATLAS** (from The Ash and Aether Linguistic Atlas) or **PROPOSED** (a design suggestion awaiting the author's approval). Those tags are kept here, because proposals are not yet canon.

## 1. Logline and tone

**Logline.** Neither document has a logline. This one is built only from the screenplay: *Years into a war, Cael, King of Aethelgard, stops a falling block with an open hand to save a young signal keeper at the foot of a besieged capital. Then he uses the same power to hold the Crown open for a second salvo and brings the whole city down. At sunrise a Korvenian and Kwind-09 fleet arrives and shelters the survivors from him, and he must decide whether to stop.*

**Epigraph** (script page 1, before FADE IN): *"Blood remembers what stone forgets." - a saying of Aethelgard.* The bible (p.14) gives the same line as the motto of Aethelgard, "The Blood-Throne".

**Tone, in the bible's words:**

- Title of p.2: **"Cold, dusty and patient"** "Every frame keeps a person small against something vast, and bright colour is rationed to five things."
- The look in one paragraph (p.2): "Pre-dawn slate and ash, then a low clean sun through dust. Wide frames at eye level, small figures against terraces, stone and hulls. Deep charcoal shadows that still hold detail. Light from a lamp, gun-flash and sunrise only. One white lamp, one violet light, blood-red banners held back until the last half minute. Fine grain. No glow, no lens flare."
- Arc of the colour keys (p.10): "Cold, then burning, then gold. Scenes 1 to 5 are slate. Scene 6 is the only orange frame. From scene 7 the film is ochre and gold." "The brightest thing is always small." "Every key has someone in it. The smallest figure in each frame is the one the scene is about."
- Reference: Denis Villeneuve's films (p.3), as nine habits: human anchor, empty sky, held wide, designed frames, low exposure, eye level wide and medium, natural sources, staged eyelines, emotional target.

**Languages** (script page "A NOTE ON LANGUAGES", PDF p.2). The film is spoken in Common Imperial, written as English. Native tongues: Aethelgard, Old Aethel (battle cry in High Drakonic); Solforiz, Kharzani; Korvenia, High Korvenic. Lines marked (subtitled) are spoken in the native tongue (the line under TRANSLATION) and subtitled with the English. Subtitles use the title face in bone, low in frame (bible p.18).

## 3. Scene list

### Scene 1: INT. KWIND-09 - FOUNDRY - NIGHT
Script p.1 (PDF 3). About 24 s. Bible shots 1.1-1.4. Colour key: bible p.5 (top).

**Characters:** a WORKER (Kwind-09, seen only as a silhouette); soot-black hands (Kwind-09); pale gloved hands (Korvenian; bible p.16 calls the ward officer's glove "the foundry's gloves again"). No faces (bible pp.5, 15).

**Beats**
1. Dark. A mechanical STROKE. Another. (1.1: the pulse, sound first.)
2. SUPER: "YEARS EARLIER."
3. A copper coupling descends on chains. Nothing gives its size until a WORKER walks under it: the coupling is four times his height. (1.2: the anchor.)
4. Soot-black hands lock the copper. Pale gloved hands close a silver ring around it. Violet light runs the seam. (1.3. Violet appears at 0:12 and not again until 6:29.)
5. Behind it, more couplings wait in the dark. Rows. No one speaks. CUT TO:

**Dialogue:** none.

**Must be seen**
- The coupling at four times the worker's height, hung on chains. The worker is the only measure of scale.
- Hands only: soot-black on copper, pale gloves closing the silver ring.
- One violet seam: "the only warning of the ward" (p.4).
- Rows of identical couplings receding into black. Nine-tenths of the frame is black. Furnace glow from below (the only under-lighting allowed in the film, p.11). Copper lit from one side.

### Scene 2: EXT. SOLFORIZ - THE FALLEN GATE - PRE-DAWN
Script p.1 (PDF 3). About 26 s. Bible shots 2.1-2.4. Colour key: bible p.5 (bottom).

**Characters:** no named characters. The Aethelgard army (wounded men walking back along the causeway), civilians on the great stair, and a Solforiz battery in the high arcades (heard chanting).

**Beats**
1. SUPER: "SOLFORIZ. YEAR 56."
2. The stroke is now a city's pulse. Four beats. Always four. (2.1.)
3. Wide: a causeway crosses a plain of fused glass to a broken gate. Beyond it the capital climbs, terrace on terrace, to the pressureworks at its summit: THE CROWN. (2.1: the empty sky, two-thirds of the frame.)
4. Aethelgard holds the gate and the forecourt behind it. Solforiz holds everything above: an inner wall, the terraces, the guns.
5. Between them: abandoned cannon, and wounded men walking back along the causeway under a mourning horn.
6. Over the gatehouse burns a white truce lamp (2.3). Under it the fighting has paused, more or less, and civilians are coming down the great stair.
7. One battery in the high arcades FIRES anyway, to a chanted order.

**Dialogue** (chanted inside the action line; Kharzani, *not subtitled*; meaning "Ignite!"; say "ih-RIQ"):
- BATTERY IN THE HIGH ARCADES (chanted order): "Ihriq! Ihriq!"

**Must be seen**
- The horizon in the lower third and two-thirds of the frame sky, so the fleet later has a sky to fill.
- The causeway as a long diagonal over the glass plain to the broken gate. The capital as a stepped ziggurat rising to the Crown.
- The white truce lamp: the brightest point in the film until it bursts.
- Amber lamps on the terraces. The bible proposes that they breathe on the four-beat pulse; this is not in the script (p.14, p.18).
- Abandoned cannon in the foreground. Wounded men walking back. A battery firing in spite of the truce.

### Scene 3: EXT. GATE FORECOURT - FOOT OF THE GREAT STAIR - CONTINUOUS
Script pp.1-2 (PDF 3-4). About 54 s. Bible shots 3.1-3.9. Colour key: bible p.6 (top), "The held stone". Camera "Near": bible p.12.

**Characters:** LIORA, the BOY, CAEL, the STANDARD-BEARER, civilians.

**Beats**
1. Where the great stair meets the forecourt stands a stone arch: a signal station, its mirrored storm lantern lit. (3.1: the mirror.)
2. LIORA, a young signal keeper, brings civilians down the damaged stair, one hand raised in the shelter sign. A small BOY keeps hold of her coat.
3. A Solforiz shell falls short and hits the terrace above her. A block tears loose.
4. It stops. Dust keeps falling past it. The battle thins to a low hum, "like pressure in the ears". (3.3: Liora under the block, the anchor. One of the six long holds.)
5. CAEL, King of Aethelgard, stands ten paces off with one hand open. Dented armour. An arm already bandaged. He is breathing too hard for a man standing still. (3.4: open hand, "it saves".)
6. He comes to her, drops to her level and takes her wrist. (3.5: the wrist, in shallow focus, from Liora's eye level, which is the only reason Cael is seen from below.)
7. He speaks (below).
8. She finds her feet. He pulls her clear (3.6). Only then does he close his hand, and the block comes down where she lay.
9. He stays with her a moment. Then the boy: he straightens the child's respirator and tests the seal with two fingers.
10. A young STANDARD-BEARER has seen it all, a bound banner on his shoulder. He was leaving the wall. He turns back. (3.8: the bound banner.)
11. Cael goes on to the gatehouse. Liora sets the boy under the arch, in sight of the truce lamp. (3.9: the truce lamp.)
12. She tells him to stay (below). Already turning for the stair, she says the rest to no one, "the way it has always been said".
13. She goes back up for the others.

**Dialogue**
- CAEL: "Look at me. Find your feet."
- LIORA *(in Kharzani, subtitled)*: "Stay." Spoken: "Iwqif."
- LIORA (CONT'D) *(under her breath)*: "May the child survive in the ash." Spoken: "Lu=yaslam al-walad fi al-rash."

(Appendix: "Iwqif" is the command form of W-Q-F; the second line is from the lament "Six Seconds of Sea". Say: iw-QIF. lu-yas-LAM al-wa-LAD fi al-RASH.)

**Must be seen**
- The arch signal station and its lit mirrored lantern. The Atlas canon is "storm lanterns with an inverted flame" (p.14).
- The block stopped in mid-air above Liora, with dust still falling past it. Nothing glows: "The object simply stops" (p.17). In the key (p.6) the light is flat with no hard shadows, and the streaks are falling dust, not rain.
- Cael ten paces off, hand open, bandaged arm (key p.6: standing silhouette at frame right, arm stretched toward the block).
- A close shot of his grip on her wrist at her eye level. This size and angle must match 10.6.
- The block landing only when his hand closes.
- Two fingers testing the seal of the boy's respirator, which is a size too big.
- The standard-bearer and his bound banner turning back.

### Scene 4: INT. SHATTERED GATEHOUSE - CONTINUOUS
Script pp.2-4 (PDF 4-6). About 87 s. Bible shots 4.1-4.11. Colour key: bible p.6 (bottom).

**Characters:** CAEL, the ENVOY of Solforiz, the CAPTAIN, a wounded SOLDIER, the NAVIGATOR (stretcher), a wounded soldier of Aethelgard (stretcher), the door guard, stretcher bearers. The BOY is seen through the breach.

**Beats**
1. A command post in a ruin. One wall is gone. Through the breach: the forecourt, the arch, the boy. (4.1: the frame inside the frame.)
2. An ENVOY of Solforiz waits, immaculate. Cael's CAPTAIN stands by. A wounded SOLDIER reports.
3. The envoy starts to speak and Cael cuts him off for the soldier. "The envoy stops. No one has made him wait for a soldier."
4. The soldier finishes his report. Cael grips the table; it takes him a breath to answer.
5. Two stretchers come in: a soldier of Aethelgard, then a NAVIGATOR of Solforiz, his eyes filmed like glass. The door guard looks to Cael. Cael nods. Both are set down inside.
6. Under the campaign map lie three judgments, under three different seals. Their folds have been mended many times. (4.5, 4.6: the seals; 4.6 in shallow focus.)
7. The envoy asks for another council. Cael puts a finger on one of the seals.
8. Through the breach: the boy, small under the arch (4.7).
9. "Nor yours." No answer. Cael's hand closes on the table. He makes it open. (4.8: "he forces a closed hand open".)
10. The envoy offers safety. "The envoy does not say it twice."
11. The gun in the high arcade FIRES again. Dust falls on the judgments (4.10). The envoy shuts his eyes. It is one of his.
12. Cael sends him back (4.11).

**Dialogue**
- SOLDIER: "Third assault, Cyning. The inner batteries still hold."
- ENVOY: "The Ash-Caliph --"
- CAEL: "He was speaking."
- SOLDIER: "Half the company reached the wall."
- SOLDIER *(beat)*: "The slow half came back."
- CAEL: "Clear the road. Bring them in."
- ENVOY: "The Ash-Caliph asks for another council."
- CAEL: "He wagered his crown and lost it. That is his seal."
- ENVOY: "The Ash-Caliph will not live beneath your law."
- CAEL: "The same law as that boy."
- ENVOY: "You do not own his people."
- CAEL: "They have never been his."
- ENVOY: "Nor yours."
- ENVOY (CONT'D): "Come to the council. You would be safe."
- CAEL: "Safe."
- CAEL (CONT'D): "I was promised that before."
- ENVOY: "Give him time."
- CAEL: "I gave him years."
- CAEL (CONT'D): "Look what he built with them."
- CAEL (CONT'D) *(to his captain)*: "Return him to his Caliph."

("Cyning" (king) and "Ash-Caliph" are Atlas terms used in Common speech.)

**Must be seen**
- The breach holds the boy and the arch in every wide (4.1, 4.7, and 5.4 in the next scene).
- Lit by the breach alone, about two stops under. Half of each face is lost. The envoy reads pale and Cael reads black. Grain is a little heavier here (p.18).
- The envoy's unmarked pale robe: "the one clean cloth in the film" (p.6). He stands against the dark wall.
- The table and campaign map, and three judgments with three different seals. "Paper is the only old thing on the table" (p.17).
- Cael's finger on a seal. His hand closing on the table and forced open.
- The blind navigator on his stretcher, in profile.
- Dust falling on the judgments when the arcade gun fires. Before the fall, dust only falls (p.11).
- In the wounded soldier's two-shot with Cael, the envoy is left out of frame (p.16).

### Scene 5: INT. GATEHOUSE - THE BREACH - LATER
Script pp.4-5 (PDF 6-7). About 58 s. Bible shots 5.1-5.9. Colour key: bible p.7 (top), "Break the Crown!". Camera "Clear": bible p.12.

**Characters:** CAEL, the CAPTAIN, the NAVIGATOR, the STANDARD-BEARER, Aethelgard's gun crews and lines. Through the breach: the ENVOY on the stair, the BOY under the arch, and LIORA among the lamps on the stair.

**Beats**
1. Through the breach: the envoy, small on the great stair, reaches the inner wall. Its gate shuts behind him.
2. On his stretcher, the blind navigator turns his face up, to where the truce lamp hangs. (5.2. The appendix says he turns to a thing a moment before it is struck.)
3. A single shot from the terraces. The lamp bursts. (5.3: white leaves the film.)
4. The captain shows Cael the causeway behind them. Open. Empty.
5. The captain offers withdrawal. Cael looks at the boy under the arch (5.4) and answers.
6. Every gun on the terraces opens up. A strike tears into Aethelgard's lines. Men who were standing are not.
7. Cael looks up, past the terraces, to the Crown. The captain follows his eyes and knows what he is about to be asked.
8. The captain warns him that the stairs are still full. They are: lamps, moving down. Liora among them.
9. Cael draws breath to speak. Loses it. Steadies. "I know." (5.7: his face fully readable.)
10. "Then give them time": the envoy's word, in his own captain's mouth. The open road is still behind them. Cael looks at it once.
11. "Bring it down." (5.8: a held wide. The bible says nobody gets a close-up for this line.)
12. The captain does not move. He warned him. He stayed. Now the order must pass through him to become fire. He turns to the guns and gives it.
13. Aethelgard's batteries swing up and FIRE. The standard-bearer reaches for the bindings of his banner. The captain's look stops his hand. Not yet. (5.9.)

**Dialogue**
- CAPTAIN: "We can withdraw with the wounded."
- CAEL: "And leave him to finish this?"
- CAPTAIN: "Those works feed the batteries and the lower city. The stairs are still full."
- CAEL: "I know."
- CAPTAIN: "Then give them time."
- CAEL: "Bring it down."
- CAPTAIN *(in Old Aethel, subtitled)*: "Break the Crown!" Spoken: "Brecath thone corune!"

(Appendix: built from Atlas grammar, a plural command with an accusative object. It echoes "Se corun brec-de", "the Crown shattered". Say: BRE-cath THO-ne CO-ru-ne, with th as in thin.)

**Must be seen**
- The envoy small on the great stair, and the inner-wall gate shutting behind him.
- The navigator turning his face up a moment before the lamp is shot. The truce lamp bursting.
- The causeway behind the gatehouse: the open, empty road.
- Lamps moving down the stairs, with Liora among them.
- The order held wide. Aethelgard's batteries firing, with flashes that clip to white for one frame. "Amber now means fire from both sides" (p.7).
- In the key (p.7): three gun barrels at lower left, their shells arcing toward the ziggurat at right; Cael a small silhouette with a raised arm; the standard-bearer beside him with a tall pole and a bound dark-red banner.

### Scene 6: EXT. THE CROWN / THE BREACH / THE STAIR - CONTINUOUS
Script pp.5-6 (PDF 7-8). About 46 s. Bible shots 6.1-6.10. Colour key: bible p.7 (bottom), "The open hand" (also the cover, p.1). Camera "Clear": bible p.12.

**Characters:** CAEL, the NAVIGATOR, LIORA, civilians on the stair.

**Beats**
1. THE CROWN. The works take the salvo. Armoured shutters close over them, plate by plate. The city is protecting itself. The last shutter starts down across the last opening.
2. It stops. The air at its edge warps. The battle thins to that hum again.
3. THE BREACH. Cael stands in the gap, black against the gun-flash, bandaged arm raised, hand open. His fingers shake. Blood soaks the binding. He holds. (6.3: open hand, "it destroys". Cael is centred and the shot is held long.)
4. Behind him the blind navigator sits bolt upright, his face to the Crown, and strikes the stretcher rail. ONE. TWO. (6.4.)
5. THE STAIR. LIORA'S POV, from the lower flight: the king at the breach. That open hand. She knows what that hand does. She looks up: the stalled shutter. (6.5: a flat, long-lens view, "she sees shape, not detail". Her look from the hand up to the shutter is one move.)
6. THREE. FOUR.
7. The second salvo goes in.
8. Only then does the hand come down.
9. THE CROWN. The shutter drops shut. Fire shows around its edges, then comes through. A tower leans and falls across the great retaining wall. The wall gives.
10. The pulse of the city stumbles. Three beats. Two. None. (6.10: the city's lamps go out together. Furnace amber leaves the film.)

**Dialogue:** none. The appendix says the navigator's four even strikes are "the Solforiz clapper code for a Seen report, a sight four beats ahead."

**Must be seen**
- Shutters closing plate by plate over the Crown, the last one stalled, and the air warping at its edge.
- Cael as a pure silhouette with no fill light: bandaged arm raised, hand open, fingers shaking, blood through the binding. The stalled shutter is the only other bright thing in the frame, so the eye joins the hand to it (p.7). In the key, the jagged edges of the breach frame him and the ziggurat sits right of frame with a bright slit at its summit.
- The navigator counting on the stretcher rail.
- Liora's long-lens view from the lower flight.
- The hand coming down only after the second salvo.
- Fire through the shutter, and a tower falling across the great retaining wall. Ochre dust is never seen before 6.9 (p.4).
- The lamps going dark as the pulse stops. This is the film's only orange scene.

### Scene 7: EXT. SOLFORIZ - THE FALL - CONTINUOUS
Script p.6 (PDF 8). About 25 s. Bible shots 7.1-7.4. Colour key: bible p.8 (top).

**Characters:** LIORA, the BOY, "a dozen others" (civilians).

**Beats**
1. Liora is already running, driving the last of them down ahead of her. Dust takes the flight behind her. (7.1, handheld.)
2. She reaches the arch. The boy. A dozen others. The lantern lies smashed. She pulls its mirror free and keeps it. (7.2, handheld.)
3. Above the broken wall a terrace settles. Its houses lean together and go into dust. The stair goes. Then the whole upper city comes down. (7.3: a held wide.)
4. When the dust thins, the skyline is gone. (7.4.)

**Dialogue:** none.

**Must be seen**
- Liora running with dust swallowing the flight behind her. These are the only handheld shots in the film.
- The smashed lantern, and Liora keeping its mirror.
- The upper city coming down: houses leaning together, the stair going. The cause is seen. Dust rises faster than the buildings fall, and there is no fireball (p.17). The dust cloud is lit from above. This is where ochre enters the film.
- In the key (p.8): the stair climbing from lower left with small figures on it, and four tall towers with cross-arms toppling to the right into a lit dust cloud.
- The empty skyline afterwards.

### Scene 8: EXT. FORECOURT - THE RUINS - SUNRISE
Script pp.6-7 (PDF 8-9). About 35 s. Bible shots 8.1-8.6. Colour key: bible p.8 (bottom).

**Characters:** the STANDARD-BEARER, CAEL, the CAPTAIN, LIORA, the men of Aethelgard. The BOY is implied under the arch: he was there in scene 7, and is seen there again in scene 10.

**Beats**
1. Silence on both sides. Air hisses from broken pipes. (8.1: the first direct sun in the film, and the empty sky.)
2. The men of Aethelgard do not agree on what they have seen. One sinks against a gun, simply glad the batteries have stopped. One cannot look away from the terraces.
3. The standard-bearer lifts his bound banner and finds his voice (8.2).
4. Nobody answers. Not Cael. The standard-bearer hears the silence and lowers the pole.
5. The silence holds. Only the pipes. (8.4: a held wide.)
6. Under the arch, grit falls from the stone. Liora looks up.
7. Beneath the hiss, a PULSE. Slower than the city's. Heavier. (8.5.)
8. The captain turns from the ruins and looks at the sky. A shadow slides across the army. It keeps coming. (8.6.)

**Dialogue**
- STANDARD-BEARER: "We've won."

**Must be seen**
- A flat ruined skyline under an empty sky, with a low sun through dust. Long shadows and warm highlights.
- Steam from broken pipes.
- The army as a band of small silhouettes, its banners still bound and almost black. One pole is lowered: the standard-bearer's (key p.8 shows it as a diagonal at the right).
- Grit falling from the arch.
- The ship's shadow crossing the army before the ship is seen.

### Scene 9: EXT./INT. THE FIRST SHIP - SUNRISE
Script p.7 (PDF 9). About 18 s. Bible shots 9.1-9.3. Colour key: bible p.9 (top).

**Characters:** no named characters. The army is below.

**Beats**
1. Out of the sunrise: lights, and a dark mass in the dust. The broken gate towers give its size. (9.1: the anchor.)
2. A keel comes clear. Then the whole vessel: lifting envelopes over an armoured body, Kwind-09 iron hung with Korvenian silver. Re-plated. Built onto, year after year. (9.2.)
3. It keeps coming. Its engines bury every other sound.
4. INSIDE THE HULL. The copper coupling from the foundry, turning under load. Scarred now. The same silver ring. (9.3: the same angle and framing as 1.2 and 1.3. This is one of the two shots where the camera leaves the ground.)

**Dialogue:** none.

**Must be seen**
- The ship sized only by the broken gate towers below it. The hull is a silhouette with a gold rim, and the sun is behind it.
- The construction: three lobed lifting envelopes over a long armoured body, with a row of silver points along it, hanging rigging lines and a keel beneath (key p.9).
- The engines bury all other sound, and the picture goes quiet with them (p.17).
- Inside the hull, the scarred coupling turns under load with the same silver ring.

### Scene 10: EXT. FORECOURT - THE RUINS - CONTINUOUS
Script pp.7-8 (PDF 9-10). About 103 s. Bible shots 10.1-10.16. Colour keys: bible p.9 (bottom), "The ward", and p.10, "The banners". Camera "Far": bible p.12.

**Characters:** LIORA, the BOY, the WARD OFFICER, CAEL, the CAPTAIN, the STANDARD-BEARER, the survivors under the arch, the army (the wounded, the gun crews).

**Beats**
1. The arch begins to give.
2. Sunrise comes through the gaps in the ship's rigging in long moving bars. One crosses Liora. She puts the mirror in it and throws the light up at the dark belly of the ship.
3. FOUR flashes. The count her city lived by. (10.2: white returns, in her hand.)
4. ABOVE, at the observation glass: a masked WARD OFFICER of Korvenia. Four flashes cross the mask. She opens a hand. (10.3: open hand, "another protects". This is the film's first high angle, and it belongs to her.)
5. The ward officer speaks (below).
6. A bell note. Violet light drops from the keel (10.4). The falling arch stops above their heads. The boy looks up.

## 5. Locations

### Spatial layout (implied by the script; directions are inferred)
From outside the city inward: **the causeway** across the glass plain (Aethelgard's rear, and "the road he was offered": "Open. Empty.") leads to **the broken gate**, its **gate towers** and the **shattered gatehouse**. The white truce lamp hangs over the gatehouse, and the gatehouse breach faces inward. Next comes **the forecourt** (Aethelgard's guns and army), then the **stone arch** signal station where the great stair meets the forecourt. The **great stair** climbs the terraces (lower flight, upper flights) to the **inner wall** and its gate (Solforiz). Above are the **terraces** and the **high arcades** with their batteries, the **great retaining wall**, and at the summit **the Crown** (the pressureworks).

Sightlines the set must allow:
- From the breach to the arch and the boy (every gatehouse wide), and up the stair to the inner wall.
- From the lower flight of the stair to the breach (Liora's POV, 6.5), and from there up to the Crown's shutter.
- From the stretcher inside the gatehouse up to the truce lamp outside above it (5.2), and to the Crown (6.4).
- Across the forecourt between Cael and the arch (10.7).
- From the gatehouse back down the causeway.

Sun and fleet: in keys 8, 9 and 10 the low sun sits behind the ruined capital. The ships come "out of the sunrise", from beyond the city, so the ruined capital lies between the army and the fleet, as in the script's closing image: "The army under its banners. The ruined capital between. The coalition filling the sky." The fleet's shadow therefore falls toward the army and along the causeway behind it, as the script says ("The shadow of the fleet lies along it"). For blocking, put the army facing the city and the sunrise, with the causeway at their backs.

Also recurring in the script: **the count of four** (pulse, rail strikes, flashes); **"time"** ("Give him time." / "Then give them time."); **the open road**, the causeway Cael looks back at (sc. 5 and 10); **the respirator seal**; and **the crown**, which Cael never wears ("He wagered his crown and lost it."). The epigraph "Blood remembers what stone forgets." sits against "Hold the stone." This last pairing is my observation, not something the documents state.

### Effects: what each may and may not do (p.17)
- **Cael's hold:** nothing glows; the object simply stops. Dust keeps falling past it and the air warps at its edge. The sound thins to a hum. It costs him: shaking fingers, blood through the binding. The bible keeps out "Glowing hands, beams or energy for his power." (p.2).
- **The fall:** the cause is seen (a tower drops across the retaining wall and the wall gives). Dust rises faster than the buildings fall, with no fireball. Then the skyline is simply gone.
- **The ward:** violet light drops from the keel on a bell note. It is light, not a wall. The stone stops above their heads and stays there.
- **The fleet:** never whole in one frame until 10.15. Its size comes from the gate towers and its shadow. Its engines bury every other sound.

### Camera, lenses, framing and style (bible pp.2, 3, 11, 12, 13, 18)
- **Six rules (p.2):** (1) Keep a person in the frame: no shot of scale without someone to measure it. (2) Hold the wide when it matters: at the order (5.8), the fall (7.3) and the silence (8.4); close shots are kept for hands, the wrist and the seal. (3) Stay at eye level: the camera leaves the ground only inside the hull (9.3) and at the ward officer's glass (10.3). (4) Light comes from things we can see: a lamp, a gun, the sun, the ward. (5) Ration the colour. (6) The picture says it before the line does: "If a shot needs its dialogue to be understood, restage the shot."
- **Frame:** 2.39:1 (proposed). The causeway, the army line and the fleet are all horizontals. If the film is delivered for phones, those horizontals need restaging (p.18).
- **Height:** eye level of whoever the shot belongs to. At the rescue that is Liora's (3.5).
- **Lenses (proposed):** wide 24-35mm for places; normal 50mm for faces and the table; long 85-135mm for looks across distance (hers at 6.5, his at 10.7).
- **Focus:** deep for the city, the fall and the fleet. Shallow only for the wrist (3.5), the seal (4.6), her hand (10.6) and his (10.8).
- **Movement (proposed):** locked off, or one slow push per scene. Handheld only while Liora runs (7.1, 7.2).
- **Holds (proposed):** six shots run longer than feels comfortable: 3.3, 5.8, 6.3, 8.4, 10.6, and 10.9 into 10.10.
- **Ground rule (proposed):** the film never looks down on the battle from the air. The first high angle is the ward officer's at 10.3.
- **Near, clear, far (p.12):** the camera follows what Liora understands. Near is the rescue (3.5, 3.6). Clear is the decision (5.7, 6.3): his face at "I know." and his hand at the shutter fully readable. Far is the ending (10.6-10.8): she sees him small, and his own close-up stays clear.
- **Four frames to come back to (p.13):** *the anchor* (1.2, 3.3, 9.1); *the empty sky* (2.1, 8.1, 10.15); *centre and edge* (Cael centred at 6.3, Liora off-centre and low at 10.6); *the frame inside the frame* (the breach holds the boy at 4.1, 4.7, 5.4).
- **Kept out of the film (p.2):** low heroic angles on Cael; glowing hands, beams or energy; a view from the ships looking down on the battle; handheld except 7.1-7.2; any colour on the Aethelgard side before 10.12; a teal-and-orange grade ("The orange here is gun-flash").
- **Light (p.11):** "Every light has a source and an owner" Shadows are charcoal, not black, and armour and stone keep their texture. Faces may go half dark; hands never do. No fill light from below the lens except the furnace in sc. 1. Dust only falls before the fall (3.3, 4.10), stands between the survivors and the army after it, and the ruins stay readable under the ships (9.1, 10.5, 10.15).
- **Finish (p.18):** cool, desaturated grade with warm dust after the fall; skin stays natural. Fine, even grain, a little heavier in the gatehouse. No flare and no bloom; highlights clip cleanly. Titles in small capitals, widely spaced, bone on black: "YEARS EARLIER.", "SOLFORIZ. YEAR 56." and the main title. Subtitles in the same face in bone, low in frame, for the four subtitled native lines.
- **Open choices (p.18):** frame shape; banner colour; the breathing lamps; Liora's hood; the medium ("For animation or generated images, keep the rules on scale, eye level, colour and holds"); a six- or eight-minute cut.

## 7. Bible pages to use as visual reference for a simple 3D blocking set

The bible says all eleven colour keys and diagrams are original drawings made for the document, with no film stills reproduced (p.18). Ranked by usefulness for blocking:

1. **p.5, key 2 "The fallen gate"** (bottom): the master layout. It shows the glass plain, the causeway diagonal to the gate, the roughly 8-tier ziggurat capital with the Crown block on top, terrace lamps, the truce lamp at the gate, an abandoned cannon in the foreground, and the horizon in the lower third. Build the city massing from this.
2. **p.6, key 4 "The gatehouse"** (bottom): the gatehouse interior. It shows the table, the three judgments, Cael's position against the envoy's, and the jagged breach framing the stepped city, the arch and the boy. Use it to set the breach's orientation toward the arch.
3. **p.6, key 3 "The held stone"** (top): the foot of the great stair, the overhanging terrace, the suspended block, Liora and the boy low, and Cael at frame right "ten paces off". Use it for the stair's footprint and the rescue positions.
4. **p.9, key 9 "The first ship"** (top): the ship's construction (three lobed envelopes, armoured body, silver points, rigging lines, keel) and its scale against the two broken gate-tower stumps, with the army line below and the sun behind.
5. **p.9, key 10 "The ward"** (bottom): the arch (round-topped, one pier broken, crown stones suspended), the violet cone from the keel, the survivors under it, the soldiers at left, and diagonal bars of sunrise through the rigging.
6. **p.10, key "The banners"**: the fleet in receding layers, the nearest hull with its row of amber housings, the army line with ragged red banners, and Cael small at centre. Use it for the final wide (10.15).
7. **p.7, keys 5 and 6** (also the p.1 cover): the battery positions relative to the Crown, and the standard-bearer's pole height against a figure (key 5). Cael silhouetted in the breach toward the Crown's lit shutter slit, with the breach edges as a frame (key 6). Use them to place the guns and the sightline from the breach to the Crown.
8. **p.8, keys 7 and 8**: the collapse (the stair at left with civilians, four toppling towers, the dust cloud), then the flat ruined skyline at sunrise with the army band, bound banners, one lowered pole and steam plumes. Use them for the ruins massing and the army line height.
9. **p.5, key 1 "The foundry"** (top): the coupling's proportions (banded drum, ring a third of the way down, two chains), a row of about seven couplings receding, and the worker for scale.
10. **p.13, composition diagrams**: the anchor (x4 scale bar), the empty sky (horizon line with two-thirds sky), centre and edge, and the frame inside the frame (breach with the arch and boy). Also the four hand gestures. Use them for camera framing templates.
11. **p.12, "Near, clear, far" thumbnails**: keys 3, 6 and 10 repeated, with the camera-position rule for each.
12. **pp.4, 14 and 15** (palette pages): material colour assignments for the blocking shaders.

## 8. Notes, discrepancies and things to confirm with the author

- **Two "Bone" and two "Ash" values:** grounds Bone #D9D2C2 and Ash #8E887D (p.4) against Aethelgard Bone #D8CFBD (p.14) and Solforiz Ash #9A9388 (p.14). **Two "Iron" values:** Aethelgard Iron #6E6F72 (p.14) against Kwind-09 Iron #3B3D40 (p.15).
- **Banner colour, breathing lamps and Liora's hood** are bible proposals, not script.
- **Scene 6's slug** names three places (THE CROWN / THE BREACH / THE STAIR) and cuts between them. The breach is the gatehouse's broken wall.
- **The wounded soldier** addresses Cael as "Cyning"; this is the only time anyone uses his title in speech.
- **The ward officer is female** (script: "She opens a hand").
- **Scene 8's boy:** the script does not name him in scene 8, but he is under the arch in scenes 7 and 10.
- **Six-minute cut:** the navigator loses his two turns (5.2, 6.4). The keys work for both cuts.
- **Shot list:** the bible cites a "timed shot list" that was not among the inputs. The 76 shot numbers are known only by their ranges and the shots the bible names.
- **Epigraph:** "Blood remembers what stone forgets." is on the script's first story page. The script does not say whether it appears on screen, and the bible lists only the two supers and the main title as titles (p.18).
