# Long Distance: review of rough cut 1

Rough cut 1 (`out/long_distance_rough_720p.mp4`, 9:08) was reviewed by two independent reviewers, a director and an animation supervisor/editor. Both saw only the film and the screenplay.

**Agreed verdict:** the story reads, the twist holds until the doorway, and the clue sits in plain sight. Restraint holds and subtitles are verbatim. Grain versus gloss and the colour script hold in every sequence. The bodies do not: hands, arms and staging fail on several of the beats the story hangs on, mostly in the last three minutes. There are also about 45 s of holds where nothing moves.

## Fix list (agreed by both, or found by one and confirmed on the frames)

### Shared: the rig (`film/rig.js`)
1. **Collars and cuffs.** The padded collar reads as a neck pillow all film, and cuffs float like bracelets with bare forearm showing above them.
2. **Necks.** There is no neck between collar and jaw in some poses (Q04 tight, Q11 final image).
3. **Daylight faces.** The light is cut into hard pale patches that read as blotched skin (Q08 294 to Q10). The colour leaving his face reads as patches, not pallor.
4. **The flush** (Q04) is a flat red disc inside the collar that reads as a wound or a red T-shirt. It should be colour rising on the skin of the neck and ears.
5. **Hands on the story beats:**
   - the phone grip (a fist, a thumb longer than the phone is wide)
   - the bag held with the thumb on the zip (sausage fingers, a spare thumb, a third hand from nowhere)
   - the hand to the nape (the arm sticks out like a wing and never reaches her neck)
   - the trade (four bodiless parallel sleeve-tubes)
   Arms attach at the shoulder.
6. **Lying down and on the couch.** The legs fold into flat boxes, the torso slides off the hips, and the legs pass through the couch arm (Q11 507–529). The arm that holds the phone beside his face comes from his shoulder, not from the bottom of the frame.

### Shared: the phone (`film/phone.js`)
7. **The app portrait** (Q11 486–494) is a doll: a head on a white sack, tube hair, a crooked mouth, and an invisible pulse. It should be the strip's face as a photograph that is too smooth: her face filling the screen, real shoulders, the long hair, a visible pulsing line.
8. **The generated faces** in the waterfall (Q06, Q09) lean toward 3D avatars. A sharp viewer may read them as avatars and get the twist early. Make them photographs that are too even, not cartoons.

### Shared: the living room (`film/sets/living.js`)
9. **The pendant's light** at night reads as three stacked sand dunes behind him (Q06).

### Sequences
10. **Q01:** halve the 18 s static wide after his eyes close.
11. **Q02 and Q07:**
    - He exists only in the mirror: put his body at the sink, or stage the mirror so we believe it.
    - The thread already shows the whole exchange above the message he is typing.
    - Trim the bathroom wides that run on.
12. **Q03:** the coworker's look doesn't read (a tiny head two rows back, popping up in two frames). Without it, turning the phone face down has no reason.
13. **Q04:** the blurred grid holds 12 s; trim it.
14. **Q06:**
    - The fork hangs frozen at his chest and never reaches his mouth, so "he stops chewing" can't play.
    - His eyeline goes off to the right, not down to the phone.
    - The pan has no handle.
    - The lock/wake inserts use a giant disembodied finger.
    - The last shot doesn't put the strip and the phone in one frame.
15. **Q08:**
    - He vanishes from the couch: the knock reaction and him setting the phone down are skipped.
    - He jumps from the door to the fridge.
    - Her eyes travelling the room have no shot of her looking, or looking away.
16. **Q09:**
    - Opening the wardrobe: he stands inside it, with his forearms crossed in an X.
    - His torso faces away while his head is in profile.
    - "We've never taken this photo" plays with the phone as a sliver at her shoulder.
    - The phone exchange plays 16 s in a wide where the faces are tiny.
17. **Q10:**
    - **Geography:** his reverses have the front door and coat hook behind him, but her over-the-shoulder puts the fridge behind him. Fix one geography for the whole argument.
    - The long holds (390–398, 416–428, 464–476).
    - The trade.
    - The nape.
18. **Q11:**
    - The phone floats without the hand that held it.
    - In the final image his hand rests on his cheek instead of over the phone.
    - The final strip is too dark to show anyone laughing.

### Not agreed, left as is
- Q05 (the bus): one reviewer says the ears and the glance land; the other says they don't read. Revisit after the rig fixes.
