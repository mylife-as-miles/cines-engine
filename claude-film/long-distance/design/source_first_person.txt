Long Distance My long-distance girlfriend and I used to live together until she moved away. And now every night we call in bed, and we text every day at work, and on my break I will message her and ask her to send me photos of her naked, and she’s always happy to do so. I’ve got hundreds of them now, along with audio clips and videos of what she wants to do to me and what I want to do to her, and it’s been like this for a while, and everything is okay.

She sent me a picture of the two of us standing in front of a waterfall recently, smiling and holding each other.

“I love this one,” I said. I even made it my phone background.

We always say goodnight, and we always say good morning, and we always say, “I love you.”

Then there was a surprise knock at the door, and it was her.

My heart fell to my stomach. I let her in.

“Why didn’t you call first?”

“I don’t know,” she said. I ran to my bedroom to grab something for her, and my phone was lying on the couch, and I heard it buzz through the wall, and everything was not okay.

I came back with an old makeup bag in my hand. She was standing over the couch, and the screen was still lit. Call me tonight?

“Don’t look at my phone,” I said.

“What is this?” she asked.

“It’s just a friend from work. It’s nothing. It’s none of your business.”

She stopped me. “I’m not talking about the text. I’m talking about the background of me and you. We’ve never taken that photo. We’ve never been to a waterfall.”

I didn’t say anything.

“And I know, maybe it’s not my place, but it’s a bit disturbing that I’m the background of your phone and we aren’t together anymore.”

“I know. I forgot to change it,” I said.

“You didn’t forget.” She looked at the picture again, at her own face smiling back at her. “Is it Photoshop?” And then, quieter: “Have you been using AI to make pictures of me?”

“I just missed you, is all.”

“We’ve been apart longer than we were ever together,” she told me. “This is not okay.”

She grabbed her makeup bag and swallowed air and left. And I just stare at the picture of us at the waterfall, putting my phone to my ear. “Are you there?” I ask. And she answers, “Yes.” And I say, “I love you.” And she says, “I love you too.”
