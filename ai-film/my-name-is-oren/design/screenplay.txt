MY NAME IS OREN



  Written by

 Osita Miles
OVER BLACK:

Polite synthetic VOICES layered over traffic. Chimes. A
traffic post settling an argument in calm, measured tones.

FADE IN:

EXT. LAUNDRETTE STREET - DAY

Three PAVEMENT SWEEPERS work the gutter in a ragged line.
Two CITY UNITS in hi-vis empty a bin. Both wear the same
grey regulation shoes.

The laundrette window. Clean decals: FREE TRANSLATION. FREE
LEGAL FORMS. FREE TUTORING WHILE YOU WAIT. Beside them, an
older sign with half its letters peeled away: FREE WASHES
FOR SCHOOL UNIFORMS.

Below the window, a pair of scuffed combat boots. Rubber
coming loose from one sole.

They belong to HELENA (30s), leaning on the wall, one hand
flat against the small of her back. In the other hand, an
old HANDSET with a cracked corner.

INSERT - HANDSET SCREEN

A drawing app. Half a page of sketched boots. Hers. The
laces unfinished.

BACK TO SCENE

She pockets the handset. The cracked corner snags on the
lining.

HELENA'S POV

Through the foot traffic, a brown PAPER BAG swinging in time
with someone's stride. Grease showing through one corner.

BACK TO SCENE

She swallows. Her eyes drop to the carrier's feet. Not grey
regulation shoes. Big ugly slippers with straps.

She pushes off the wall.

                       HELENA
           Excuse me. Hey, buddy. Excuse me.

The figure stops. The bag stops swinging. This is OREN.

                       OREN
           Good afternoon.

She looks up at him. Stops. Just below his ear runs a fine

                      HELENA
          Oh. You're one of the fake ones.

                      OREN
          My name is Oren.

                      HELENA
          Right. Right, sorry. Recognising
          you as an individual and stuff. I
          didn't mean any offence.

                      OREN
          I am not offended. I can't be.

                       OREN
          Hungry?

                      HELENA
          Yeah. Haven't eaten properly for a
          few days. They cut off my benefits,
          so I can't even --

Her eyes go to the bag. Come back.

                      HELENA (CONT'D)
          Doesn't matter.

Oren looks down at the bag.

                      OREN
          This belongs to the person I
          support.

A PAVEMENT SWEEPER bumps the toe of her boot.

                       SWEEPER
          Apologies.

It reverses and tries the same gap again. Oren puts his
slipper in its way and holds it there until the machine goes
round them.

                      OREN
          There is a community meal service
          at the clinic on Mercer Street.
          Lunch ends at two. It is one
          thirty-one.

                      HELENA
          I know where it is, buddy.

Oren goes still. Not switched-off still. Waiting still.

                      HELENA (CONT'D)
          Sorry. You are trying to help.

She puts a hand against the wall and takes the weight off
one leg.

                      OREN
          Are you in pain?

                      HELENA
          Oh, we know each other.

                       OREN
          You and --

                      HELENA
          Me and the pain. Yeah.

                      OREN
          Would you like assistance sitting
          down?

A low wall beside the laundrette. He doesn't take hold of
her. He stands close enough that when her leg wobbles she
can put a hand on his shoulder. She does, and lowers
herself.

Her hand stays on his shoulder a beat after she is down. She
looks at her palm as she takes it away.

                      HELENA
          That's a hell of an introduction.

                      OREN
          My name is Oren.

                      HELENA
          I know. I got that one.

He stays standing. The bag hangs level with her face. She
turns her head away from it.

                      HELENA (CONT'D)
          You got somewhere to be?

                      OREN
          Yes. I am delivering this to my
          client at the clinic. Her daughter
          is with her. It needs to arrive by
          two. I can assist you, provided it
          still arrives on time.

                      HELENA
          Don't let me keep you.

                      OREN
          We are going in the same direction.

                      HELENA
          Are we? I didn't say I was going.

                      OREN

                      HELENA
          It's already been appealed. Twice.

He waits. She unlocks the handset.

                      HELENA (CONT'D)
          Those. Not the recordings.

She presses send. A small light wakes under the skin at his
temple. He does not move.

                      HELENA (CONT'D)

The light goes out.

                      OREN
          Yes, Second Lieutenant Helena
          Duncan.

Her head comes up.

                      HELENA
          Do you know how long it's been

                       HELENA

                       HELENA

He is still standing over her. She shifts along the wall.

                      HELENA (CONT'D)
          Can you sit? Here. With me.

He sits and puts the lunch between his feet. She has to move

                      OREN
          May I speak simply?

                      HELENA
          Go on.

                      OREN
          The benefits office appears to have
          fucked you.

A noise comes out of her nose. She tries to stop it. It
comes again. She folds forward laughing, which hurts, so she
laughs at that too.

                      HELENA
          Oh, Jesus. Yeah. Those fuckers sure
          did.

                      OREN
          Was that simple enough?

                      HELENA
          Yeah, buddy. About right.

                      OREN
          Oh. Good.

He doesn't laugh. When she looks down, her boot is touching
the lunch bag again.

                      OREN (CONT'D)
          I can't add to the appeal. It needs
          a person to open it. The clinic has
          a benefits adviser today. May I ask
          them?

                      HELENA
          You can ask. No promise?

                      OREN
          No. I cannot approve the appeal.

                      HELENA
          Good. I don't need another
          congratulations before anything's
          happened.

The light at his temple wakes. Goes out.

                      OREN
          The desk has replied. They will
          keep a portion of the meal aside.
          The adviser can look at the notice
          after two. Look at it. Not decide
          it.

She looks at him. Properly, this time. A scrape along his
chin. One slipper mended with blue tape.

                      HELENA
          You are terrible at selling this.

                      OREN
          The accurate version is difficult
          to sell.

                       HELENA
          Yes. Please. The food, yes.
                (beat)
          Okay. The adviser too.

                      OREN
          It is one thirty-six. It is a
          twenty-minute walk. Can you make it
          without assistance?

                      HELENA
          Yeah. I think I can.

He picks up the bag and stands. She stays where she is. He
waits.

                      OREN
          Can you make it without assistance?

                      HELENA
          You asked me that.

                      OREN
          You haven't stood up.

                      HELENA
          Yeah, well. Maybe ask something
          else.

He lowers the bag. He doesn't ask anything else. She rubs

                        HELENA (CONT'D)
          When I say I can, sometimes I mean
          I know I'm supposed to.
                 (beat)
          Last time I got as far as the

          was going in. I said no. Like I'd

She wipes her nose on her sleeve.

                      HELENA (CONT'D)
          Can you walk me in? To the desk, I
          mean. Just stay there a bit.

He glances at the bag.

                      HELENA (CONT'D)
          If you've got time.

The light at his temple wakes.

                      OREN
          My client is calling.

                      CLIENT (V.O.)
                (through his speaker)
          Oren? Where's my lunch?

                      HELENA
          Go. You have to go.
                                                             8.


                      OREN
                (to the call)
          I would like to walk someone to the
          clinic. At her pace I will arrive
          at ten past two.

                      CLIENT (V.O.)
          Ten past? No. Two o'clock, Oren.

                        OREN
          Understood.

The light goes out. Helena puts both feet on the pavement.

                      HELENA
          Right. Better get going, then.

He moves the bag into his left hand and offers his right
arm. She stares at it.

                      HELENA (CONT'D)
          Just till we're round the corner.
          Then you go on ahead.

                        OREN
          All right.

She gets halfway up and has to stop. Her fingers are bunched
in his sleeve. She loosens them, finds his forearm, tries
again. He stays exactly where he is until she has her feet
under her.

A step. Another. He walks at the pace that makes two
o'clock. She tugs his sleeve.

                      HELENA
          Slow down, Oren.

The light at his temple wakes. One blink. Out.

He slows down.

They reach the corner. She doesn't let go. He doesn't go on
ahead. The paper bag swings again, slower now, in time with
her.

They turn the corner together.
