## Film bible (Look A, overcast; the look and the voices travel in the prompt text)

**Look string (A).** Overcast English afternoon, soft flat daylight, muted slate and wet-concrete greys, faded red brick, hi-vis yellow the only saturated colour, warm fluorescent spill from the laundrette, fine 35 mm grain, gentle halation, naturalistic, never glossy, never teal-and-orange.

**Camera package.** ARRI Alexa XT · 2.35:1 · restrained 1.5× anamorphic · Cooke Speed Panchro-inspired rendering: gentle softness, moderate microcontrast, organic falloff, restrained oval bokeh, smooth highlight roll-off. Soft, low-contrast, motivated light: the overcast sky as the primary source, and the laundrette window's warm spill as the secondary.

**Influences, each with its purpose (settled; copied unchanged into the MOVIE COLOR section of every auteur prompt).** Grammar, never imitation: no shot, frame or character is copied, and no frame from these films is used as a reference.
- Denis Villeneuve, *Prisoners* (Roger Deakins): an ordinary grey street made monumental; soft overcast light; precise, symmetrical compositions in everyday places.
- Denis Villeneuve, *Arrival* (Bradford Young): muted, soft, overcast intimacy; faces close in diffuse grey light; contact with a non-human played quietly.
- Denis Villeneuve, *Blade Runner 2049* (Roger Deakins): a synthetic person filmed with dignity and stillness; small figures against large flat planes; negative space.
- Andrea Arnold, *Fish Tank* (Robbie Ryan): handheld closeness at eye level that breathes with Helena.
- Clio Barnard, *The Selfish Giant* (Mike Eley): tenderness without sentiment in northern English light, on real streets.
- Lynne Ramsay, *Ratcatcher* (Alwin Küchler): the story told in body details: feet, hands, fabric.

**Character identity (draft, until the sheets are approved).**
- *Helena Duncan.* 30s, lean from not eating, an athletic frame gone tired. Hair pulled back in a short practical ponytail with strands loose; no make-up. She has style, worn thin: good pieces, old now, kept and mended. She wears a cropped, boxy olive field jacket in faded recycled ripstop from her service years (dropped shoulders, high funnel collar, flat bonded seams, no insignia), with the **left** elbow mended with a darker patch, worn open (as in the chosen casting image). Under it is a heavyweight charcoal funnel-neck knit; below, wide, high-waisted washed-black utility trousers cropped above the ankle. Her combat boots are classic brown-black leather lace-ups, scuffed grey at the toes, with the **right** sole coming loose at the toe (as in the chosen casting image). She keeps a hand flat on the small of her back. Her old handset has a cracked corner.
- *Oren.* A care android who passes at a glance for a man in his forties: average height, a slightly soft build, a plain kind face with skin a little too even, short neat dark hair, clean-shaven. A fine seam runs from just below his RIGHT ear down the side of his neck (adopted from Q03 t1). A small light sits under the skin of his RIGHT temple, and there is a scrape along his chin. He wears a care agency's work shirt and no coat on a cold grey day, which is a quiet tell. In the story her hand feels "his shoulder was warm through his shirt". The shirt is a soft, boxy navy one in brushed cotton with a short band collar, buttoned to the top, with a small plain badge clipped at the left chest. The badge is blank: in the story it names a real company (Grok), which the film does not show. Below it are relaxed tapered mid-grey trousers, a little short. Deliberately out of place are his big ugly padded grey felt slippers, with two wide hook-and-loop straps and chunky rubber soles, the **right** one mended across the toe with blue tape. He carries a brown paper lunch bag with the top rolled, grease showing through one corner.

**Street (for every environment prompt).** Victorian and Edwardian brick terraces as they stand today, retrofitted rather than replaced: heat-pump units and slim insulation panels on side walls, solar film on some upper windows. There are fewer cars, all quiet and electric; some parking bays are planters and cycle racks. Slim, matte municipal street furniture (traffic posts, delivery lockers) has no screens and no lights. Kerb sweepers work the gutter, and old shopfronts carry clean, plain decals. Nothing glows and nothing is chrome; signage is sparse; it is lived-in, damp and ordinary.

**Voice Bible (VOICE DNA; draft until the author approves, then immutable and copied unchanged into every prompt).** Revised after Q03 t1: "quick and clipped" made Helena rush her lines, so it read "briskly and dryly, never rushed". Revised again after Q03 t2 (the author: "she's speaking too fast"): "briskly" and "emotion leaks as speed" are gone; she talks "at an easy conversational pace with a breath between sentences", and emotion leaks "never as speed". Third revision after the repo check (2026-11-21): pace goes into the voice itself ("unhurried, one breath per sentence, leaning on one word in each"; Oren "the same slow, even tempo"), because timestamps alone did not slow her (Q03 t3). Fourth revision (2026-11-24, before Q11, her most emotional scene): "never as theatrical crying" becomes "she stays composed", so the prompt carries no word for crying (LESSONS 37).
- *Helena Duncan.* A woman of 33, English, with northern working-class vowels (Greater Manchester) flattened by army years. Low-mid pitch, with a dry rasp of tiredness. She talks dryly and unhurried, one breath per sentence, leaning on one word in each: every word lands clearly, and she deflects with dry humour; her sentences sometimes stop short ("so I can't even —"). When her back bites, her breath catches mid-phrase. Her laugh escapes through her nose before she can stop it. "Buddy" is light and habitual. Emotion leaks as swallowed endings and a thicker voice; she stays composed. She swears plainly, without venom. Never posh, never cockney, never American.
- *Oren.* A man's voice of no fixed age (he passes for forty), neutral southern English. Mid-low pitch, warm but even: the same calm at every line. Unhurried, with precise consonants and every sentence finished; questions rise only slightly. He takes no audible breaths between phrases and makes no filler sounds ("um", "er"). He says time exactly ("one thirty-one"). "The benefits office appears to have fucked you" is said in exactly the same calm, factual tone as everything else. Never robotic, never a vocoder, no metallic or synthetic processing. The only tell is that every line has the same slow, even tempo; he finishes every sentence and never laughs. He says his name OR-en, the first syllable stressed, like "foreign" without the F.
- *The client (V.O.).* An elderly English woman in her eighties, thin and querulous, impatient but not unkind ("Oren? Where's my lunch?"). Heard only through the small speaker at Oren's collar: a narrow, slightly tinny small-speaker sound, close to his body.
- *Sweeper.* A small, bright, courteous synthetic voice, slightly higher than a person's and perfectly cheerful, identical every time. It only ever says "Apologies."
