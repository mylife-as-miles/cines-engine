## SCENE INTENT

Hunger makes Helena move. Through the passing feet and coats she sees a greasy paper lunch bag swinging in someone's stride; she swallows, and her eyes drop to the carrier's feet: not grey regulation shoes, but big ugly slippers with straps. She pushes off the wall and goes after him, calling out lighter than she feels. He stops, the bag stops, and he turns to her: "Good afternoon." We still do not see his face. By the last frame the two of them stand a metre apart, and she has not yet looked up.

MY NAME IS OREN: one overcast English afternoon, about 2041, grounded and never sci-fi. Muted slate and wet-concrete greys, faded red brick and warm-neutral blacks. The laundrette's warm cream light is the only warm source. Hi-vis yellow appears only on the city's machines: the one saturated colour. Helena's olive and Oren's navy stay muted; the blue tape on his slipper is the only clean blue. Felt, never displayed: never neon, never glossy, never teal-and-orange. Fine 35 mm grain, gentle halation.

**Influences, each with its purpose** — Denis Villeneuve, Prisoners (Roger Deakins): an ordinary grey street made monumental; soft overcast light; precise, symmetrical compositions in everyday places. Denis Villeneuve, Arrival (Bradford Young): muted, soft, overcast intimacy; faces close in diffuse grey light; contact with a non-human played quietly. **Camera package** — ARRI Alexa XT · 2.35:1 · restrained 1.5× anamorphic · Cooke Speed Panchro-inspired rendering: gentle softness, moderate microcontrast, organic falloff, restrained oval bokeh, smooth highlight roll-off. ## VOICE DNA (immutable)

HELENA DUNCAN: A woman of 33, English, with northern working-class vowels (Greater Manchester) flattened by army years. Low-mid pitch, with a dry rasp of tiredness. She talks quick and clipped and deflects with dry humour; her sentences sometimes stop short ("so I can't even —"). When her back bites, her breath catches mid-phrase. Her laugh escapes through her nose before she can stop it. "Buddy" is light and habitual. Emotion leaks as speed, swallowed endings and a thicker voice, never as theatrical crying. She swears plainly, without venom. Never posh, never cockney, never American.

OREN: A man's voice of no fixed age (he passes for forty), neutral southern English. Mid-low pitch, warm but even: the same calm at every line. Unhurried, with precise consonants and every sentence finished; questions rise only slightly. He takes no audible breaths between phrases and makes no filler sounds ("um", "er"). He says time exactly ("one thirty-one"). "The benefits office appears to have fucked you" is said in exactly the same calm, factual tone as everything else. Never robotic, never a vocoder, no metallic or synthetic processing. The only tell is that he never hurries, never trails off and never laughs. He says his name OR-en, the first syllable stressed, like "foreign" without the F.
