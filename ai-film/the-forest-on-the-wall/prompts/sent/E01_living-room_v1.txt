# THE FOREST ON THE WALL — ENVIRONMENT REFERENCE PROMPT

## THE LIVING ROOM, CHRISTMAS EVE

Create a photorealistic cinematic environment reference image for THE LIVING ROOM, CHRISTMAS EVE.

This is a FILM PRODUCTION LOCATION REFERENCE, not concept art, not an architectural visualization, not advertising photography.

The environment must feel physically real, inhabited, spatially coherent, and suitable for repeated use as a continuity reference across multiple generated film shots.

---

# LOCATION IDENTITY

LOCATION:
The living room of a family house, late on Christmas Eve

COUNTRY / REGION:
A snowy town (no specific country)

SETTING:
Residential living room

TIME PERIOD:
Present day

SOCIAL / ECONOMIC CHARACTER:
A modest, comfortable family home

OVERALL FEEL:
Cozy, quiet, lived-in, the house asleep

The location should feel like a place that existed before the camera arrived.

It must not feel production-designed for symbolism.

---

# SPATIAL GEOGRAPHY

Establish clear, believable geography.

Include:

- One long plain cream wall runs across the back of the frame, from left to right. It is bare: nothing hangs on it and nothing stands against it.
- At the left end, a side wall at a right angle to the cream wall holds one window with a deep painted wooden sill. Snow falls outside in the dark.
- A brass desk lamp stands on that windowsill, switched on, its shade turned to throw its light across the room onto the cream wall.
- At the right end of the cream wall, an open doorway with no door leads into a dark hall.
- Just past the doorway, in the right-hand corner of the room, stands a decorated Christmas tree about 1.8 m tall, with small warm-white lights, a few red and gold glass baubles and a simple star on top; a few wrapped presents under it.
- A large wool rug lies on the floorboards in front of the cream wall, the open floor where a child can sit facing the wall.
- The camera's side of the room is out of frame.
- Camera positions: the whole wall from the middle of the room at eye height; close on the windowsill; low behind the tree; over the shoulder of someone sitting on the rug.

The geography must be easy to understand and remain reproducible from different camera angles.

Do not create impossible architecture.

Do not create furniture that blocks basic movement.

Do not create a room that changes scale from one side to another.

---

# ARCHITECTURE

Preserve believable architecture appropriate to:

- country
- building age
- social class
- function
- climate

Specify:

WALLS:
Smooth painted plaster, warm cream, matte, a faint hand-plastered texture

CEILING:
Plain, painted off-white, ordinary height, no light fitting switched on

FLOOR:
Worn oak floorboards, a large cream-and-red wool rug in front of the cream wall

WINDOWS:
One painted wooden window in the left side wall, small panes, a deep wooden sill at waist height, a little frost in the pane corners

DOORS:
An open doorway with a plain painted wooden frame at the right end of the cream wall; the hall beyond is dark

TRIM / MOULDING:
Plain painted wooden skirting boards and door frame

FIXTURES:
An old painted radiator under the window; one power socket low on the side wall for the lamp

Architectural details should feel ordinary and regionally credible.

---

# MATERIALS + TEXTURE

Use tactile, photorealistic materials:

- matte painted plaster
- worn oak floorboards
- thick wool rug
- painted wood
- aged brass
- real pine needles
- glass baubles
- cold window glass with a little frost

Surfaces should contain subtle wear.

Nothing should look freshly generated, pristine, glossy, or showroom-perfect.

# PRODUCTION DESIGN

Include only story-relevant objects.

PRIMARY OBJECTS:

- the brass desk lamp on the windowsill: an old adjustable desk lamp with a small brass dome shade on a jointed arm, lit, its cable running down to the socket
- the decorated Christmas tree in the right-hand corner, past the doorway
- the wool rug in front of the bare cream wall

SECONDARY LIVED-IN DETAILS:

- a few wrapped presents under the tree
- the radiator under the window
- a little frost in the corners of the window panes

Objects should feel casually accumulated over time.

Avoid excessive dressing.

Avoid obvious symbolic props.

The environment should remain visually usable for performance.

WARM TUNGSTEN NIGHT, CHRISTMAS RED AND PINE.

- warm cream

For this environment specifically:

DOMINANT:
The cream wall in warm amber lamplight, falling away into deep warm darkness

SECONDARY:

ACCENT:

# LIGHTING DESIGN

ONE SMALL WARM LAMP.
HARD-EDGED SHADOWS.
DEEP WARM DARKNESS.

Primary motivated source:

The brass desk lamp on the windowsill. Its small tungsten bulb throws a warm pool of light across the cream wall from the left, raking along it. Anything in its beam casts a crisp, hard-edged shadow on the wall.

Secondary motivated source:

The Christmas tree's small warm lights, glowing softly in the right-hand corner; and a faint cool blue-grey glow from the snowy night through the window.

Maintain:

- the room mostly dark, with the cream wall the brightest surface
- crisp, sharp-edged shadows from the lamp
- shadows that keep a little detail, so the room's geography stays readable
- natural falloff from the lamp into darkness
- realistic practical-light behavior
- believable night-time exposure

No ceiling light.

No light sources other than the lamp, the tree lights and the window.

No beauty lighting.

No exaggerated rim light.

No glossy commercial lighting.
