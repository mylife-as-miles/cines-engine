1   INT. ORISON - CATHEDRAL OF THE ASCENSION - DAY                 1

    White stone and light. Columns no thicker than a man climb
    two hundred feet into mist. Petals fall from the high
    galleries, too slowly.

    SUPER: "THE KINGDOM OF ORISON. YEAR 800 OF THE ASCENSION."

    Ten thousand guests in white, under the banners of the seven
    kingdoms. Beyond the arches there is no land and no horizon.
    Only cloud, and white warships at anchor in the air, every
    flag flying.

    A BRIDE and GROOM kneel beneath an open tower. In it hangs a
    new bell, bronze, the size of a house, still slung in the
    chains of the fleet barge that lifted it. The HIEROPHANT
    (70s) raises his arms.

                           HIEROPHANT
               Heaven chose us from the earth and
               raised us above the unworthy. What
               heaven has raised --

                           TEN THOUSAND VOICES
               Let no hand bring down.

    GRAND ADMIRAL SERATH (50s) stands among the admirals, a
    collar of heavy gold at her throat. Up here it weighs almost
    nothing. She watches the rite the way she would watch a
    harbour. At her shoulder: VICE ADMIRAL CASSIEL (60s),
    correct to the last button. He has wanted that collar for
    ten years.

    A GIRL of seven, strewing petals, trips beside her. The
    basket leaves her hands and drifts. Serath takes it out of
    the air without looking, and gives it back.

    An AIDE offers the day's lifting warrant and red wax. She
    presses her ring into the wax without reading. Her seal: an
    anchor in a ring of stars.

    The aide lifts the sealed warrant toward the tower. Above it
    the barge lets go. Its chains fall slack. The bell settles
    onto the cathedral's own beam, and the kingdom takes its
    weight. A hammer the size of a door swings back.

    The bell is struck.

2   EXT. ANVIL VALE - A FIELD - DAY                                 2

    SUPER: "ANVIL VALE."

    Quiet. A low grey sky, and in it one darker shape that
    covers half of it. It has always been there.

    Under the quiet, a hum too deep to hear. It is in the ground
    and in the teeth.

    A FARMER (40s) works a furrow. Everything here is built to
    bear: the low stone house, buttressed like a fort; the ox's
    yoke; his own back. When he stands he does not straighten.
    Nobody here does.

    His DAUGHTER (6) brings him water in a wooden bowl. He
    drinks and sets it on the wall. She keeps looking at it.

    The water is trembling. Ring inside ring.

    The ox drags at its rope. Crows leave the furrows and cannot
    climb. The farmer looks to the mountain the valley is named
    for. A line of dust has opened across its face. No sound
    yet.

    Then the face of the mountain lets go.

    He has the girl under his arm before the sound arrives. She
    has the bowl. The whole valley is running.


3   INT. ORISON - CATHEDRAL OF THE ASCENSION - CONTINUOUS           3

    The bell's note is still in the air. Ten thousand gloved
    hands applaud, almost soundless. On a gallery rail stands a
    gold cup of wine. Its surface is perfectly still.


4   INT. ORISON - COUNCIL CHAMBER - DAY                             4

    SUPER: "THREE DAYS LATER."

    A gold cup on a white table. The wine in it is trembling.
    Ring inside ring. The CHANCELLOR (60s, tired, decent) cannot
    stop watching it.

    A round room open to cloud. Outside, bells are ringing that
    no one has rung. Serath stands at an arch. Below her, cloud
    is rising through the lower terraces like a tide. Cassiel
    waits by the door, hands behind his back. An old ENGINEER
    has the floor, robes dusty to the knee.

          are closing it against our weight.

          Then let it be broken.

5   EXT. BENEATH ORISON - DAY                                         5

    A naval cutter drops out of the cloud. Above it the
    underside of the kingdom fills the sky: raw rock, roots,
    chains thick as towers. Serath has never seen it from below.
    From here it is not beautiful. It is only enormous, and you
    can watch it sink.

    Below: a plain of black basalt to the horizon, in columns,
    like a city no one finished.

    At its edge, across the head of a green valley, stands THE
    WEIR, the ancient station that takes the weight of Orison. A
    mile of fused black stone without a seam or a tool-mark.
    Nine gates. Two are shut. Nothing visible pours through the
    others. But beneath them the grass lies combed flat, and a
    bird that crosses there drops like a stone.

    Beyond it, SARN VALE. Terraces, orchards, the smoke of many
    hearths.


6   EXT. THE TREATY HOUSE - COURTYARD - DAY                           6

    An old fort on the lip of the plain, older than either flag.

    Serath steps from the cutter onto the ground. Her knees
    almost go. Behind her the aide falls outright. The gold
    collar drags at her neck like a hand. Surface soldiers watch
    the sky people learn to stand. Nobody laughs.

    The yard is full of refugees, grey with rock dust. A woman
    in armour beaten from imperial hull-plate is handing out
    spears: MARL (30s), the dust of Anvil Vale still in her
    hair.

    She puts one into the farmer's hands. He is alone. His free
    hand goes to the child's wooden bowl at his belt, and stays
    there.

    They both watch the admiral pass. She notes the look, as she
    would note a gun, and does not know what it is.

    KARST waits at the door. Fifties. Ink on his fingers. An
    imperial engineer's coat with the insignia cut away.

                          KARST
              Admiral. You will want to sit down.
              Everyone does, the first time.
                    (she does not)
              It is only your own weight. Down
              here that is a light day.

                          SERATH
              You hold an imperial Weir. Return
              it by the evening bell and I will
              take your surrender on generous
              terms.

                          KARST
              We did not attack your city,
              Admiral. We stopped carrying it.


7   INT. THE TREATY HOUSE - HALL - DAY                                7

    Dark. One wall is a single face of basalt, cut with writing
    in two scripts, side by side. She can read one. It is the
    language of her prayers. Under both, worn almost flat, runs
    a third that belongs to neither people. The treaty was cut
    straight across it.

    At its foot, two columns of seals. Seven crests she has
    saluted all her life. Beside them, eleven she has never
    seen. Cut the same size. Standing on the same line.

                          SERATH
              "...the seven cities, raised by the
              hands of all, and borne by the
              nations of the high ground, until
              the fire is stone."

    Karst shoulders the shutters open. Daylight. The black
    plain.

                          KARST
              It was stone within forty years.

                          SERATH
              We were chosen.

                           KARST
              You were sheltered.
                    (beat)
              By then you had gardens.

    He holds a lamp over the floor. It is covered with paper:
    hundreds of lifting warrants, each held down by a stone. A
    hearthstone. A roof slate. The corner of a doorstep.

    Every one carries the same seal. An anchor in a ring of
    stars.

    She walks the rows. A dockyard. A battery. The Admiralty
    tower. Thirty years of her hand.

                      KARST (CONT'D)
          I drew where each one would fall.
          You sealed them.

The last stone is a grey shard, still shedding dust. She
knows the wax. She knows the date.

                      SERATH
          The marriage bell.

                      KARST
          Ninety tons. Anvil Vale, the same
          hour. The bearing-lands have been
          full since before you were born.
          Every bell is a mountain now.

She takes off her glove and lays her bare hand on the shard.
She leaves it there.

                      SERATH
          I did not read it.

                       KARST
          No one above reads them. That is

He turns back to the plain.

          And your price.

8   EXT. THE PLAIN AND THE SKY ABOVE IT - DUSK                        8

    The underside of the cloud, amber. Then it breaks in a
    thousand places.

    The fleet of Orison comes down through it. White hulls like
    the naves of churches, rank above rank, until there is more
    ship than sky. Gunports open in long rows, with a sound like
    a city unlocking its doors.

    From the flagship a lamp flashes up at the city: the terms
    and the truce, in clear. On every ship signalmen take them
    down, and officers read over their shoulders. Gun crews
    crowd the ports. Most have never seen the ground.

    On the plain the armies of eleven nations look up. Spears.
    Old cannon. Farmers. Behind them the one road out of Sarn
    Vale is black with people.

    At the Weir, the wheel that works the gates is forty feet
    across, its spokes worn bright by eight hundred years of
    hands. Fighters have their shoulders to it, Marl at their
    head. Karst stands at the lock-pin, one hand on it.

                           MARL
              I dug for two days, Karst. My seal
              is not on your truce.
                    (beat)
              All nine gates. All at once, before
              she fires. Let them learn it the
              way Anvil learned it.

                          KARST
              She has not fired.


9   INT. FLAGSHIP - BRIDGE - DUSK                                     9

    Brass, glass, quiet. Serath. Her FLAG CAPTAIN (40s). A
    hundred officers at their stations. Overhead, through the
    glass, the roots of Orison, lower than at noon.

    On the rim of the city the Council's lamp is dark. She has
    been watching it for an hour. By her order every ship in the
    line has a glass on one road. The tally comes down the
    fleet, lamp to lamp.

                           FLAG CAPTAIN
              The count, Admiral. Nine thousand
              clear of the vale. Of forty.
                    (beat)
              They are carrying beds.

From far above, the evening bell. She bends to the sighting
glass.

THROUGH THE GLASS: the Weir. The wheel. Karst, very small,
looking up at her.

She swings down the valley. The road out of Sarn Vale,
choked. Carts. Herds. A woman with a door on her back,
because it is hers. They will not be clear by nightfall.
They will not be clear by morning.

A small boy on his father's shoulders, shading his eyes,
looking straight up. He seems to be looking at her.

ON THE BRIDGE, she straightens. In her left glove, the
shard. The lamp on the rim has begun to flash. The flag
captain reads it.

                      FLAG CAPTAIN
          The Council's answer, Admiral.
          "What heaven has raised --"

He does not finish. Everyone on the bridge knows the rest.

Serath raises her right hand.

On every ship a signal arm rises with it. Ten thousand
gunners watch. Silence. Even the bells have stopped.

AT THE WEIR, Marl throws her weight on the spoke. The wheel
creaks. Karst does not take his hand from the pin.

ON THE BRIDGE, Serath's hand comes down. Not the chop that
means fire. Slowly. Flat. Palm to the deck.

                      FLAG CAPTAIN
          ...Guns, Admiral?

                      SERATH
          Anchors.

Nobody moves. A lamp is flashing from the second ship of the
line. The flag captain reads it without wanting to.

                      FLAG CAPTAIN (CONT'D)
          From Vice Admiral Cassiel.
          "The bell has rung. The Grand
          Admiral is relieved. The fleet
          fires on my signal."

He looks at the lamp. At his gunners. At her.

Serath unclasps the gold collar and lays it on the chart
table.

10   EXT. THE PLAIN AND THE SKY ABOVE IT - DUSK                     10

     From the flagship's keel an iron anchor falls on a mile of
     chain. It falls for a long time. It strikes the basalt. The
     sound comes late: one iron note, like a bell.

     Nothing follows it.

     A thousand ships hang where they are. On the second ship of
     the line a signal arm is still raised: Cassiel's. Every
     captain in the fleet can see them both.

     Cassiel's arm drops. The chop that means fire.

     Not one gun answers.

     ON A GUN DECK down the line, a GUNNER (19): lanyard round
     his fist, the road he has counted in his sight. He looks at
     the next gunner, who is looking at him. Their lieutenant
     opens his mouth, and shuts it. Nobody will be first.

     Then, far down the line, a small ship lets go its anchor.
     Then the one beside it. Then ten. Then the sky is raining
     iron, and when it stops there is a forest of chain between
     heaven and the ground.

     The flagship's great white banner comes down its mast. Then
     the next ship's. Then the next. Not all. Here and there a
     ship keeps its colours and its height, and does nothing.

     Cassiel's ship does not strike. It turns, alone, and climbs
     back into the cloud. Nobody fires on it.

     AT THE WEIR the fighters take their shoulders from the
     spokes, one by one. Marl does not. She is watching the
     banners come down, and her face is doing something she has
     not given it leave to do.

     She takes her hands off the wheel as if it burned.

                           MARL
               I will not carry them. And I will
               not drop them.
                                                                 11.


     She walks away along the Weir. A few follow her. Most stay.
     Karst draws the pin and sets his hands to the wheel.

                           KARST
                Slowly.

     The wheel turns, a hand's breadth at a time. Gate by gate,
     the Weir closes.

     The cloud above the plain begins to tear.

     A ceiling of rock, wider than the horizon. Roots. Chains.
     Then terraces streaming mist, bridges, hanging gardens,
     towers, and over them all the cathedral, its open tower
     holding the last of the sun.

     A kingdom is coming down out of the sky. The sound is not
     loud. It is large. Every stone on the plain hums with it.

     The armies of eleven nations have hated a shadow all their
     lives. Now they see what cast it. Some of them kneel. They
     do not mean to.

     INSIDE ORISON: the banners of the cathedral, which have
     floated for eight hundred years, drop and hang straight. The
     hierophant, for the first time in his life, has to sit down.
     The chancellor stands at the arch with both hands on the
     stone. Below her, for the first time in hers, there is
     green.

     ON THE PLAIN, the roots of Orison meet the black stone. The
     columns take the weight, and hold. Dust rolls out in a ring
     for miles. At the Weir the ninth gate closes.

     Then the sound stops. All of it. The hum that has been in
     this ground for eight hundred years is gone, and nobody knew
     it was there until now.


11   EXT. THE PLAIN - LAST LIGHT                                       11

     Silence.

     A wall of white marble stands where this morning there was
     sky. Before it, very small, the soldiers of eleven nations
     do not know what to do with their hands.

     Something is wrong with them. They are standing differently.
     All along the line, backs bent since birth are coming
     upright. A boy jumps, comes down late, laughs once, and
     stops.

     The farmer unhooks the bowl and pours water into it from his
     flask.
                                                            12.


The water does not move.

He looks at it for a long time. He is taller than he was
this morning.

At the foot of an anchor chain Serath stands bare-throated
in the dust, Karst beside her. Above them the terraces climb
into the dusk, tier over tier, every rail lined with people,
until they are too far to be people. All of it is looking
down at one white uniform on the black stone. Nobody cheers.
Something is thrown. It falls fast, and lands short.

She does not look up. She crouches and sets the grey shard
on the ground, among the black. When she rises she is
looking past the city, at the horizon. Karst looks where she
looks.

A gate opens at the foot of the lowest terrace. Its stair,
which has ended in air for eight hundred years, ends on the
ground. Nobody comes down it. Then somebody small does.

The girl from the cathedral. Each step costs her. She has
never weighed this much. On the last one she stops and looks
at the ground, which is black, and has no edge.

She steps onto it.

Her legs fold. She sits down hard and puts both palms flat
on the stone, as if it might move. It does not.

She looks up. Ten paces away a man with a spear and a wooden
bowl is looking at her. His knuckles are white on the spear.
The spear does not move.

He does not smile. He does not look away. Neither does she.

Behind her, ten thousand faces on the terraces. Behind him,
an army. Nobody speaks. There is nothing yet that can be
said.

WIDE, AND WIDER: the kingdom on its plain. The chains of its
fleet. Beyond the Weir, Sarn Vale, green, its hearths still
lit.

And on the horizon, very far and very high, in a sun that
has already set down here: six more.

Signal lamps pass between them. A lone white ship, tiny at
that height, is climbing toward the nearest.

                    NOTES ON THIS DRAFT

Changed since the second draft, from the two reviews:

The fleet earns its refusal.
The flagship sends the terms and the truce up in clear, so
every ship reads them. By the Admiral's order every ship
counts the road out of Sarn Vale. When Cassiel signals, no
gunner will be first, and no lieutenant gives the word. Then
one small ship anchors. Not all follow: some keep their
colours and do nothing.

A truce, and its limit.
Karst brings ten seals of eleven: no hand raised against
anyone who comes down unarmed, until the harvest is in. The
eleventh seal is Marl's, and she says so at the wheel. This
is the first paper Serath reads.

Voices.
The hierophant decrees and the chancellor orders. The
engineer hesitates. The flag captain reports what he cannot
stop seeing. Marl speaks from two days of digging.

An older hand.
The Weir has no seam and no tool-mark. Under the treaty's
two scripts runs a third that belongs to neither people.
Nothing is explained.

Scale that can be shot.
The four million are felt, not counted: terraces climbing
into the dusk, every rail lined, until the people are too
far to be people.

Smaller fixes.
The sluice is explained by contrast: close it and the weight
backs up, break it and it pours. The ship in the last shot
is Cassiel's.

Proposed in this script where the concept left the matter
open: the names Orison, Karst, Marl, Cassiel, the Weir,
Anvil Vale and Sarn Vale; the eruption, and "until the fire
is stone"; weight that behaves like water; light above and
heavy below; lifting warrants under Serath's seal; the
harvest truce and its ten seals; the farmer and the petal
girl as the two witnesses of the ending.
