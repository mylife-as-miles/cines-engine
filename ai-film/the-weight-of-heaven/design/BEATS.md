# The Weight of Heaven: beats

Split as *My Name Is Oren* was (branch `ccr-a1ca9fd6-0m96vq`, `ai-film/my-name-is-oren/design/BEATS.md`). The screenplay is split into **42 beats in eight movements**. A beat is one change: something happens, and something is different after it.

**Script ids** point to `design/script.json`, which `tools/parse_script.py` (Oren's parser) builds from the screenplay: L01–L45 are the 45 lines of dialogue (the same ids as the line book, `audio/lines.json`) and A01–A121 the action, scene headings and transitions. No dialogue is retyped.

**Timing.** The draft at the Visual Bible's pace runs 14:05. The cut is **6:40** (author), and it gets there by design rather than by speeding up (`design/CUT_PLAN.md`):
- **Four movements are montages cut to the song** (I, III, V, VII). They compress time: whole scenes play as a few shots each, intercut on the bars.
- **Four movements are held** (II, IV, VI, VIII). They keep the bible's holds.
- **37 of the 45 lines are kept, 12 of them trimmed by cuts only** (a proposal awaiting the author). Cut lines are marked *(cut)* below; trimmed ones *(trim)*.

Times in the held movements come from the kept lines' word counts at an unhurried 2.4 words a second, plus the action. Times in the montages snap to the song's bars (84 BPM, 2.86 s a bar) once the song is recorded. A table read will confirm them.

## The spine

Serath seals what she never reads. The film is the afternoon she reads it, and then chooses which weight to carry: the city's, or a valley of forty thousand.

**Hands are the film's hidden structure.** One gesture carries four meanings, then a fifth (the Visual Bible's "Hand" page):
- **gloved, it seals** without reading (B04);
- **bare, it knows**, on the shard (B20);
- **raised, it could fire** (B27);
- **flat, it anchors** (B28);
- the girl's two bare palms on the stone close the set (B41).

The hymn runs the other way. "What heaven has raised, let no hand bring down" is sung in the cathedral (B02) and sent back as the Council's answer (B27). Then it is undone, first by Karst's "We would have you come down" (B21), then by the song's last words (B37), and finally by a child stepping down onto the ground (B41).
