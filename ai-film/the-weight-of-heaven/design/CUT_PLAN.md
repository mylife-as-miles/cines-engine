# The Weight of Heaven: the 6:40 cut

The author's decision (25 November): the film runs **6:30 to 6:50**, not 14:05, and parts of it become **montages cut to music** to move the story faster. The model is the music-driven montage of game cinematics and animated series (Arcane, the League of Legends cinematics): parallel actions intercut on the beat, a musical hook carrying time forward, and a hard stop into silence when a moment must land. We borrow the technique only. The film stays live action, its look stays the Visual Bible's, and the music is our own.

Everything below is a proposal until the author approves it. Nothing is cut from the script without the author's yes: the line table in section 5 is a proposal.

---

## 1. The shape

Four montages carry time; four held blocks carry meaning. The film is planned at **6:40**. The hall (block D) can grow or shrink by ten seconds, and the opening and ending by five each, so the same plan cuts to 6:30 or 6:50.

| Block | Time (6:40) | Script | Kind | Music |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. The bell | 0:00 to 0:40 | Scenes 1 to 3 | **Montage** | M1 "Let No Hand" |
| B. The council | 0:40 to 1:30 | Scene 4 | Dialogue | Low score, under the voices |
| C. Below | 1:30 to 2:00 | Scenes 5 and 6 | **Montage**, ends on Karst's line | M2 "Below" |
| D. The hall | 2:00 to 3:35 | Scene 7 | Dialogue: the heart of the film | Almost none |
| E. The fleet comes down | 3:35 to 4:30 | Scenes 8 and 9 | **Montage**, then a hard stop | M3 "The Fleet" |
| F. Anchors | 4:30 to 5:15 | Scene 10.1 to 10.14 | Held wides, then a release | Silence, then M4 enters |
| G. The descent | 5:15 to 5:50 | Scene 10.15 to 10.23 | **Montage** | M4 "Come Down" |
| H. The first ground | 5:50 to 6:40 | Scene 11 | Held, silent | Silence, then one returning note at "six more" |

About 2:40 of the film is montage and 4:00 is held. The time goes where the words are: the hall gets 95 seconds and the council 50, so that each still has room for its pictures (the hand on the shard, the warrants on the floor) and is not just talk. The story beats are all still there; what goes is the time between them.

## 2. What never speeds up

The Visual Bible's holds stay holds. These moments are never cut on the beat and never scored over:

1. **The bell is struck (1.10)** and its note hangs over the cut to Anvil Vale.
2. **"I did not read it" (7.10)**: her bare hand on the shard.
3. **"…Guns, Admiral?" / "Anchors." (9.11 to 9.12)**: the music stops dead before the question.
4. **The first anchor falls its whole mile (10.1)**: one iron note, like a bell, and nothing follows it.
5. **No gun answers (10.4)**: Cassiel's chop, then silence on the gun deck.
6. **The landing (10.22)**: the hum and the music stop together.
7. **The girl and the farmer (11.11 to 11.14)**: the last look, held.

The montages get their force from these stops.

## 3. The four montages

**A. The bell (0:00 to 0:40).** The film opens over black on the bell's single stroke, then cuts back to before it. The wedding and Anvil Vale are intercut as one sequence on the beat: petals falling too slowly above, the plough and bent backs below; the seal pressed into wax above, the water trembling in the bowl below; the barge letting go above, the crows unable to climb below. The congregation's response, "Let no hand bring down", is the musical hook, sung by the ten thousand. On the strike of the hammer the mountain's face lets go, in silence. The sound arrives late. Cut to the gold cup above, perfectly still.

**C. Below (1:30 to 2:00).** The cutter drops out of the cloud. Shots run fast and low: the underside of the kingdom, the basalt, the Weir with its nine gates, a bird dropping like a stone, Sarn Vale's hearths. The cutter sets down, Serath's knees almost go and the aide falls. Refugees grey with dust, Marl handing out spears, the farmer's hand on the bowl at his belt. The music cuts on Karst in the doorway: "We did not attack your city, Admiral. We stopped carrying it."

**E. The fleet comes down (3:35 to 4:30).** The cloud breaks and the fleet comes through it. The gunports open in rows, and that sound, a city unlocking its doors, becomes the percussion. The intercut runs between:
- the signal lamps flashing the truce from ship to ship, and officers reading over shoulders;
- the road out of Sarn Vale, black with people, and the woman with a door on her back;
- Marl and the fighters putting their shoulders to the wheel, and Karst with his hand on the pin;
- the tally coming down the fleet lamp to lamp: "They are carrying beds";
- the Council's lamp flashing its answer, "What heaven has raised —".

Serath raises her hand, and on every ship a signal arm rises with it. The music climbs and then stops. Silence. "…Guns, Admiral?" "Anchors."

**G. The descent (5:15 to 5:50).** A kingdom comes down out of the sky. The ground's hum rises into the music, intercut on the beat:
- the ceiling of rock, then the terraces, bridges and gardens, with the cathedral tower holding the last sun;
- the armies seeing what cast the shadow, some kneeling;
- inside Orison, the banners dropping and hanging straight, the Hierophant sitting down, the Chancellor seeing green for the first time.

On the landing, the dust rolls out in one ring, and the hum and the music stop together.

## 4. Music

The score is built on one original theme: the cathedral's response, **"Let no hand bring down"**, set as a hymn. It belongs to Orison at the start, sung by ten thousand in white. It returns in M3 as the fleet's own pride and in M4 as the city falls slowly onto the ground. At "six more" it comes back as a single note, unresolved.

| Cue | Block | Character | Ends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 "Let No Hand" | A | Choir and low strings over a slow pulse; the hymn's first statement | Cut by the mountain's silence, then the still cup |
| Council bed | B | Sustained low strings, barely there | Under the Hierophant's last line |
| M2 "Below" | C | Low, heavy percussion, the hum underneath, no melody | Hard cut on Karst's first line |
| M3 "The Fleet" | E | Full: drums built from the gunports' sound, brass, the hymn in the low brass, building in steps | Dead stop before "…Guns, Admiral?" |
| M4 "Come Down" | F (late) and G | Enters as the anchors rain; the hymn in full choir, slower and heavier, the hum rising inside it | Stops with the hum on the landing |
| End note | H | One sustained choral note | Into the title |

**Words or no words.** The montages in the reference works are often driven by songs with lyrics. Here the hymn's one line, "Let no hand bring down", can be the only sung words, and its meaning turns by the end. Score, choir and the hymn are made in house: Lyria for the instrumental score; a choral or song model for the sung hymn, chosen when we audition. Each cue is cut to the picture, and then the picture is recut to the beat.

## 6. What it means for the shots

- Montage shots run one to two seconds on screen, trimmed from five-second generations: plenty of motion, one clear action each. Held shots run as long as the bible asks.
- The bible's roughly 140 shot numbers stay the map. In the montages some shots shrink to a single beat, and some are cut: likely the crows (2.5), the girl's basket (1.7), the boy on his father's shoulders (9.6) and the jumping boy (11.3), as in the bible's own twelve-minute cut.
- The shot list is built against this plan: each block gets its time budget, and each montage is cut to its cue.
