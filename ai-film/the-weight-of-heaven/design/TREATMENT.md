# The Weight of Heaven: treatment

A film by Osita Miles. Built on the screenplay's third draft and the Visual Bible, version 1 (both 24 November 2026, in `design/source/`). Where this page and those documents disagree, they win.

## Logline (proposed)

A floating kingdom is sinking under its own weight. Its Grand Admiral is sent down to take back the Weir that carries that weight away, or break it by the evening bell and drown a valley of forty thousand people. On the ground she learns that every warrant she ever sealed dropped a mountain on someone below. At the evening bell she does not fire: she lets go the anchors and brings heaven down.

## The film in one paragraph

Orison, one of seven sky kingdoms, celebrates eight hundred years aloft by hanging a ninety-ton bell; in the same hour a mountain in Anvil Vale, one of the bearing-lands that carry the kingdom's weight, lets go. Three days later the city is falling, the people below hold the Weir that carries its weight away, and the Council gives Grand Admiral Serath until the evening bell: if the Weir is not returned, break it and let the weight pour onto Sarn Vale. At the Treaty House the engineer Karst shows her the warrants under her own seal, the shard of Anvil Vale, the eight-hundred-year-old treaty and the offer: close the Weir slowly and set the city down on the dead basalt plain. The Council refuses. She raises her hand, and every gun in the fleet waits for it, and she lays it flat: anchors. Her deputy Cassiel orders fire; no gunner will be first. The fleet anchors, Karst turns the wheel, and the kingdom comes down out of the sky onto the stone. The hum that has been in the ground for eight hundred years stops, the people of the ground stand upright for the first time, and a girl from the cathedral steps off the last stair onto a ground that has no edge, ten paces from a farmer with a spear and a wooden bowl. On the horizon, six more kingdoms are still in the air.

## The look, in words

Light above, heavy below. White marble in cloud, lit softly from every side: columns too thin to stand, petals falling too slowly, gloved hands. Below, grey daylight comes in low under a dark mass that fills the top of the frame: black basalt, bent backs, buttressed stone, bare hands. Wide frames, small figures, long holds. A gold collar, a red wax seal, one green valley, an amber dusk under the cloud, blue last light. Fine grain. No glow, no lens flare, and no gun ever fires.

## What the film never shows

Weight as glow, beams or shimmer. A gun firing. A low heroic angle on Serath. Open sky over the plain before the landing. Handheld camera, except as the valley runs (2.8). A teal-and-orange grade. Readable English on any prop. An explanation of the Weir's stone or the third script.
