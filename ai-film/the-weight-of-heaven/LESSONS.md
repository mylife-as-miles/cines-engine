## Villeneuve for the camera, Cameron for the storytelling

The Visual Bible's own list ("What we take from Villeneuve and Cameron"), applied to every prompt. It borrows the method only, never a frame.

**Villeneuve (cinematography):**
- **A human anchor:** a small figure in the big frame, so its size can be judged.
- **The held wide:** stay back and let the shot outlast comfort, whenever weight or scale is the point.
- **Designed frames:** symmetry for Orison, a figure isolated off-centre.
- **Natural sources, low and soft:** in Orison the cloud is the source.
- **A line timed to a reveal.**

**Cameron (visual storytelling):**
- **Everything is based on something real.** The barge releases like a ship's crane setting a load down on a quay. The hammer is drawn back on ropes like a church striker. The wax is pressed and lifted the way a real seal is.
- **Cause, then effect, each readable on its own:** the release, then the settle; the swing, then the strike, then the shudder.
- **Borrowed movement:** above, cloth and petals move as if under water.
- **White is never one white:** cool where the cloud lights it, warm where the sun reaches.
