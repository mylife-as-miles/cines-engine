# The Weight of Heaven: lessons (running list)

What this production teaches, kept as it happens, as *My Name Is Oren* kept its own (`ai-film/my-name-is-oren/LESSONS.md`, branch `ccr-a1ca9fd6-0m96vq`). Each later prompt is checked against this page before it goes to the author.

## From Q01 t1 (the first Seedance 2.5 draft)

| # | Lesson | Evidence | Applied in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **An action needs its own time.** "The chains fall slack, slowly, and the bell settles" inside a 2.9 s shot came back as a still picture: nothing moved. Give every physical change at least one bar (2.9 s), write it as a change from a start state to an end state with times ("at 1.5 s the barge lets go; by 4 s the chains hang in loose loops and the bell rests on the beam"), and give it a held wide where it can be seen. | Q01 t1 shot 5. | Q02 shot 5: the release gets two bars (5.7 s) in a held wide. |
| 2 | **A reference image fixes an object's scale and kind.** The hammer sheet reads as a sledgehammer, so the take hung a sledgehammer from a chain, small against the bell. State size against something in the same frame ("its iron head taller than a man, as long as a door, the bell twice its height") and show them together in a wide. | Q01 t1 shot 6. | Q02 shot 6: the hammer beside the bell, side on, in a wide; its size written against the bell and the beam. |
| 3 | **The last shot gets squeezed.** The strike, the last thing in the prompt, got 0.5 s, and the take ended before it landed. Never end a generation on its climax: put the climax early in its shot and hold after it ("the strike at 0.8 s, then hold on the shuddering bell"), and give the generation a quiet final shot. | Q01 t1 shot 7. | Q02: the strike lands 0.8 s into shot 7, and a held insert (the still cup) follows it. |
| 4 | **Wordless openings run long** (Oren lesson 34 again). The nave ran 6.7 s for 5.7 planned, and everything after it shifted. Give every shot an exact window and a "cut at" time, and keep the opening shot to one bar unless it carries words. | Q01 t1: cuts 6.67, 10.54, 13.83, 16.33, 18.13, 19.54 against a board of 5.71, 8.57, 11.43, 14.29, 17.14, 18.57. | Q02 shot 1 is one bar, opening on the start frame. |
| 5 | **"A slow drift" alone gets a locked-off shot.** Give a camera move a direction, a distance and what slides past ("the camera glides 6 m down the aisle; the columns pass on both sides"). | Q01 t1 shot 1 (static). | Every Q02 move has a direction and a distance. |
| 6 | **The model chose its own reverse angle, and it worked.** It put the camera behind the Hierophant rather than behind the couple, so the couple faced the lens. Say what is in the foreground and who faces camera; otherwise the model picks. | Q01 t1 shot 3. | Q02 names the foreground and who faces camera in each shot. |
| 7 | **Name where the crowd is.** "Looking down along the congregation" put the guests in the opposite gallery. Say "on the nave floor below" or "in the galleries". | Q01 t1 shot 4. | Q02 shot 1 continues the gallery view as it came back. |
| 8 | **Music slips in under "No music"** (Oren lesson 53). The take carries an ambient score bed. Harmless here: the montage's sound is the song and our own voices, laid in the edit. | Q01 t1, Gemini's listen: "ominous ambient music". | Unchanged; the sound of every montage take is a guide only. |
| 9 | **No 1080p** (the author, 25 November). Takes are not completed to 1080p. | | All generations at 480p. |
| 10 | **The author sees everything before anything is generated:** the storyboard and animatic, the references in order, the exact prompt, the parameters and the cost. Nothing is sent without their go. | The author, 25 November. | Q02 onward. |

| # | Lesson | Evidence | Applied in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 17 | **Words alone do not direct.** A prompt of shots, lenses and timings gave generic coverage twice: the model chose its own staging, merged shots, and ignored sizes. The staging has to be shown. | Q01 t1 shots 5–7; Q02 t1 throughout. | Q02 v2: the previs itself is attached as @Video 1, a coarse block-out. |
| 18 | **A block-out gives blocking, paths, timing, camera and cuts, and nothing else** (fal's Seedance 2.5 guide, "Blockout References and Rendering"). Say so in the first line; forbid its look ("do not use its blockout appearance, materials or scene"); map every shape to its subject, one sentence each; give appearance with images. Do not restate every move in words, or the words fight the reference. | fal.ai/learn/devs/how-to-use-seedance-2-5. | Q02A and Q02B v2 prompts follow that template line for line. |
| 19 | **Keep the block-out clean and simple.** No labels, mattes, path lines, axes or camera frustums: they leak. Simple geometry; limbs only where the action is complete. Colour-code the principals so the mapping cannot be mistaken. | fal's guide; the export is `CLEAN=1 node animatic.js`. | `refs/blockout/Q02A_blockout.mp4`, `Q02B_blockout.mp4`. |
| 20 | **Scale comes from people in the frame.** The hammer's sheet is left out; two bell-ringers stand beside it in the block-out and haul it on ropes. | Lesson 12, twice. | Q02B shot 2. |
| 21 | **Never re-open on the last shot of the previous sequence.** Q02 t1 opened on Q01's gallery and held it 6.9 s under invented singing, straight after the same people had spoken. Open on a cut to something new, and keep sound concrete: "reverberation" in a cathedral came back as a choir. | Q02 t1 shot 1; the author. | Q02A opens on a cut to Serath; it is generated silent, and Q01's own tail is laid under it in the edit. |
| 22 | **Shorter generations.** fal's guide recommends 5 to 10 s per reference video; a failed 15 s costs half of a failed 30 s. | fal's guide; Q02 t1 ($5.73 for nothing kept). | Q02 is split into Q02A (15 s) and Q02B (16 s). |
| 23 | **With a video reference the bill changes**: tokens = output height × width × (input + output seconds) × 24 / 1024, at $0.0128 per 1,000 at 480p: about $0.129 for every second of block-out plus every second generated. | fal's model page. | Costs in `prompts/Q02.md`. |
| 24 | **A clean block-out is obeyed.** Q02A t1 cut within a frame of the block-out's three cuts, kept every camera position, the push and the track, and timed the trip, the catch and the press to it; no colour or mannequin leaked. What it still invents is what the block-out does not show: a stray bell in a background, the basket turning over in the air. Put anything that must not be invented into the block-out or the mapping. | Q02A t1: cuts 2.88, 8.58, 11.42 s against 2.86, 8.57, 11.43. | Every generation from here. |
| 25 | **Sound is always on.** Q02A t1 was generated silent, on my call, to avoid invented singing; the author could not judge it and had to pay for it again. Keep the model's sound on and write it as cues over a low continuous bed (`score-and-sound-design.md` §6: source, material, action; no word like "reverberation" that invites a choir). | The author, 25 November: "Why silent". | Q02A v3 and everything after. |
| 26 | **The block-out is obeyed, so its physics must be right.** In Q02A t1 the basket flew up to head height and turned over, and Serath caught it at full arm's length, because the block-out drew it that way. Build motion from mechanics, not from keyframes that look busy: a cause (her toe catches her hem), a ballistic path under one stated gravity (Orison's slow fall, about 1 m/s²), a contact where the object really is (her hanging hand, a hand's width of movement), follow-through (the wrist gives, the basket swings once), and a reaction after it (the girl sits back and looks up). From the handbook's references: behaviour, never labels; a hit is one causal event with preparation, contact, body and tail; reactions lag 0.3 to 0.5 s; distances in metres. | Q02A t1 shot 2; the author: "not natural and not real physics". | `tools/board/board.html`, the `tripT` sequence (v3). |
| 27 | **The author's budget is limited: every generation must count.** No take is spent to test an idea that the block-out, the board or a still could test free. One take per generation; the package is shown first; a retake needs a named fault and a fix that can be seen in the block-out before any money is spent. | The author, 25 November. | From here on. |
| 28 | **The block-out is staging, not motion** (the author, 25 November). Say so in the prompt's first lines: use @Video 1 only for camera positions, camera movement, cuts, where each subject stands and travels, and when each event happens; do not copy the stiff way its figures move; all movement is the model's own, with real weight, balance and physics. Practitioners say the same: the clay reference is for camera travel, subject position and reveal timing, and limbed stand-ins make motion stiff unless told otherwise. | Q02A t2: the girl's fall and recovery came back natural, with the cuts still on the block-out's frames and no music. | Every prompt from here. |
| 29 | **Every block in the block-out must be mapped, and built in its real shape.** In Q02B t1 the ringers' platform, a slab on a post that the prompt never named, came back as a flat white panel on a pole; the beam, a plain grey box, came back as timber; the barge, off the top of the frame, was not drawn at all. Name every visible shape and its material in the mapping, keep unnamed shapes out of frame, and frame what must be seen. | Q02B t1 shots 1 and 2. | Future block-outs. |
| 30 | **Edit before paying for a pickup.** Four takes, none approved whole, made a working movement when cut for their good shots: hide (a crop took the unreal ringers out), separate (a choir bridge joined Q01 to Q02), and cause-then-effect across takes (the lifted warrant from one take, the falling chains from another). Look for the cut first; generate only what no cut can give. | Cut v1 of movement I, 33.5 s from Q01 t1, Q02 t1, Q02A t2 and Q02B t1. | Every sequence: an edit pass before any retake. |
| 31 | **Put a cut in front of independent reviewers before calling it done.** Five agents, each with one lens (Villeneuve's camera, Cameron's storytelling, editing, sound, the look and the opening), none shown the editor's notes, found what the editor had not: the edit's own faults (a seal sound 125 ms late, two thumps from crossfade tails, frozen last frames) and the central one (a cold viewer does not see the premise). Give each the script, the shot list and the tools to look and measure; have them tag every fix free or paid. | `edit/reviews/I_bell_v4_review.md`. | Before any cut goes to the author as finished. |
| 32 | **Finish in this order:** the cut with nothing that moves the frame; the grade (its accent and shaping regions are fixed rectangles on each shot's plain framing); then pushes, shakes, punch-ins and fades; grain once over everything; the matte; the sound. | `tools/finish_bell.py`. | Every cut. |
| 33 | **On a white film, whole-frame statistics mislead.** Most of the "mismatch" between shots was content, not grade; match like for like (cloth with cloth, sky with sky). Gold and seal red share a hue with brown skin: keep the accent keys off faces by region and test against detected faces. | `grade/README.md`. | The grade. |
| 34 | **In the mix, hierarchy is measured, not assumed.** The biggest event must measure biggest (the stroke now 7 LU over the loudest line; it was 0.2 under). A bell needs its own partials: a correct 48 Hz hum alone reads as a gong, and it vanishes on a phone, so check a phone-band version too. One long pass of an original choir, never a looped phrase. Take the tails of other takes out before they thump into the next shot. | `audio/I_bell_v5_cues.md`. | Every mix. |
| 35 | **Tool notes for the next sound job.** Soundbox now installs weights with `pull <model>`, not `model install`; build audio.cpp with only the Stable Audio model (about four minutes); opus-sound-layer could not be cloned and freesound.org is blocked here, so small Foley came from earshot's catalogue and the repository's own CC0 files; Stable Audio cannot make "gloved" applause or a convincing steady wind. | The foundry's report, 26 November. | `references/sound.md` when next revised. |
| 36 | **Check a delegated build within minutes, and build the previs myself.** The agent given the Anvil Vale set wrote nothing for fifty minutes before the author asked why it was slow; the set, the shot lists and the exports then took the editor less time than the wait. A set is quick to iterate by rendering stills and looking; hand out independent, checkable jobs (a grade, a sound library, a review), not the thing every other step waits on. | Q03, 26 November. | Every sequence. |
| 37 | **Run the cold-viewer test on every take before the author sees it.** A witness shown Q03A t1 with no context retold the scene correctly (the water, the tremor, the animals, the line on the mountain, "a massive inverted landmass" overhead), which is the check the bell sequence failed. It also showed what the block-out leaked: a line drawn straight in the previs came back as a clean cut across the cliff, not as dust. Draw a thing in the block-out as rough as it should be. | Q03A t1, `out/review/Q03A_t1_witness.json`. | Every take. |

## Villeneuve for the camera, Cameron for the storytelling

The Visual Bible's own list ("What we take from Villeneuve and Cameron"), applied to every prompt. It borrows the method only, never a frame.

Studied for Q02 v2 on StillsLab's Villeneuve pages (Dune: Part Two, Dune: Part One, Blade Runner 2049, Arrival: about 230 frames) and its essay on his style, "Scale, Silence, and Letting the Frame Talk". What recurs, and where Q02 uses it:

| What recurs in his frames | Used in Q02 v2 |
| --- | --- |
| One figure centred and alone in a pale, almost empty frame; symmetry; a slow push | A1: Serath one step ahead of the rank, frontal |
| Profiles and silhouettes read against empty space; the eyes off camera | A2: the catch in profile, her eyes forward |
| A dark or large foreground shape with the face beyond it, from low | A3: the warrant low in the frame, her face above |
| The extreme insert of hands and one small object | A4: the ring into the wax, hands only |
| The tilt or low angle that reveals a huge object in haze; a held frame after it | B1: from the seal up the tower to the bell |
| Small people beside a vast machine, so its size can be felt | B2: two ringers and the hammer |
| The graphic top-down frame | B3: the cup |

**Villeneuve (cinematography):**
- **A human anchor:** a small figure in the big frame, so its size can be judged.
- **The held wide:** stay back and let the shot outlast comfort, whenever weight or scale is the point.
- **Designed frames:** symmetry for Orison, a figure isolated off-centre.
- **Natural sources, low and soft:** in Orison the cloud is the source.
- **A line timed to a reveal.**

**Cameron (visual storytelling):**
- **Everything is based on something real.** The barge releases like a ship's crane setting a load down on a quay. The hammer is drawn back on ropes like a church striker. The wax is pressed and lifted the way a real seal is.
- **Cause, then effect, each readable on its own:** the release, then the settle; the swing, then the strike, then the shudder.
- **Borrowed movement:** above, cloth and petals move as if under water.
- **White is never one white:** cool where the cloud lights it, warm where the sun reaches.
