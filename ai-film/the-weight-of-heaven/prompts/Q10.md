## Why it is staged this way

- **It continues from Q09's last frame**, as the author joins scenes. Karst has turned to her and is silent there; here he speaks.
- **What the papers are is seen before it is said.** The lamp passes over them first: paper, a small map, a red mark, and a piece of a house on every sheet. Then, on her face, "I drew where each one would fall. You sealed them." Cameron's order: the thing, then its name.
- **His hardest line is heard on her face.** She is the one it is said to, and no lips have to be matched for twelve of his twenty words.
- **Lamplight, not daylight, on the floor.** The shot list: "paper takes the light and basalt does not." The approved hall shows the whole floor in daylight once the shutters are open, and a take wears its reference's light; so the open hall is relit here (free) with the daylight only at the opening, and the rows she walks are in the hall's shadow. The lamp is the first warm light in the film below the kingdom.
- **The ink on his fingers carries the lamp.** The hand that drew them is the hand that lights them; it is the only part of him in shots 2 and 4.
- **The shard has its own shot, and she does not touch it yet.** Her bare hand on it is Q11, the hinge of the film.
- **Her eyes are seen.** She looks down at the floor for most of the take; the camera is put below her eye line, at her chest and then at her knee, so that it looks up into a downturned face lit from below.
- **The last shot has one sentence and then nothing.** If the hold comes back long it is trimmed; if her line is dropped, the shot alone is a five-second pickup.
