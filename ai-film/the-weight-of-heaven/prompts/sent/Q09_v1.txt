## SCENE INTENT
On the ground, beneath a kingdom that floats in the sky, that kingdom's Grand Admiral, Serath, has been brought into an old dark hall by Karst, an engineer of her own people who went over to the people below nineteen years ago. One whole wall of the hall is the treaty between the sky and the ground, cut in black rock eight hundred years ago. She finds she can read it: it is in the language of her prayers. She reads its end aloud and does not yet see what it means. Karst opens the shutters on the plain outside and tells her. She answers with what she has always been taught; he answers that, and he is not unkind. Every voice stays low. She is learning something; he has known it for years.

## MOVIE COLOR
Black rock, white cloth, grey daylight; the gold of Serath's collar is the only colour. Shots 1 and 2 are low key, about two stops under: exposed for her white coat in weak light, so that the coat is a dim grey, her face sits just above black, the basalt is near black with only the edges of its cut marks catching light, and the blade of daylight between the shutters is the one bright thing in the frame. From shot 3 the exposure is set for the daylight on a face: the sky in the opening burns pale, the hall around stays dark, the basalt takes almost no light and cloth and paper take it. A face is never the brightest thing in the frame. The daylight is cool and soft, an overcast sky, with no sunbeams.
Influences, each with its purpose — Denis Villeneuve, Dune and Blade Runner 2049: one small pale figure before a vast dark wall; a profile edged with light against black; a figure as a dark shape in a bright doorway; a line timed to what is revealed; a hard thing heard on the face of the one it is said to. James Cameron: cause, then effect, each readable alone: the pull, the shutters part, the light arrives, the plain is seen; every look answered by what it looks at.
Camera package — ARRI Alexa 65, large format, spherical primes, fine grain. Shallow focus on faces. Every shot is on a steady camera and moves slowly or not at all.

## VOICE DNA (immutable)
SERATH: The high speech of Orison: a soft, unplaced Received Pronunciation, older and gentler than a modern one, with no regional mark. A woman of fifty-five with a low alto, warm but closed, a little grain at the bottom of the range. She speaks at a measured pace and always finishes her sentences; she never hurries and never raises her voice. Here, on the ground, her breath is a little heavier between phrases; her voice itself does not change.
KARST: The grammar of the court spoken with the vowels of the ground: an educated Received Pronunciation flattened toward a northern English plainness by nineteen years below. A man of fifty-six with a dry, tired baritone, a slight rasp, low energy. He is unhurried. His sentences end flat and falling. He is never angry. When something matters most he gets quieter, not louder.
No one else is in the hall.

## AUDIO DESIGN
A stone hall, close and dry, with no echo. In shots 1 and 2: stillness, her breath, a thread of wind at the gap of the shutters. In shot 3: old iron hinges, the weight of oak moving, then the open wind of the plain coming in, which stays under shots 4 and 5. Both voices are close and clear in every shot.

## PERFORMANCE ARC
SERATH: reads an old text aloud as a scholar would → hears its last words as she says them → sees the plain → answers with what she was taught, and for the first time it does not sound like enough.
KARST: waits while she reads → opens the shutters and states the fact to the plain → answers her without heat → gets quieter where it matters.

## THEMATIC / SYMBOLIC / STRUCTURAL LOGIC
Her own scripture says the lifting was meant to end. The proof is the ground outside the door. The dark room is what she was taught; the daylight is what is so.

## PHYSICS LOCKS
Normal speed and real weight throughout. The shutters are heavy oak: they start slowly, swing slowly and stop with their own weight. Cloth hangs and stirs in the wind once the shutters are open. Dust turns in the daylight, a little. Heads turn at a living pace, never snapping. Hands and fingers move slowly and exactly.

Each quoted line is spoken once, word for word, by the one named, in the order written, and nothing else is said by anyone. Serath's mouth moves only during her own lines; Karst's only during his. Speech is unhurried in every line. No music or singing.
