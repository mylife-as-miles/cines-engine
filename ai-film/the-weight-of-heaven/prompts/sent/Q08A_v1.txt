## THE WEIGHT OF HEAVEN — Q08A · SARN VALE · THE TREATY HOUSE YARD · HER OWN WEIGHT
References: @Image 1 — first frame of shot 1: the valley from the top of the black wall · @Image 2 — first frame of shot 2: the yard of the old fort, the cutter set down, Serath at the foot of its gangway · @Image 3 — character sheet: SERATH
Reference mode · 11 s · 3 shots · 2.35:1 · photoreal live action · no speech · no music

## SCENE INTENT
Three separate views, cut hard. First the valley that lies beyond the black wall: farms, sheep, smoke, all of it alive and unaware. Then the yard of an old stone fort on the ground, where a white naval cutter has set down. Serath, Grand Admiral of the kingdom that floats above the cloud, has never stood on the ground. She steps onto it and for the first time in her life feels her own weight: her knees almost give. Behind her, her aide falls outright. The people of the ground watch the sky people learn to stand. Nobody laughs. Last, close: the gold collar of her rank drags at her neck like a hand, and she lifts her head against it.

## MOVIE COLOR
The colour, light and haze of @Image 1 and @Image 2, unchanged. The valley: soft grey day, muted green. The yard: cold grey and black, rock dust in the air; the cutter, the admiral and the aide are the only white things, and the gold of her collar is the only colour. Exposed for the pale things; faces are lit softly from the side and are never the brightest thing in the frame. Blacks deep but never crushed.
Influences, each with its purpose — Denis Villeneuve, Arrival and Dune: the one who does not belong, sharp and pale among dark still watchers; a body told by weight; stillness. James Cameron: a thing felt for the first time shown on the body before the face.

## PERFORMANCE ARC
SERATH: steps down as she has stepped off a thousand decks → her body fails her for half a second → she will not let it show → she holds her head up against the weight of her own rank.
THE AIDE: follows her → falls.
THE WATCHERS: still.

## THEMATIC / SYMBOLIC / STRUCTURAL LOGIC
Weight is real, and she has never felt it. The people who carry it all their lives watch her find out.
