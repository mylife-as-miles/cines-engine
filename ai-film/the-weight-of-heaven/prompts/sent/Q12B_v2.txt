## SCENE INTENT
Serath, Grand Admiral of a kingdom of cities held up in the sky, and Karst, an engineer of her own people who went over to the people of the ground long ago, are in an old hall on a great empty plain. He has told her the plain could hold her whole kingdom and she has asked what he wants for it; now he states his terms, she answers with what she takes them to mean, and he corrects her, more gently than he has said anything; then both are silent. It plays as one quiet, unbroken conversation: two faces, each framed alone, every voice low, nothing hurried.

## MOVIE COLOR
Dark masonry, pale cloth, grey daylight; the gold of Serath's collar is the only strong colour. All four shots are low key, as @Image 3 and @Image 4: the faces dim, the room dark around them, the background near black.
Influences, each with its purpose — Denis Villeneuve, Arrival and Blade Runner 2049: a face close and dim against the dark; stillness; a line said only after the other has been looked at. James Cameron: every look is answered by what it looks at.
Camera package — ARRI Alexa 65, large format, spherical primes, fine grain. Shallow focus on the eyes. Every shot is on a steady, planted camera and does not move.

## VOICE DNA (immutable)
SERATH: The high speech of Orison: a soft, unplaced Received Pronunciation, older and gentler than a modern one, with no regional mark. A woman of fifty-five with a low alto, warm but closed, a little grain at the bottom of the range. She speaks at a measured pace; she never hurries, and her voice stays low but fully voiced, never a whisper.
KARST: The grammar of the court spoken with the vowels of the ground: an educated Received Pronunciation flattened toward a northern English plainness by nineteen years below. A man of fifty-six with a dry, tired baritone, a slight rasp, low energy. He is unhurried. His sentences end flat. He is never angry. When something matters most he gets quieter, not louder. His voice is the same voice in shot 1 and in shot 3.
Only these two are in the hall.

## AUDIO DESIGN
The sound of a bare room of masonry, recorded as it is: close, dry and very quiet, with a little of the room on the voices. Far off and faint, air moving over open ground. Both voices are close and clear. Nothing else sounds.
