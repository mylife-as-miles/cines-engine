## SCENE INTENT
Serath, Grand Admiral of a kingdom of the sky, has been brought down into an old dark hall on the plain by Karst, an engineer of her own people who went over to the people below long ago. Set against one wall of the hall is the treaty between her people and the people below, cut in black rock centuries ago. She finds she can read it: it is in the old tongue of her own people. She reads its end aloud and does not yet see what it means. Karst opens the shutters on the plain outside and tells her. She answers with what she has always been taught; he answers that, and he is not unkind. Every voice stays low. She is learning something; he has known it a long time.

## MOVIE COLOR
Black rock, white cloth, grey daylight; the gold of Serath's collar is the only strong colour. Shots 1 and 2, and shot 3 until the shutters open, are low key, two stops under: nothing in the hall is brighter than a dim grey, her white coat is that dim grey, the basalt is near black with only the edges of its carved marks catching light. In shot 1 the weak grey daylight of the yard door, behind the camera, is the only light, and nothing in the frame is bright; in shot 2 the thin line of daylight between the shutters is the one bright thing in the frame. Once the shutters are open each shot has its own exposure: shots 3 and 5 are exposed for the sky in the opening, so that Karst and the hall are dark shapes against it; shot 4 is exposed for the daylight on Serath's face, a stop under, the hall behind her dark. The basalt takes almost no light; cloth takes it. A face is never the brightest thing in the frame. The daylight is cool and soft, from an overcast sky.
Influences, each with its purpose — Denis Villeneuve, Dune and Blade Runner 2049: one small pale figure before a vast dark wall; a face close and dim against black rock; a figure as a dark shape in a bright doorway; a line timed to what is revealed. James Cameron: cause, then effect, each readable alone: the pull, the shutters part, the light arrives, the plain is seen; every look answered by what it looks at.
Camera package — ARRI Alexa 65, large format, spherical primes, fine grain. Shallow focus on faces. Every shot is on a steady camera and moves slowly or not at all.

## VOICE DNA (immutable)
SERATH: The high speech of Orison: a soft, unplaced Received Pronunciation, older and gentler than a modern one, with no regional mark. A woman of fifty-five with a low alto, warm but closed, a little grain at the bottom of the range. She speaks at a measured pace; she never hurries, and her voice stays low. Here her breath can be heard, a little heavier; her voice itself does not change.
KARST: The grammar of the court spoken with the vowels of the ground: an educated Received Pronunciation flattened toward a northern English plainness by nineteen years below. A man of fifty-six with a dry, tired baritone, a slight rasp, low energy. He is unhurried. His sentences end flat and falling. He is never angry. When something matters most he gets quieter, not louder.
No one else is in the hall.

## PERFORMANCE ARC
SERATH: reads an old text aloud as a scholar would → hears its last words as she says them, small before the slab → sees the plain → answers with what she was taught, and for the first time it does not sound like enough.
KARST: waits while she reads → opens the shutters and states the fact to the plain → answers her without heat → gets quieter where it matters.

## THEMATIC / SYMBOLIC / STRUCTURAL LOGIC
The wall she can read says her kingdom's height was meant to end. The proof is the plain outside. The dark room is what she was taught; the daylight is what is so.
