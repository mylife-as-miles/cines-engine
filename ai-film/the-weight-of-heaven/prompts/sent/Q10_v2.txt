## SCENE INTENT
Serath, Grand Admiral of a kingdom of the sky, stands in an old hall on the plain with Karst, an engineer of her own people who went over to the people below long ago. He has opened the shutters on the dead plain outside, and she has answered him with what she was always taught. He answers that, without heat. Then he shows her what covers the floor of the hall: thirty years of her kingdom's orders, hundreds of sheets, each with her own mark in red wax, a stone lying on many of them. On the last sheet lies a broken piece of grey rock, and she recognises it. It plays as one unbroken conversation in a quiet room: two people, four plain shots, every voice low.

Influences, each with its purpose — Denis Villeneuve, Dune and Blade Runner 2049: a figure as a dark shape against a bright doorway; two figures in a great dark room with one small light between them; a face close and dim in profile; stillness, and a line timed to what is shown. ## VOICE DNA (immutable)
SERATH: The high speech of Orison: a soft, unplaced Received Pronunciation, older and gentler than a modern one, with no regional mark. A woman of fifty-five with a low alto, warm but closed, a little grain at the bottom of the range. She speaks at a measured pace; she never hurries, and her voice stays low but fully voiced, never a whisper.
KARST: The grammar of the court spoken with the vowels of the ground: an educated Received Pronunciation flattened toward a northern English plainness by nineteen years below. A man of fifty-six with a dry, tired baritone, a slight rasp, low energy. He is unhurried. His sentences end flat. He is never angry. When something matters most he gets quieter, not louder. His voice is the same voice in shot 1 and in shot 2.
No one else is in the hall.

## AUDIO DESIGN
A bare hall of rock, close and dry, with a little of its own echo on the voices. Under everything, low, the open air of the plain at the shutters. In shots 2, 3 and 4 the small steady hiss of the lamp's flame. Both voices are close and clear. No drone, no boom, no music.
