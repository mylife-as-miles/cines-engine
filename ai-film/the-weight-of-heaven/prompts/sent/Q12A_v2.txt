## SCENE INTENT
Serath, Grand Admiral of a kingdom of cities held up in the sky, and Karst, an engineer of her own people who went over to the people of the ground long ago, are in an old hall on a great empty plain. She has just admitted that she sealed orders she never read. Now he stands at the tall opening with his back to her and looks out at the plain, and he tells her what it could do for her kingdom; and she answers him in three words. It plays as one quiet, unbroken moment: a man looking out, a woman watching his back, both voices low, nothing hurried.

## MOVIE COLOR
Dark masonry, pale cloth, white sky; the gold of Serath's collar is the only strong colour. Shot 1 is exposed for the sky: the sky soft and white, never burnt out, the plain dark grey, the man a mid-dark shape before them. Shot 2 is low key: her face dim, the room dark around it.
Influences, each with its purpose — Denis Villeneuve, Arrival and Dune: a figure seen from behind at one side of the frame, with the wide empty thing it looks at filling the rest; stillness; a face close and dim against the dark. James Cameron: every look is answered by what it looks at.
Camera package — ARRI Alexa 65, large format, spherical primes, fine grain. Each shot is on a steady, planted camera and does not move.

## VOICE DNA (immutable)
SERATH: The high speech of Orison: a soft, unplaced Received Pronunciation, older and gentler than a modern one, with no regional mark. A woman of fifty-five with a low alto, warm but closed, a little grain at the bottom of the range. She speaks at a measured pace; she never hurries, and her voice stays low but fully voiced, never a whisper.
KARST: The grammar of the court spoken with the vowels of the ground: an educated Received Pronunciation flattened toward a northern English plainness by nineteen years below. A man of fifty-six with a dry, tired baritone, a slight rasp, low energy. He is unhurried. His sentences end flat. He is never angry. When something matters most he gets quieter, not louder.
Only these two are in the hall.

## AUDIO DESIGN
The sound of a bare room of masonry, recorded as it is: close, dry and very quiet, with a little of the room on the voices. Through the opening, far off and faint, air moving over open ground. Both voices are close and clear. Nothing else sounds.
