## SCENE INTENT
This follows the sequence before directly. The Council of Orison has just heard that holding the city's height means drowning a valley. Serath, the Grand Admiral, standing apart at her arch, names what is in the valley. The Hierophant answers her with a larger number and a kindness. The Chancellor, who cannot stop looking at a trembling cup of wine, speaks the order anyway. Serath asks for time. Nobody raises a voice, and the Council never turns round to look at her.

## MOVIE COLOR
White stone in cloud, cool and grey: pale grey marble, grey-white cloud, white cloth. The gold of Serath's collar is the only colour. Every shot is exposed for the cloud in the arches: the room sits just under it, each face is lit softly from the side of the arches and falls off on the other side, and a face is never the brightest thing in the frame. Whites keep their detail. No cast shadows.
Influences, each with its purpose — Denis Villeneuve, Dune and Arrival: symmetry, stillness, shallow focus up close, silence before speech, a hard thing heard on the face of the one it is said to. James Cameron: every look answered by what it looks at.
Camera package — ARRI Alexa 65, large format, spherical primes, fine grain. Shallow focus on faces. Every shot moves slowly and smoothly on a steady camera.

## VOICE DNA (immutable)
SERATH: The high speech of Orison: a soft, unplaced Received Pronunciation, older and gentler than a modern one, with no regional mark. A woman of fifty-five with a low alto, warm but closed, a little grain at the bottom of the range. She speaks at a measured pace and always finishes her sentences; she never hurries and never raises her voice. Her breath is silent.
THE HIEROPHANT: The high speech at its purest: a liturgical Received Pronunciation, old-fashioned and round. A man of seventy-four with a rich, resonant bass-baritone, deep and full in the breath, a voice used to filling vast rooms without effort. Every sentence has a liturgical cadence and comes to rest on a falling end, like a pronouncement. He is perfectly steady and has never been in doubt.
THE CHANCELLOR: The high speech of Orison, softly. A woman of sixty-four with a tired mezzo-alto, a little husky, slow and plain. She is honest and says hard things without ornament.
No one else speaks.
