Q01 — THE RITE. 20 s, 7 shots, 21:9, composed for a 2.35:1 matte: keep faces and the bell inside the middle band of the frame. Photoreal live action, clean and sharp, fine detail.
@Image1 the Cathedral of the Ascension: the nave, the slender columns, the galleries, the open tower at the far end · @Image2 the wedding guests, everyone in white · @Image3 the Hierophant (front, back and face) · @Image4 the bride · @Image5 the groom · @Image6 the marriage bell, dark bronze · @Image7 the fleet barge that lifts the bell, the bell slung beneath it in four chains · @Image8 the hammer that strikes the bell from outside, a heavy iron head on a white timber arm.

LOCKS: The cathedral exactly as @Image1: honed white marble; columns no thicker than a man rising about two hundred feet into mist; galleries along both sides; tall open arches onto cloud; at the far end the open tower with one stone beam across it. Ten thousand guests in white, as @Image2, stand on both sides of a clear central aisle, facing the tower, gloved hands folded, still. Beneath the tower, on a low marble step, the Hierophant exactly as @Image3 faces the congregation, in his white robes and tall white headdress. Before him the bride (@Image4, her long veil) and the groom (@Image5) kneel side by side on the marble, facing him, heads bowed, eyes open on the step. The bell exactly as @Image6 hangs in the tower, still held in the four chains of the barge (@Image7), which hangs in the open sky above the tower's open top. The hammer exactly as @Image8 hangs on its white arm from the beam, beside the bell. Long banners of cream and pale grey hang from the galleries and drift sideways, slowly. White petals fall through the air very slowly, about a quarter of normal speed, as if through water. All light is soft daylight from the cloud.

LOOK: White marble in cloud, lit softly and evenly from every side, shadowless; whites keep their detail; faces are never the brightest thing. Cool and muted: white, pale grey and the bell's dark bronze, which is the darkest thing in every frame. Large-format digital (ARRI Alexa 65), spherical lenses, deep focus, clean highlights. The camera floats: every shot moves slowly and smoothly, slightly above head height, as if weightless.

VOICES (verbatim):
THE HIEROPHANT: The high speech at its purest: a liturgical Received Pronunciation, old-fashioned and round. A man of seventy-four with a rich, resonant bass-baritone, deep and full in the breath, a voice used to filling vast rooms without effort. Every sentence has a liturgical cadence and comes to rest on a falling end, like a pronouncement. He is perfectly steady and has never been in doubt.
THE TEN THOUSAND: A vast congregation answering in unison in a cathedral open to the cloud: men, women and children of every age. The response is murmured, not shouted, in an unhurried liturgical cadence. It sounds like habit, not passion, and the nave carries it into a long tail.

SHOTS (every quoted line is spoken exactly once, in this order, and nothing else is said):
1 (0–5.7 s) The nave from the aisle, 24 mm, slightly above head height, centred and symmetrical: a slow, steady drift down the aisle toward the open tower. Columns rising into mist, petals hanging in the air, the guests in white on both sides, the bell small and dark in the far tower. From 2.6 s the Hierophant's voice, off-screen, unhurried, carries through the nave and continues over the cut into shot 2: "Heaven chose us from the earth and raised us above the unworthy." (about 5.5 s)
2 (5.7–8.6 s) The Hierophant, frontal and symmetrical, 50 mm, from the waist up, past the heads of the kneeling bride and groom at the bottom of frame: a very slow push in. He finishes the sentence, his eyes open and looking out over the congregation, and his arms rise slowly and easily, palms up.
3 (8.6–11.4 s) Low, behind the kneeling bride and groom, 28 mm: a slow tilt up from the couple, past the Hierophant's raised arms, to the bell hanging in the tower in the barge's chains. He calls out: "What heaven has raised --" (about 2 s; he stops there, unfinished)
4 (11.4–14.3 s) From the left gallery, high, 28 mm, looking down along the rows of the congregation: a slow drift. Ten thousand voices answer together, murmured: "Let no hand bring down." (about 2.5 s) Banners drift below the gallery rail.
5 (14.3–17.1 s) From the tower floor looking straight up through the open tower, 20 mm, near-still: the barge above the open top, its four chains running down to the bell. The barge lets go: the chains fall slack, slowly, and the bell settles onto the stone beam.
6 (17.1–18.6 s) Level with the beam, side on, 35 mm, locked off: the bell, and beside it the hammer on its white arm swinging slowly back, away from the bell.
7 (18.6–20 s) From the aisle looking up at the tower, 32 mm, locked off: the hammer swings in and strikes the bell's side. The video ends on the strike.

SOUND: the cathedral's long, soft reverberation; the voices as above; the iron of the chains; at the very end one deep bell stroke. No music.
