## The scene, as written

"A naval cutter drops out of the cloud. Above it the underside of the kingdom fills the sky: raw rock, roots, chains thick as towers. Serath has never seen it from below. From here it is not beautiful. It is only enormous, and you can watch it sink. Below: a plain of black basalt to the horizon, in columns, like a city no one finished. At its edge, across the head of a green valley, stands THE WEIR ... Nine gates. Two are shut. Nothing visible pours through the others. But beneath them the grass lies combed flat, and a bird that crosses there drops like a stone. Beyond it, SARN VALE. Terraces, orchards, the smoke of many hearths."

## Why it is staged this way

- **The thing, then the one who sees it, then what she sees.** The cutter under the mass; her face, eyes up and then down; then down: the plain, the wall, the valley. Cameron's rule that a look is answered by what it looks at.
- **Size is shown by something small and known.** The cutter is a fleck beside a chain; its rail sits in the corner of the view down; smoke and sheep give the valley its size.
- **The weight is invisible, so it is shown by what it does.** Flat grass and a bird that falls, the screenplay's own image, as one cause and effect in one frame.
- **The last shot is the valley she has been ordered to drown, and nothing happens in it.**
- **The wall is never called a weir in the prompt.** The word would bring water, and the screenplay says nothing visible pours through.
