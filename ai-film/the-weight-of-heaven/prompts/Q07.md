## The scene, as written

"A naval cutter drops out of the cloud. Above it the underside of the kingdom fills the sky: raw rock, roots, chains thick as towers. Serath has never seen it from below. From here it is not beautiful. It is only enormous, and you can watch it sink. Below: a plain of black basalt to the horizon, in columns, like a city no one finished. At its edge, across the head of a green valley, stands THE WEIR ... Nine gates. Two are shut. Nothing visible pours through the others. But beneath them the grass lies combed flat, and a bird that crosses there drops like a stone. Beyond it, SARN VALE. Terraces, orchards, the smoke of many hearths."

## It is a music montage

- The four montages are cut to an original song (`design/SONG.md`); this one is the song's second verse, "Below": eight bars and a two-bar tag at 84 BPM, 28.6 s, shared with Q08. Q07's share is about four bars, 11.4 s.
- A bar is 2.86 s. The cutter, her face and the bird are generated at 3 s or more, so that each can fill a bar after its ends are trimmed; the plain and the valley are shorter and will be half-bars or less. The reviewer recommended 16 s (about $3.53); the take stays at the 14 s and $3.09 the author was shown.
- **The song does not exist yet.** Its words are still a proposal in `design/SONG.md`, and the plan there is that the song is made first and the montages are cut to it. Movement I was made without it. So these shots are timed to the planned bars and will be recut when there is a song. A question for the author: approve the words and make the song before more montage reels, or carry on and recut later?
- The video model is asked for natural sound only. The verse that goes over these pictures: "We built it wide, we built it low, / we learned to walk the way you lean; / eight hundred years we held the shade / of gardens we have never seen."

## Why it is staged this way

- **The one who sees it, and the thing.** Her face, eyes going up; the cutter under the mass; then what is below: the plain, the wall, the valley. Shot 2 ends on her look up, so in the edit the underside can answer it. Cameron's rule that a look is answered by what it looks at.
- **Size is shown by something small and known.** The cutter is a fleck beside a chain; its rail sits in the corner of the view down; smoke and sheep give the valley its size.
- **The weight is invisible, so it is shown by what it does.** Flat grass and a bird that falls, the screenplay's own image, as one cause and effect in one frame.
- **The last shot is the valley she has been ordered to drown, and nothing happens in it.**
- **The wall is never called a weir in the prompt.** The word would bring water, and the screenplay says nothing visible pours through.
