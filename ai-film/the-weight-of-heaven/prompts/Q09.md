## Why it is staged this way

- **The room is dark until he opens it.** The screenplay: "Dark." Then "Karst shoulders the shutters open. Daylight. The black plain." The whole beat is one change of light, so it is given a cause (he pulls), an effect (the blade of day becomes a door) and an answer (the plain), each in its turn. The hall's reference shows the room after the shutters are open, and the model takes its light from the reference more than from words (lesson 41), so the dark state is made as a picture of the same room.
- **His line is timed to what is revealed.** The voice bible: "Timed to the shutters opening on the plain." He says it to the plain, his back to us, as the leaves come to rest: the stone he is talking about is what we are looking at, and it is sharp. No lips to match.
- **She reads with her back to us, then in a profile that is almost a shadow.** A long archaic sentence on a lit face is where this model rushes or slurs (the Hierophant in Q04). Here nine of its words fall on a wide where her face is not seen, and the rest on a profile edged with light. The sentence is split at its comma across the cut.
- **The daylight finds her before she answers.** Shot 4 is shot 2's frame with the light changed: the same face, the same wall, now seen. Her three words are the only ones in the take said toward the camera.
- **He turns to her only for the last line.** "You were sheltered" is said to the plain; "By then you had gardens" to her, more quietly (the voice bible: when something matters most he gets quieter).
- **Nothing of the screenplay's that would look staged is in it.** Nobody walks in, nobody points, and the seals at the wall's foot are in the wide but are not explained: seven and eleven are for Q13, where she counts them. The papers on the floor are not looked at; they are Q10.
- **No first frame was generated.** Both hall images are the approved reference, changed here by hand; the people come from their sheets (lesson 136).

| Finding | Change |
| --- | --- |
