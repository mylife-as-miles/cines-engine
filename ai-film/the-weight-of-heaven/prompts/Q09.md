## How it joins the scene before

The author, 27 November, on version 1: "How do it connect with previous scene". It did not: the yard ends, in cut v20, on Serath's profile in daylight as Karst's second sentence ends, and version 1 cut from there to a dark wide with her already at the wall. No shared image, which is how the author joins scenes (Q06 opens on Q05's last frame; Q08 was to continue from Q07's).

Version 2 opens on the match: her profile, facing left, her head where the yard's last frame has it (the tip of her nose on the centre line, her chin two thirds of the way down, the collar at the bottom edge), upright, in the dark of the hall, the carved slab where the yard's sky was. A straight cut: the same face, the daylight gone. The yard's frame is not attached to the take, because a "framing only" reference comes back as the whole earlier picture (Q06's seal) and a take wears the light of its first image; the placing is written out, and the edit can register the two heads by a small scale and shift, as it did for Q05 into Q06. Sound: the yard's wind stops on the cut; the hall is still for two thirds of a second before she reads.

## Why it is staged this way

- **The room is dark until he opens it.** The screenplay: "Dark." Then "Karst shoulders the shutters open. Daylight. The black plain." The whole beat is one change of light, so it is given a cause (he pulls), an effect (the blade of day becomes a door) and an answer (the plain), each in its turn. The hall's reference shows the room after the shutters are open, and the model takes its light from the reference more than from words (lesson 41), so the dark state is made as a picture of the same room.
- **His line is timed to what is revealed.** The voice bible: "Timed to the shutters opening on the plain." He says it to the plain, his back to us, as the leaves come to rest: the stone he is talking about is what we are looking at, and it is sharp. No lips to match.
- **She reads on her profile, and the last words fall on the wide.** Eighteen words on a dim, readable face in one shot; then one breath, the cut, and "until the fire is stone" with her back to us, small before the whole slab, Karst already at the shutters: the words that matter are laid over the picture of what they are about to open. One break in the sentence, at a breath. (Version 1 kept every reading word off a lit face; the join costs that.)
- **The daylight finds her before she answers.** Shot 4 is the reverse of shot 1, from the shutters' side: the same face at the same slab, now in daylight, and she turns from the rock to the light. Her three words are the only ones in the take said toward the camera.
- **He turns to her only for the last line.** "You were sheltered" is said to the plain; "By then you had gardens" to her, more quietly (the voice bible: when something matters most he gets quieter).
- **Nothing of the screenplay's that would look staged is in it.** Nobody walks in, nobody points, and the seals at the wall's foot are in the wide but are not explained: seven and eleven are for Q13, where she counts them. The papers on the floor are not looked at; they are Q10.
- **No first frame was generated.** Both hall images are the approved reference, changed here by hand; the people come from their sheets (lesson 136).
