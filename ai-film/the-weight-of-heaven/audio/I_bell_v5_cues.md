Every sound is a cue in `CUES` in the tool: an ID, a stem, a time on the film's clock, a source, a level, an EQ, a position, a hall send and a reason. A cue names its foundry file first; if the file is missing the tool warns and uses its stand-in (a take excerpt or numpy synthesis), or skips the cue. Shot starts come from `edit/I_bell_v5.edl.json`. The five biggest levels (the two strokes, the two lines, the answer, the landing) are measured with their hall and trimmed by the tool on every build, so the hierarchy holds whatever file is swapped.

## The bell: what it is made of, and why

The take's own stroke does not read as a bell: earshot hears "a cinematic hit with a long tail" and, in a forced choice, a gong (75-86 %). Its ring is a dense cluster at 165-350 Hz and its transient is noisy. Three things were tried and measured before the present build:

- The take's partials re-made as long sines, with sub layers: gong, 93 %.
- A bell partial series made in numpy (hum, prime, minor third, fifth, nominal, twelfth): better as a description (z +3.6), still a gong in the forced choice (67-76 %).
- A cast church bell from earshot's catalogue (`note/church_bell`, computed locally, no model, no network) played slower so its prime sits on the take's main partial (191.6 Hz): a bell, 85-91 %. The same bell an octave lower (prime 96 Hz, the "house-sized" pitch) is a gong again, 93 %: to this ear-model a very low struck bell IS a gong.

So the ring is that cast bell at prime 191.6 Hz and hum 95.8 Hz; the foundry's `bell_body` (47.9 and 95.9 Hz) sits under it at -8 dB; a clang of the bell's own upper partials an octave up marks the contact; the take's strike is kept 15 dB down (louder, both witnesses hear a hit or a braam) and that strike one and two octaves down adds mass on the hit only. The bell is built once and struck twice: the opening stroke is the same sound 6 dB lower with a low shelf (-4 dB at 140 Hz).

`--bell-octave-down` tunes the ring to prime 96 Hz and hum 48 Hz. It is the bigger bell; earshot calls it a gong. That choice is the director's.

There is no foundry file for the ring (the tool looks for `audio/sfx/bell_ring.flac` first), so the bell's ring is marked as a stand-in in the table above. If earshot is not installed the tool falls back to the numpy series and says so.

## What only the director's ear can judge

1. **The opening stroke.** earshot hears a bell (98 %); the witness, watching a black screen, hears a drone, a gong or a braam more often than a bell. If it does not say "bell" at once: raise `BELL['clang_db']`, lower the hall on BELL-01 (`send`), or shorten its tail.
2. **The bell's size.** Its strike note is 191.6 Hz with a hum at 96 Hz and a sub at 48 Hz. Is that a bell the size of a house, or a church bell with weight under it? `--bell-octave-down` is the other bell. Also its warble, and whether the ring (a modelled cast bell) sounds like bronze.
3. **The choir.** Choir 1 (mixed, sustained; the default) against choir 2 (low men; `I_bell_v5_mixcheck_choir2.mp4`). Is it heard under the lines (it sits 12.7-14.0 dB under speech at 1-4 kHz, at the brief's limit, and the witness is split)? Does choir 1 read as a choir or as one soprano over a pad? Does choir 2 low like cattle (the foundry's own warning)? Choir 1 is tuned to A = 440; the bell's G is 38 cents flat of that. They never sound together, but the choir's last chord is a G.
4. **The Hierophant in the hall**: wet 13 dB under dry. Enough hall, and are the words still clear on a phone? The answer is spread wide with more hall (8 dB under): big enough, too washed?
5. **The seal.** Is the press heard over the choir, and does it sound like wax taking a ring or like a click? The lift is a tick (the foundry's file is tagged as a camera shutter; it is kept at -16 dBFS peak under the take's own sound).
6. **The wind.** It fails every earshot check (hiss, surf). It is kept low as air; the near-silence before the landing is that air alone at -43.5 dBFS. Does it read as height, or as noise?
7. **The landing.** Deep enough, and clearly not a bell stroke? It is 7.9 LU under the stroke and has 3.5 dB less low end.
8. **The applause.** The foundry could not make it read as gloved; it is full band, very low and in the hall, gone by 34.0 s. Soft and large, or just quiet clapping? Leaving it out is one line (`FX-12`).
9. **The ending.** The bell's tail is ridden up 4 dB so it survives to the black; the last second is a fade. On a phone the last two seconds are faint (-36 to -37 dBFS above 300 Hz): the note that is left is low.
10. **Q01's own music** is still under line 1 (it cannot be taken out of the take); between the lines it is held 9 dB down. Does it sit with the bell's tail?
11. **The crowd bed**: one stretch of it reads as whispering to earshot. Presence, or voices?
12. **Small Foley**: paper, sleeves (kept 36 dB down after the witness heard a louder version as a blade), the timber strain before the release (is a creak right for chains?), the grit.
13. **Width and low end on real speakers**: L/R correlation 0.37; 48 Hz and 96 Hz carry the bell's weight and a phone plays none of it (there the stroke is still 5.6 LU over the dialogue).
