# Movement I, cut v4: five independent reviews (25 November 2026)

The author asked for a panel of reviewers to criticise cut v4 (`out/edits/I_bell_v4.mp4`, 34.75 s) against Denis Villeneuve's cinematography and James Cameron's visual storytelling, and to score cinematography, composition, editing, sound effects, the overall epic-fantasy look and its strength as an opening. Five agents reviewed it separately; none saw the editor's own notes or each other's reports. Each pulled frames (about 90 each; the editor all 834), measured, and used Gemini as a fallible witness for motion and sound. Nobody could hear the cut: sound is judged from measurement, earshot and that witness. The review file is a 992×432 proxy, so fine texture is not judged.

References used: Villeneuve's frames on StillsLab (Dune: Part Two, Dune: Part One, Blade Runner 2049, Arrival) and its essay on his style; Cameron's Titanic frames on StillsLab (the only Cameron film it carries); the handbook's `editing.md`, `score-and-sound-design.md` and `color.md`, which adapt the reference repositories (vidmoat/skills, calesthio/generative-media-skills, Benitoow/cinema-skills, badgids/Story-Film-Skills and others, credited there).

Overall: about 5 out of 10. The festival-programmer reviewer would not programme the film on this cut.

## What every reviewer praised

- The opening nave: one-point symmetry, real scale, the bell as the only dark mass in a white frame.
- Serath: her eyes never drop to the page in any frame (checked across eight frames). The best human image in the cut.
- The ring and wax: red as the only saturated colour; the contour lines on the warrant, a plant for the valley.
- The hush before the stroke (about −42 dBFS at 30.1 s) and a stroke in sync to the frame (30.82 s).
- The wine is dead still while the gloved hands behind it move.
- Technically clean: no flash frames, no clicks, gaps or clipping at any join.

## The problems, by agreement

1. **The film's premise is not on screen** (all four picture reviewers; the first item on each list). The bell already hangs in frame one, so nothing arrives. In the wide "release" the bell is 42 pixels wide of 992 and sinks a few pixels; chains then fall from beneath it, which is backwards; no barge is seen. Shown the cut with no context, the witness understood "a wedding in the sky, an official seals a paper, a bell is rung", took the warrant for a marriage certificate, and found no weight anywhere. It called the genre dystopian science fiction, not epic fantasy.
2. **The bell changes in every shot**: a bare beam in the far tower (shot 1), an ordinary bell on a timber beam with a dish above it (12–13.8 s), chains and a hammer on the right (from below), a bridge-tower rig (the wide), a plain beam with the hammer on the left (the stroke). The aisle group is three figures in shot 1 and four in the wide.
3. **Nobody is seen speaking.** Line 1 plays over the wide; line 2 over the back of the Hierophant's hat; his one face shot is 2.5 s of near-silence with his eyes shut for the first 1.3 s; the answer plays over a gallery where two or three mouths move.
4. **Rhythm and climax.** Five shots in a row run about three seconds; the cut slows into its climax (the stillest, second-longest shot sits just before the stroke); the hammer stays stuck to the bell for 1.4 s; the cup gets 2.5 s. One framing (the nave wide, twice) takes 31% of the runtime.
5. **Light, grade and finish.** White on white with no shadow side: figures and walls share one value. The ten shots do not share one grade (black points from 2 to 51 of 255; the raised-warrant shot is brighter, cooler and softer than its neighbours). No grain. Blank banners and plain marble read as a clean render; the three accents are dull (the seal brick-brown, the wine black).
6. **Sound** (the specialist, by measurement):
   - The bell is not the biggest thing: it ties a line of dialogue for loudness, has less low end than the landing 4.7 s earlier, rings at 192 Hz (a tower bell, not a house), and is faded out 2.8 s after it is struck.
   - The choir is one 3.87 s phrase, sample-identical, four times; it masks the seal and sits level with the crowd's answer.
   - There is no world: the track is mono, the Hierophant's voice is dry, 99% of the energy is below 2.6 kHz.
   - The seal press, the story's cause, is inaudible and seven frames late.
   - Two thumps have no cause on screen (16.3 s and 28.2–28.5 s).
   - The film fades out in the middle of the applause.

## Faults that are the edit's own (mine), found by the reviewers

- The seal's sound is 125 ms late because the sound runs unbroken across a three-frame picture trim.
- The two causeless thumps are the tails of my crossfades (Q01's tail at 16.3 s; the release take's tail at 28.2–28.5 s).
- The applause track carries a second copy of the bell's tail.
- The air bed sits at −49 dBFS RMS; the cut note's "−38 dBFS floor" was its peak.
- The cut listed at 16.29 s falls at 16.333 s; the last three frames are frozen.

## Fixes that cost nothing (editing, grade, sound), ranked

1. **Build the opening the script asks for**: three to four seconds of black, one bell stroke with a long decay, the super "THE KINGDOM OF ORISON. YEAR 800 OF THE ASCENSION.", then the nave on the decay.
2. **Make the stroke the biggest event and let it ring**: dialogue down (line 1 by 4 dB, line 2 by 7.5 dB), the landing down 4 dB, the stroke unchanged; no fade on the bell; a low layer under it (the stroke itself an octave and two octaves down, quietly); hold the cup five to six seconds so the ring can die; never end on applause.
3. **Re-cut for rhythm** (the editor's table, about 27 s): enter the Hierophant as his eyes open (8.0 s); the gallery only as long as the answer (about 1.4 s), its last words landing on Serath; leave Serath while her hand is still moving; trim the seal; enter the raised warrant later; leave the hammer 6 to 16 frames after contact.
4. **One grade, then a look, then grain** (the colour reference's order): match every shot to the best one (blacks, white balance; pull the raised-warrant shot down and warm it); desaturate everything but the accents; lift the gold, the seal red and the wine; grain once; matte to 2.39:1. Three or four looks shown to the author on the same frames before anything is rendered.
5. **Build the sound's world**: stereo; cathedral reverb on Q01's voice; a crowd-presence bed; wind with real high end in place of the noise bed; the seal's Foley close and on time; chains, rope creak and a swing whoosh from a free library or Stable Audio on CPU; the two causeless thumps removed.
6. **Make the weight felt**: a three-to-six-frame camera shake on the landing and on the stroke, then the locked-off cup.
7. **The choir**: decided by the author (see the disagreement above).
8. **Small things**: a slow push on the opening wide; the frozen last frames dropped; the seal sound's offset corrected.

Two things the free fixes cannot do: put the bell's arrival on screen, and give Serath a shot of her own.
