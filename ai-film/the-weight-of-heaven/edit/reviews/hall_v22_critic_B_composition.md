# The hall, cut v22: composition and staging (critic B)

File judged: `out/review/film_v22_hall.mp4` (1151 frames, 24 fps, active picture 992×416 = 2.39:1). Every frame was extracted and looked at; cuts were found by frame difference. All positions below are percentages of the active picture: x from the left edge, y from the top edge. Lens lengths are how the frame reads, not data. Reference frames are named by folder and file under `scratchpad/stills/` (d1 = Dune: Part One, d2 = Dune: Part Two, br = Blade Runner 2049, ar = Arrival). Sound and missing lines were not judged.

## Verdict in four lines

- Three pictures in the hall are in the idiom and could sit in one of those films: the dark profile at the slab (14.33), Karst's back against the plain (32.50, until his eyes reach the lens), and the lamp on the floor with the hall behind it (36.04, foreground only).
- The sequence as staged does not fit. Its payoff shot (29.21) is a woman turning to face the camera in flat front light and speaking towards it; its last shot (44.96) is a studio portrait on black with a stare into the lens; its only wide (20.71) is an off-axis coverage angle that lasts 2.9 s; and no frame anywhere gives the treaty wall or the hall any size.
- The cut from the yard into the hall (14.33) is not a match. The eye lands 18.5% of the frame width and 13% of the height away from where it was, the face is 1.19 times larger, and the head is pitched about 20° differently.
- The yard still contains every fault the author named: a rank of men in a line (5.29), a frontal man centred in a black doorway, used twice (7.00, 11.71), two people who stop and turn to the camera (3.5–5.3), and the aide going down on his knees (6.4–7.0).

## 2. The hall, shot by shot

### H1, 14.33: profile at the slab

A long-lens profile at eye height with the wall raking away to the left. She is put at the right edge so that 68% of the frame is what she is reading, and the eye (76, 30) and the fingertip (19→35, 75) make one diagonal. The staging is found: she is absorbed and gives nothing to the camera. The frame is locked for 6.4 s, which is right.

Three flaws. The middle of the frame, x 35–65, holds nothing but soft grey stone, and it is the brightest part (centre of the brightest tenth at (39, 24)); face and finger are pushed to opposite edges. The fingertip sits at 72–78% of the height with the hand cut by the bottom edge. The slab's vertical end at x ≈ 65–66 stands 3% in front of her nose tip (68.5), a tangent.

Could it pass: yes, as a still. Closest: md7/d1_0094 for the profile and th8/d1_0066 for the touch. What d1_0066 does that this does not: the touch is at (58, 50), the centre, and the arm leads the eye to it.

### H2, 20.71: the hall wide

The only frame that shows where anything is, and the shortest shot in the hall at 2.92 s. Two points of light, her coat at x 37 and the slit at x 74.5, on a black field: that idea is sound, and both people have their backs to us, which is found, not posed.

It fails as a Villeneuve wide on four counts.

1. The camera stands near the right wall and looks down the room with the vanishing point at x 75. The left wall takes 58% of the width at a rake. Mirror correlation is +0.02. Every hall wide in the reference set is square on the room's axis (+0.63 to +0.93). This is the angle from which a camera can see everything, which is what coverage looks like.
2. The room is small. The timber ceiling is in frame, the slab is about 1.8 times her height, and she can reach its middle. In md8/d1_0080 the architecture runs out of every edge of the frame and the opening alone is taller than the man.
3. The left edge is a plank side door (x 0–14) and the bottom-left corner is the palest patch of floor papers (x 3–25, y 80–97). Both pull the eye to the frame's edge.
4. The slab, which is the subject of the scene, is a foreshortened dark trapezoid that cannot be read. No shot in the sequence shows it square.

Layering is weak: floor, two figures in the middle distance, back wall; nothing in the foreground. The size ratio of the two figures is 1.8 to 1, the inexpressive middle: neither one huge and one tiny (md/br_0043) nor two equal on an axis (md/d1_0072).

Could it pass: no. What gives it away: the off-axis angle, the beams and the barn door, darkness that is only under-exposure and not shaped. Closest: md8/d1_0080.

### H3, 23.62: Karst opens the shutters

An axial cut-in from the wide with a match on his raised hands; that cut is correct. The frame is symmetrical (mirror +0.95) and level, and a silhouette in a bright slot between black stone is a Villeneuve shape.

The staging ruins the reveal. He stands dead centre, fills about 86% of the frame height, and covers the middle of the opening he has just made. The black basalt plain, the thing revealed, gets two slivers 8% wide each, about 8% of the frame. From 26.04 he stands with his arms at his sides while nothing happens for 3.2 s: a man on a mark. The door leaves face the camera and are lit pale (x 30–40 and 60–70 at 26.38), so the frame is five bands, stone, pale planks, sky, pale planks, stone, and not a blade of light in black.

Could it pass: half. What gives it away: the figure is as large as the opening, and the pale plank doors. Closest: th7/ar_0066, where the opening is larger than the people in it, and md/d2_0126, where the figure stands beside the daylight.

### H4, 29.21: Serath turns into the daylight

The best half-second of the shot is its first: her profile against the slab, eyes closed as the light arrives (29.21–29.6). Then she turns 90° towards the camera, squares her shoulders to it by 30.46, and speaks (31.67). The light is flat on both cheeks with no shadow side, the wall behind her is evenly lit, and the brightest thing in the frame is her coat under her chin. Head at the left third with 60% of the frame given to the letters is a fair layout. The frame is posed: a person turning out to the lens to deliver a line.

Screen direction: the slab was screen-left in H1 and H2 and is screen-right here. The camera has gone to the other side of her. That is legal after the on-axis H3, and her look (screen-right) pairs with his in H5 (screen-left) and with the wide (she left, he right). The cost is that H4 opens as a mirror image of H1.

Could it pass: no. What gives it away: front light, the turn to camera, speech towards camera. Closest: md/d2_0127 and md/br_0041. What they do: the light comes from behind or one side, and the woman at the wall is seen from behind her shoulder looking along the wall.

### H5, 32.50: Karst at the opening

The frame of the sequence. Back and shoulder on the right third, the door leaf closing the right edge, a level horizon at 59.7, and 57% of the frame empty sky and plain for him to face. Locked. The slow turn of the head is found, not performed. This is the one frame with real scale.

One flaw: in the last 0.6 s (from about 35.5) his eyes arrive on the lens. In md8/br_0076 the look passes the lens.

Could it pass: yes, from 32.50 to about 35.3. Closest: md7/ar_0048.

### H6, 36.04: the lamp

The only shot with three planes: lamp and hand sharp in front, stones and papers in the middle, the opening and a figure beyond. Floor-level camera, one warm source, a slow creep. That is the method of md/d1_0014.

Three flaws. The far figure's head is outside the top of the frame for the whole shot (her collar is on the frame line), so the frame reads as an accident. She stands against the jamb at x 46–53, to the right of the opening (x 37–45), a pale blur on dark wall, when the opening would make her a clean black shape. And the viewer has just seen a man in a pale coat standing at that opening; a soft, headless, pale-coated figure in the same place reads first as Karst, and the hand as a stranger's. Karst and Serath have exchanged places with no picture of either moving. Also, a sheet and a seal appear in the foreground between 36.04 and 36.9.

Could it pass: the foreground yes. What gives it away: the cut-off figure. Closest: md/d1_0014, where the people beyond are whole, sharp and placed directly over the object.

### H7, 40.92: the shard

A real insert: one object, centred at (48, 47), a 16% push, warm side light. It is a little busy: four red seals, and the largest slides under the bottom edge from 42.0 and is half cut by 42.9. The brightest area is the paper below the shard, not the shard.

Could it pass: yes as an insert, with the bottom edge cleaned. Closest: md/d2_0129, which is top-down, square to the frame, on a plain black field.

### H8, 44.96: Serath's face

Eyes down, then lifting: after the shard this reads correctly as her looking at it. But she is on the centre line against a field with no tone (78% of pixels under 20) and no place: H6 put her at the open shutters with her back to us, and this is not there. Her eyes sit at 23% of the height with the forehead cut, and the bottom quarter of the frame is collar and coat, which is brighter than her face. From 46.4 she looks into the lens for 1.5 s. It is a portrait sitting.

Could it pass: no. What gives it away: the black backdrop and the stare. Closest: md/d2_0124, where the face fills the frame to the chin at 95%, sits off the centre line, is lit from one side and looks off the lens; and th/d1_0048, where the dark behind the face has tone.

## 3. The sequence as staging

**Sizes.** Profile close (6.4 s), wide (2.9), full figure (5.6), close (3.3), close from behind (3.5), then after the jump in time: object with depth (4.9), insert (4.0), face (3.0). The first five have intent: detail, room, act, reaction, answer. But the proportions are upside down: the wide is the shortest shot and the man standing in a doorway the second longest. The last three are all tight; nothing re-establishes the room after the jump, and the two people never share a readable frame again.

**Geography.** From H2: slab on the left wall, shutters in the back wall, papers on the floor. H1, H2 and H3 agree. The lamp is not in the wide. Who is where at 36.04 cannot be read.

**Camera height.** Five of eight shots are at standing chest or eye height. Only H6 is low. None is low and far, which is what makes architecture loom in the references.

**Camera movement.** Locked or a push of 16% or less in every hall shot; horizons measured level (0.0°) in H3 and H5. This discipline is correct and must stay.

**Eyelines and screen direction.**

| Cut | Result |
|---|---|
| Yard: Serath looks screen-left in 5.29, 7.83, 12.54; the two watchers look screen-right | Agree |
| Yard: Karst in the doorway looks at the lens | No direction; he connects to nobody |
| 14.33, yard into hall | Same direction (left), not registered |
| 20.71 and 23.62 | Agree; 23.62 is an axial cut-in with a match on action |
| 29.21 | Slab jumps from screen-left to screen-right |
| 29.21 to 32.50 | Her look right, his look left: agree |
| 36.04 | Positions exchanged, no bridge |
| 44.96 | Eyes down to the object: works. Then the lens |

**Repeats.** 7.00 and 11.71 are the same framing of a man who does not move (frame-to-frame change 0.04 of 255: a still photograph). H4 opens as H1 mirrored. Three of the eight hall shots (H4, H5, H8) end on a face turned to the camera.

**The cut at 14.33, profile to profile.** Not a match.

| | Yard, last frame (14.29) | Hall, first frame (14.33) |

| Eye | (57.5, 17) | (76, 30) |

Same subject, same direction, nearly the same size, displaced a fifth of the frame: that reads as a jump, not a match. The yard shot's first frame (12.54) has the head level and matches the hall's attitude almost exactly (nose to eye offset (−8, +16) against (−7.5, +17)); the shot then tilts away from the match before the cut. Registering it would need the yard shot played backwards, punched in 1.32 times, and H1 punched in 1.25 times, which puts the fingertip on H1's left frame edge. Not worth it. The free fix is in section 5, item 6.

## 4. The yard, short verdict

- **0.50, the boat wide.** Dark backs at both edges and white in the middle is the right idea (md8/d1_0062). It runs 0.71 s, which is a flash, and its last three frames are her lurch off the ramp. At this size (she is 36% of the height) a boot reaching the ground cannot be seen.
- **1.21–5.29, the spear and the bowl.** One set-up, with a splice inside it at 3.54. Two people in flat profile either side of a spear at x 39.5, at eye height, in even light and deep focus, with extras wandering behind who notice nothing. From 3.5 both turn their heads to the camera. This is the lowest frame in the film: television coverage, posed. The reference for watchers is md8/d2_0217: long lens, faces overlapping and filling the frame, eyes going past the camera.
- **5.29, Serath between two dark backs.** The layout works (mirror +0.58, white on the centre line between two black masses). Behind her a rank of men in dark coats stands shoulder to shoulder facing us (x 63–80, more at x 8–15): people in a row. They are not in the wide at 0.50, and the wall behind has changed from a flat curtain wall to a round tower. From 6.4 the aide goes down on his knees and by 6.96 his head is on the ground. The author asked for the fall to be removed; it is still here. md/d1_0013 shows what Dune does with ranks: they recede on diagonals into depth under something huge; they are not a line parallel to the picture at the back.
- **7.00 and 11.71, Karst in the doorway.** Frontal, on the centre line (subject x 34–66, centre 49.9), mirror correlation +0.96, black behind, motionless, eyes at the lens, twice. This is the man at the back of that place. The frame it was modelled on, md8/d1_0082, puts the still figure at x 25–38, turned three-quarters, with dark architecture across the other 60%.
- **7.83, Serath close** and **12.54, Serath in profile from below.** Both at the standard: long lens, soft ground, eyes off the lens, the head given room on the side she looks to. Keep both.

## 5. Faults ranked, with fixes

**1. 29.21–32.50 (H4). The scene's payoff is its most posed frame.** The daylight is the event of the scene, and it is spent on a woman turning out to the camera in flat front light and speaking towards it. This is what the author called staged.
- FREE: cut out at 30.25 (frame 726), in three-quarter view while the head is still turning, before her shoulders square. Lay her line over H5: Karst's back, and his head turning to her voice. The shot becomes 1.04 s: light on the profile, eyes open, the turn begins, cut.
- NEW FOOTAGE: from behind her right shoulder on the hall side (the same side as H1 and H2), 75–85 mm, eye height, locked. The back of her head and the collar soft and dark at x 60–75. The slab square to the lens and sharp across the rest. The daylight comes in from screen-right and rakes across the carved letters towards her. She turns only her head, to screen-right, into profile, eye near (58, 38), the cheek towards us in shadow. She never faces the lens.

**2. 20.71–23.62 (H2), and the whole hall: nothing has size.** The only wide is off-axis, lasts 2.9 s, and shows a room with a timber ceiling and a wall 1.8 times her height. The treaty wall is never shown square. Scale is the first thing a Villeneuve frame has.
- FREE: crop to x 14–100, y 0–86 (scale 1.163). Serath lands at x 27, the slit at x 70, balanced about the centre; the side door and the pale corner papers leave the frame. Lengthen to at least 4.4 s: the shot is locked and almost nothing moves, so slow it to 66%, or use more of the take if it exists.
- NEW FOOTAGE: camera square to the treaty wall, 24–28 mm, 1.0 m high, locked, 5–6 s. The wall fills the frame from edge to edge and runs out of the top: no ceiling, no beams, no door. Serath with her back to us at x 36–38, head at y 62, feet at y 84 (22% tall), right hand on the stone. The shutters are out of frame to the right; one blade of daylight from their slit lies on the floor and climbs the wall at about x 70. Karst is absent, or a dark shape at x 90–96. When the shutters open off-screen, the light crosses the wall from right to left and reaches her in the same locked frame. Models: md8/d1_0080 for the axis, th/d2_0147 for the size of the figure.

**3. 44.96–47.96 (H8). A portrait on black, with a stare into the lens.**
- FREE: punch in 1.33 times from the top edge (keep x 12.5–87.5, y 0–75). Eyes move to 31% of the height, the chin to 96%, and the collar and coat leave the frame, so the face is the only light thing. Cut out at 46.6, about five frames after the eyes arrive, not 36.
- NEW FOOTAGE: 85 mm, her face at x 55–75, eyes at 35% of the height. Eyes down towards screen-left and low (the lamp), then lifting to screen-left (Karst), not to the lens. Lamp light from low on the left; the open shutters a soft cold shape behind her at x 10–30, so the frame has a place and two sources.

**4. 23.62–29.21 (H3). The man blocks what he reveals, then stands on his mark for 3.2 s.**
- FREE: cut out at 27.0. Bring the two door leaves down about two stops with a matte (x 30–40 and 60–70 at 26.4, narrowing to 30–36.5 and 63.5–70) so the frame is black, light, black.
- NEW FOOTAGE: from deep in the hall on the axis, 35 mm, 1.0 m, locked. The shutters take x 43–57. Karst is 30–34% of the frame height, at the left leaf; he pulls it and steps aside to the left so the opening is clear. The horizon at 47 is visible across the whole opening and the light runs down the floor towards the camera.

**5. 36.04 (H5 into H6). Two people exchange places without a picture of it, and the far figure has no head.**
- FREE: start H6 at 37.7, after the foreground sheet and seal have finished appearing. This does not cure the confusion.
- NEW FOOTAGE: the same floor-level frame, lamp at x 70–84, hand on the roll, tilted up about 4° so Serath is whole: head no higher than y 12, feet at y 45. She stands inside the bright opening as a black shape, centred at x 41. Focus on the hand, then pulled to her over the last 1.5 s. Nothing may appear or vanish on the floor.

**6. 14.33. A false match from the yard into the hall.**
- FREE: re-order the end of the yard so a different picture stands between the two profiles: Serath close (7.83), Serath in profile (12.54), then Karst in the doorway once, then the hall. The black doorway then leads into the black hall, and the near-match is gone.

**7. The yard: the fall, the rank, the frontal man, the turn to camera.**

Give the boat wide at least 2.5 s from earlier in its take and end it at 1.04, before the lurch; if the take has no more, drop it and open on 5.29.

**8. 40.92–44.96 (H7). The cut seal on the bottom edge.**
- FREE: a soft matte darkening the bottom 20% of the frame, so the seal stops pulling the eye off the shard.

**9. 14.33–20.71 (H1). The empty bright centre and the collar in the corner.** Small.

## 6. At the standard: keep

- **H5, 32.50–35.33.** Back and shoulder on the right third, a level horizon at 59.7, 57% of the frame empty, locked.
- **H1, 14.33–20.71.** The eye-to-fingertip diagonal, the long lens, the 6 s locked hold.
- **H6's set-up.** Floor-level camera, a single warm source, three planes. The idea is right; the far figure is what is wrong.
- **H7** as a slow push on one object.
- **The cut at 23.62.** Wide to the man's back on one axis, matched on his hands.
- **The pairing of looks across 29.21 and 32.50,** which also agrees with the wide.
- **The camera's behaviour in the hall.** Locked or barely moving, level, no handheld, no orbit.
- **The yard close-ups at 7.83 and 12.54.**
