**Across both (fact).**
- Hesitation (4): 9 beat parentheticals in Part One, 3 in Part Two; otherwise one named act in an action line before the cue: "She swallows hard and chooses truth."; "Paul looks down in silence."
- An order can cut off a broken line: "If the Duke's son lives --" / "No Atreides will live." The objection follows with no action line between (Part One sc. 42AD, p. 44).

## Recommendations (inference, each from the facts above)

1. **Put to the author: let the name fall on Serath.** These scripts give the hard word's moment to the listener (C, sc. 77). Shot 5 would end on the stop and his look toward her; shot 6: her back, a quiet off-screen "Sarn Vale.", then the turn. Gains: shot 5 fits (as planned, its first fourteen words take 4.7 s at three a second, leaving under a second for the pause and the name); the turn's cause is inside the shot; no mouth is visible on the two hardest words, so they can be replaced in sound without lip-sync. Costs: it reverses the choice of the name on his face, and puts two words in the shot most often squeezed.
2. **If the name stays on his face, thin shot 5 to two acts.** Wording: "He stops. His eyes go to Serath, then down to the floor. To the floor, quietly:". Drop the separate swallow and "rest there": the look sets up the cut to her; the swallow does not.
3. **Shot 3: make the reaction body-sized.** In B a room answers a number with posture; eyes cannot be read at 28 mm. Wording: "After the number the Chancellor's head bows back toward the cup. No one else moves."
4. **In the edit, not the prompt: leave shot 4 on its last word.** A and D cut on the order. The silence then plays on the Engineer in shot 5.
