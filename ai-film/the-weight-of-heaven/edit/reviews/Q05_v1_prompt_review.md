## (a) Verdict

Fix first: about fifteen minutes of wording, all free, no re-staging. The geography, the eyelines, the six lines and the timing are right; what is wrong is a handful of words that name things no frame should show (a turn, a back, a cup) and a first shot that is given a job without a time.

## (b) Fixes, most damaging first

### 1. Shot 3 names the turn it does not want, and holds his head with a "does not move" (lesson 73; Oren 37; Q04's own note that such phrases freeze a frame)

A frontal single cannot show a man not turning; it can only show him turning. "over his shoulder" is an action.

- [ACT] `He does not turn round. His eyes are open and stay on the engineer in front of him, just left of the lens. His face is calm. He lets a moment pass, then answers her over his shoulder without moving his head, slowly, each word set down. After it his mouth closes.`
  → `He faces us, his head level and at rest. His eyes are open and stay on the engineer, off-screen, just left of the lens. His face is calm. He lets a moment pass, breathes in once, then speaks, slowly, each word set down, his eyes where they are. After it his mouth closes and only the air is heard to the cut.`
- [DIALOGUE] `(after a still moment; 6.4 to 8.3 s)` → `(after a still moment and his one breath in; 6.4 to 8.3 s)`
- PERFORMANCE ARC `untroubled; he does not turn round to her; he is kind.` → `untroubled; he faces forward from first to last; he is kind.`
- SCENE INTENT `Nobody raises a voice, and the Council never turns round to look at her.` → `Every voice is quiet. The Hierophant answers facing forward; only the Chancellor turns her head.`

"only the air is heard to the cut" also covers the second of quiet after his four words (he spoke five words in 0.95 s in Q04), so the model does not run on into the next sentence on his face.

### 2. A second body is named in every single (lesson 45)

"to the Hierophant's back", "to his back", "on his back", "the Council's backs", "a man's back" ask for a back in Serath's close shot (an over-the-shoulder, drawn three times and never the same). "Serath is behind him, over his right shoulder", twice in the locks, asks for a soft Serath behind each of them. Q04 wrote such looks as "off-screen to the left", and it worked.

- Location lock: delete `; Serath is behind him, over his right shoulder` and `; Serath is behind her, over her right shoulder`.
- Shot 2 `go past the right side of the camera, to the Hierophant's back.` → `go past the right side of the camera, to the Hierophant, off-screen across the room.` and `says it evenly, as a fact, to his back.` → `says it evenly, as a fact.`
- Shot 4 `her eyes stay on his back;` → `her eyes stay past the right side of the camera;`
- Shot 6 `past the right side of the camera, on the Council's backs.` → `past the right side of the camera, on the two at the table, off-screen.`
- PERFORMANCE ARC `states a fact to a man's back` → `states a fact across the room`
- THEMATIC `The Council decides with its back to the one who must carry the decision out. The only person who looks at Serath is the one who orders her.` → `The Council decides without looking at the one who must carry the decision out. The only person who looks toward Serath is the one who orders her.`

### 3. The cup is in no frame and in eight places; the intent names a flood (lessons 52, 73, 78; Oren 32)

"says the first sentence to the cup" can be played as a woman speaking into a cup. The plan itself says "The cup is not in any frame of Q05".

- Shot 5 [ACT] `look down at the cup below the frame. She breathes in and says the first sentence to the cup, plainly;` → `look down, below the bottom edge of the frame. She breathes in and says the first sentence with her eyes down, plainly;`
- Shot 5 [ACT] `Then her eyes come up from the cup, her head turns a little toward the left of the frame, so that she faces us,` → `Then her eyes come up, her head turns toward the left of the frame until she faces us,` (from three-quarters to frontal is about 45 degrees, not "a little")

### 2. Shot 1

The direction is right. The time is the bare minimum: Q04's quarter-turn of the head took 1.2 s, a start-frame shot holds before it moves (lesson 11), and "never snapping" is in the physics locks. Expect either the cut to catch her mid-turn (harmless: the cut to shot 2 is then a cut on movement) or the shot to run to about 2.5 s, which comes out of what follows. Q04's own start-frame shot ran to time (2.62 for 2.6). Two risks in the words: "facing the room" does not say where the room is in this frame (it is behind the camera), and her eyes are not named at the end, so she may finish looking into the lens. Fix 4 covers all three. The frame itself is clean; the dark mark at her hand is her signet, worn over the glove as designed (on her left hand here; Q02's notes put it on her right, which is the take's doing and not this prompt's).

### 3. Shot 4

The wording is as strong as the pattern that worked in Q04, with three guards (the [ACT] line, the speaker tag, the audio lock). The case is harder than Q04's: a frontal face in close-up that has just spoken and will speak again, not a back. Nothing in the wording pushes the line into her mouth; "his" and "behind the camera" are the only loose words (he is to the right, where she is looking). A cut back to him is unlikely: Q04 kept six shots in order. Fix 5 puts the closed mouth first and names the voice as a man's from the right.

### 7. Literal words

Fixes 1, 2, 3 and 5. Also read and left alone: "a larger number and a kindness", "each word set down", "a bargain stated and not pleaded", "like a pronouncement", "to the floor of the room". "every look answered by what it looks at" is not what the cutting does (her look is answered by his face, not his back); harmless.

### 8. Staging

True to the screenplay: "(to Serath)" stands only on "Break it.", and only that is played to her. I would not re-stage. The Hierophant's second sentence on her face is the right choice: it is the hard word given to the listener, it keeps seven words off a mouth that rushed last time, and it leaves his voice replaceable. What is lost is small: his face being kind.

What will not read from Q05 alone is "the Council decides with its back to her". No frame shows a back; the audience has only Q04's wide, a quarter of a minute earlier. What will read is the difference between the two: he stays still, she turns. That is enough for this take. If the author wants the backs seen, Q06 is the place (a wide from Serath's arch into the room shows both of them from behind, with the Engineer facing them), not more words here.
