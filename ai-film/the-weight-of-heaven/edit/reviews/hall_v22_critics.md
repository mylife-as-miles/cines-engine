# The hall as it stands in cut v22: three critics

The author, 28 November, on `out/review/film_v22_hall.mp4`: "Now how reviewers look in to this film v22 hall... Do this fit a Denis or dune film... The cinematography, composition etc". Three independent critics, each with every frame extracted and about 240 frames of Dune: Part One, Dune: Part Two, Blade Runner 2049 and Arrival to measure against; none saw another's report. Nothing was changed in the film.

- A, cinematography: `hall_v22_critic_A_cinematography.md`
- B, composition and staging: `hall_v22_critic_B_composition.md`
- C, direction and cut: `hall_v22_critic_C_direction.md`

## Verdict

All three: **no, as cut it does not pass for a Villeneuve film.** C: it is below the council scene.

## What all three say

1. **Serath's turn (29.21) is the worst frame and it is the one the scene turns on.** Lit like a portrait sitting (2.35 stops over the reference cheek, 0.9 stop across the face, from the side she looks away from); she turns out to the camera and speaks towards it; her hair goes from black to grey inside the shot and her face reads as another woman; the mouth and brows over-act.
2. **The hall is not one room in one light.** The slab is shown at three exposures and as three different stones; her face moves through six stops; the daylight outside falls four stops between the two takes (noon, then dusk); Karst wears four coats; a lit lamp appears that was not in the wide; at 36.04 the two people exchange ends of the hall in one frame.
3. **Nothing has size.** The only wide lasts 2.9 s, from an off-axis coverage angle, of a room with a timber ceiling and a wall 1.8 times her height. The treaty wall is never seen square. The floor of orders, the point of the scene, is never seen lit.
4. **The shutters shot is the best action and still falls short:** Karst fills the opening he makes, so the plain gets 8 % of the frame; then he stands on his mark for 3.2 s; his back is lit from the camera; the sky is clipped paper white.
5. **Looks into the lens in five shots** (the two watchers, Karst in the doorway twice, the end of Karst's turn, Serath's last face). Her last close-up is a centred head on a black void.
6. **The yard still has what the author named:** the aide's fall, her stumble, a rank of men in a row, the frontal man in the doorway (twice), two people turning to the camera; three shots under a second.
7. **The join from the yard is not a match:** her eye lands a fifth of the frame away and the head is tipped about 20 degrees.
8. **What reads as machine-made:** carved marks like Latin capitals that redraw themselves under her finger; a wax seal that comes and goes on the rolled sheet; one repeated frame in four through the lamp shot (measured in the take itself, not the edit).

## What is at the standard

- **32.50, Karst at the opening** (7 to 7.5 from all three): back and shoulder on the right third, a level horizon, the sky five stops over his cheek as Deakins and Fraser set it. Cut before his eyes reach the lens.
- **The design of the profile at the slab** (long lens, raked wall, one finger), **the first second of the shutters** (a black frame, a blade of sky), **the shard** as an insert, **her eyes down and lifting** after it.
- **The yard's two close shots of Serath** (7.83 and 12.54) and the white figure between two dark backs.
- **The camera's behaviour:** locked or barely moving, level.

## What the critics propose

**Free, in the edit and the grade** (A and C each put the hall at about 6 after it):
- Cut out of Serath's turn in under a second, on the turn, and lay "We were chosen." off-screen over Karst's back, so that his turn answers her voice.
- One matching grade for the hall: the slab down to one level, her face and coat brought into one range, Karst's back and the door leaves down two stops in the shutters shot, the sky rolled off, black put back into the shard, the opening in the lamp shot lifted three stops, grain over all.
- Trims: the shutters out at 27.0 s; Karst's turn out before the lens; the last face punched in and out by 46.6 s; the hall wide cropped and run longer; the lamp shot dropped or started late.
- The yard re-cut: no fall, no stumble, no shot under 1.25 s, the doorway once and off-centre.

**New footage, in the order they rank it** (A: the hall can reach 7 to 7.5 with the first two):
1. Serath's turn again: near profile at the dark slab, one cold edge of daylight on one side of the face, a quarter turn toward the light and not toward the camera, lips barely moving.
2. A wide with size: square to the treaty wall, which runs out of the top of the frame, she small with her back to us; the light crosses the wall when the shutters open. Or C's version: from the slab end, down the hall, the daylight running along the floor over row after row of sheets.
3. The lamp shot with one simple action, a flat sheet, the opening bright behind and her whole in it.

## For the next packages

Several of these faults were written into the prompts: the turn "almost square to us", the wide on the reference's own off-axis view, Karst "in the centre" of the shutters, close shots at eye height. The critics' framings (the listener seen from behind her shoulder, the wall square to the lens, the figure beside the opening and a third of the frame high) are the brief for what is made next.
