AUTOPILOT

Written by

Osita Miles

19th October 2026


FADE IN:

INT. DANIEL'S BEDROOM - DAWN

A PHONE ALARM shivers against a bedside table. 6:14 A.M. A hand silences it on the second ring.

On the screen: BATTERY 7%. Beneath it, a clutter of background apps still running.

DANIEL, 38, kind-faced and worn thin by long days, squints at the number. He has the particular tiredness of a parent who has not finished yesterday.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          Have you ever forgotten your phone?

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          I don't mean lost it. Just left it
          somewhere, and carried on as though you
          hadn't.

He turns the phone over in his hand. The other side of the bed is empty. SARAH has already left for work.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          When did you realise?

INT. KITCHEN - MOMENTS LATER

Daniel sets his work bag beside the front door. Usually, his phone goes straight into its outside pocket.

Not today.

He plugs it into a charger beside the kettle instead. The screen lights up with a small, reassuring CHIME.

He glances at the battery icon, satisfied, then heads upstairs.

The phone remains on the counter.

INT. BATHROOM - MORNING

A familiar sequence, almost mechanical: the shower ON, steam on glass, shampoo, rinse, razor, towel, toothbrush, tap OFF.

Daniel doesn't hesitate once. He could perform this morning without looking.

Downstairs, the RADIO is already playing.

                    RADIO PRESENTER (V.O.)
          We're expecting another scorcher today.
          Temperatures will climb well into the
          thirties by lunchtime--

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          You do something often enough, and your
          brain stops asking you to do it. It
          just does it for you.

Daniel hangs the towel on the same hook. Reaches for the same shirt. Follows the same footsteps out of the room.

INT. KITCHEN - LATER

EMILY, 4, all sleepy eyes and uneven socks, studies a piece of toast as if it has personally disappointed her.

Daniel pours cereal, caps a travel mug of coffee, and wipes a little milk from her chin with his thumb.

                    EMILY
          I don't want this bit.

                    DANIEL
          That's the crust.

                    EMILY
          I know what it is.

He cuts the crust off. She rewards him with a solemn nod.

                    EMILY
          Are you getting me today?

                    DANIEL
          Of course. After work.

                    EMILY
          Not late?

He looks at the clock. The morning has begun to get away from him.

                    DANIEL
          Not late. Promise.

He kisses the top of her head. Emily reaches up and straightens his crooked collar, imitating her mother.

                    DANIEL
          Thank you, boss.

Daniel lifts his coffee, wallet, keys and bag in one practiced sweep. He opens the front door with his elbow.

Behind them: the phone on the counter, charging quietly.

EXT. DRIVEWAY - MORNING

Even this early, the air feels baked. Daniel recoils from the HOT STEERING WHEEL and pulls his fingers away.

He opens the rear door and buckles Emily into her child seat, behind the driver's seat. She has a soft rabbit with one ear worn almost flat.

CLICK. The harness fastens. CLICK. The car door closes.

INT. CAR - CONTINUOUS

Sunlight cuts across Emily's face in a hard, white strip. She squints and turns away.

                    EMILY
          Bad sun.

                    DANIEL
          What's it done now?

                    EMILY
          It's in my eyes. It won't let me have
          my little sleep.

Still secured in her seat, Emily leans toward the patch of shade behind Daniel's headrest. From his mirror, her face disappears.

                    DANIEL
          Better?

                    EMILY
          Mm-hmm.

                    DANIEL
          You can sleep a bit. I'll wake you when
          we get there.

He adjusts the rear-view mirror to see the traffic. Starts the engine.

The radio fills the small car. Emily closes her eyes.

EXT. RESIDENTIAL ROADS - MORNING

A sequence of turns Daniel has made hundreds of times. A bakery. Two sets of lights. The crossing near the park. The same impatient van at the same junction.

Daniel takes a sip of coffee. A drop lands on his sleeve. He looks down long enough to wipe it with his thumb.

Through the passenger window, a colourful painted wall slips past, out of focus.

Daniel keeps driving.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          Think about the last journey you make
          every day. What do you actually
          remember of it?

He drums two fingers against the wheel, already thinking about work.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          Usually, just getting there.

EXT. OFFICE CAR PARK - MORNING

8:32 A.M.

Daniel pulls into his usual space. Kills the engine. Takes his work bag and travel mug from the front passenger seat.

He steps out and closes the door. BEEP. The locks engage.

We stay with Daniel as he hurries across the car park and into the building. A glass door sighs shut behind him.

INT. OFFICE - MORNING

Air conditioning. Cool, dry, wonderful air. Daniel loosens his tie in relief.

PRIYA, early 30s, passes his desk carrying a stack of reports.

                    PRIYA
          Morning. It's brutal outside.

                    DANIEL
          Steering wheel nearly took my
          fingerprints off.

                    PRIYA
          How's Emily?

                    DANIEL
          She's fallen out with the sun.

Priya laughs. Daniel smiles at the thought of his daughter.

                    PRIYA
          Report ready?

                    DANIEL
          Two minutes.

He opens his laptop. The smile fades into concentration. Another normal day begins.

INT. CONFERENCE ROOM - LATE MORNING

A projector HUMS. Numbers on a wall. Daniel fields questions and turns pages without once looking at his watch.

                    DANIEL
          The revised figures are on page four.
          If we bring the deadline forward, the
          costs don't change.

Pens move. Heads nod. Someone opens a window; a wave of hot air pours in. It is shut again immediately.

A wall clock moves from 10:18 to 11:43.

INT. OFFICE KITCHENETTE - MIDDAY

The coffee machine stutters. Someone's phone RINGS on a nearby table.

Without thinking, Daniel reaches into the outside pocket of his bag.

His hand closes on nothing.

He tries his jacket. His trouser pocket. Back to the bag. His expression goes blank for one small, ridiculous second.

Then--

FLASH: INT. KITCHEN - MORNING

A charging cable. The glowing battery icon. The phone beside the kettle.

BACK TO SCENE

Daniel exhales through his nose.

                    DANIEL
          Oh, for God's sake.

Priya appears with a fresh mug.

                    PRIYA
          What have you done?

                    DANIEL
          Left my phone at home. Put it on charge
          before my shower.

                    PRIYA
          Need to borrow mine?

                    DANIEL
          No. I'm all right. I know exactly where
          it is.

He almost laughs. One daft mistake. Nothing more.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          I hadn't remembered leaving it. I'd
          remembered only when I reached for it
          and it wasn't there.

INT. KITCHEN - DAY

The empty house. Afternoon light on the counter. A phone attached to its charger, screen black.

Nobody there to hear it. Nobody there to pick it up.

EXT. CITY STREETS - AFTERNOON

The hottest part of the day arrives without mercy.

Heat ripples above the tarmac. A pedestrian crosses from one island of shade to another. A dog lies beneath a bus shelter. A man at a roadside stall pours melting ice into a bucket.

The sun presses against glass and brick and metal. No wind moves.

INT. OFFICE - AFTERNOON

A quiet, refrigerated world behind sealed windows. The air conditioner HUMS steadily.

Daniel signs a form. Files an email. Ticks a box. Another meeting. Another cup of coffee.

On his desk sits a small photograph: Sarah and Emily, both laughing at something just outside the frame.

He straightens the photograph so it faces him. Then returns to his work.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          The strange thing was, I didn't feel
          anything missing. The day had gone as
          it always did. My brain had filled in
          the rest.

INT. OFFICE - EARLY EVENING

5:37 P.M.

Daniel sends the final email. The outgoing message CHIMES. He shuts the laptop and looks across a half-empty office.

                    PRIYA
          Same time tomorrow?

                    DANIEL
          Unfortunately.

He picks up the bag. Keys. Wallet. Coffee mug. The objects arrange themselves in his hands just as they did that morning.

He leaves without thinking about the route home.

EXT. OFFICE CAR PARK - EVENING

The low sun turns every windscreen copper. Daniel reaches his car and opens the driver's door.

A dense wave of trapped heat hits him. He recoils.

And a strange smell. Sour. Wrong. He pauses, unable to place it.

He looks down at the empty coffee mug, checks the front passenger footwell. Nothing.

                    DANIEL
          Jesus.

He lowers both front windows. Starts the engine. The vents cough a weak breath of hot air before the air conditioning catches.

He drives out of the car park.

INT. CAR - MOVING - EVENING

Traffic drifts through the heat. Daniel loosens his collar and keeps the radio low.

                    RADIO PRESENTER (V.O.)
          ...the hottest day of the year so far.
          Temperatures are expected to ease
          overnight--

Daniel turns it down another notch. He stares at the road, lost in the exhausted quiet that follows a working day.

No voice comes from behind him.

EXT. DANIEL'S HOUSE - EVENING

Tyres crunch across the driveway gravel. The air has finally begun to cool.

Daniel steps out. SARAH, 36, still in her work clothes, is waiting in the doorway. Her expression is ordinary, even warm--until she looks past him.

                    SARAH
          Where's Emily?

Daniel stops. His keys hang from one finger.

                    DANIEL
          What?

                    SARAH
          Emily. Where is she?

His face drains.

                    DANIEL
          The nursery. Oh, Christ. I-- I didn't
          pick her up.

                    SARAH
          Daniel--

But he's already back in the car. The engine roars awake. Sarah runs down the steps as he reverses out.

EXT. NURSERY - DUSK

Daniel brakes outside a low brick building, bright animals painted along its outer wall. The same colours that blurred past his window this morning.

The playground is silent. The last of the evening sun stretches the swings' shadows across the tarmac.

He hurries to the main entrance, straightening his collar, already rehearsing an apology.

                    DANIEL
          I was caught at work. I lost track of--
          No. My phone was--

He stops.

The front door is boarded with a sheet of plywood. Fresh scratches score the lock. A notice is taped at eye level.

INSERT - THE NOTICE

"DUE TO VANDALISM OVERNIGHT, PLEASE USE THE SIDE ENTRANCE. TODAY ONLY."

BACK TO SCENE

Daniel stares at the words.

The front door was already damaged this morning. It had to have been. If he had come here, he would have used the side entrance.

But he doesn't remember the side entrance.

He doesn't remember handing Emily to a member of staff. Her coat. Her rabbit. A goodbye. Nothing.

A low, thin RINGING begins, though there is no sound around him.

                    DANIEL
          No.

FLASH CUTS - THIS MORNING

-- A phone plugged in beside the kettle.

-- Emily turning her face away from the bad sun.

-- Her head disappearing behind the driver's seat.

-- The colourful nursery wall slipping past the car window.

-- Daniel wiping coffee from his sleeve. Keeping the wheel straight.

-- His car pulling into the OFFICE car park.

-- The small, friendly BEEP of the doors locking.

BACK TO SCENE

Daniel turns, slowly, toward his car.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          My phone was on the counter.

His eyes go to the dark rear passenger window. From where he stands, he can see only his own reflection.

                    DANIEL
          Emily?

No answer.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          My phone was on the counter.

He runs.

EXT. NURSERY CAR PARK - CONTINUOUS

Daniel yanks the rear door handle. LOCKED.

His thumb struggles over the remote. The car answers with the same polite BEEP it made nine hours ago.

He pulls the door open.

We do not see inside.

We see DANIEL. His eyes settle on something below frame. For a moment his face seems not to understand what it is seeing.

Then it does.

His breath catches and stays caught. One hand reaches into the car. He makes a small, broken sound--the beginning of his daughter's name, with nowhere to go.

The world falls SILENT. Even the evening traffic disappears.

Daniel drops to his knees beside the open door.

QUICK FLASH - INT. KITCHEN - MORNING

The phone on the counter. The charging symbol pulses, green and peaceful.

BACK TO DANIEL

He is still kneeling there, one arm extended into the car. The sun sinks out of the glass.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          My phone was on the counter.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          And my daughter was dead.

A long, terrible stillness.

                    DANIEL (V.O.)
          Autopilot disengaged.

                                        FADE OUT.

                                        THE END
