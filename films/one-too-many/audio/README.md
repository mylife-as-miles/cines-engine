# Sound

The soundtrack is built with [opus-sound-layer](https://github.com/Bodila51/opus-sound-layer). It is real CC0 recordings placed from the film's own timing, mixed and then measured.

## How it is built

1. **Recordings.** Every sound was searched for on Freesound with a CC0 filter (`tools/freesound.py`). Openverse indexes the same recordings, but its API timed out from the build machine. Each candidate was cut and judged by the sound layer's own `sfx.py` checks (`tools/prep_sounds.py`). Organic sounds such as the scream, laughter and chair drags were trimmed whole (`tools/trim_sound.py`), and ambiences were made into seamless loops (`tools/make_loop.py`). Every pick was checked on waveform and spectrogram sheets. The chosen files are in `sfx/` and `beds/`, with credits in `CREDITS.md`.
2. **Beds.** The engine (`film/sound.js`) plays the recorded fire, walla, gas, siren and heartbeats through each scene's existing envelopes (`samples.js`, made by `tools/samples.py`). `tools/calibrate.js` matches them to the levels of the synthesised beds they replaced. The score (the 7th-harmonic drone, the dread pad, the party e-piano and the unison) is still synthesised, along with the tinnitus, the phone tones and the counting pings. Run `node tools/stem.js out/bed.wav` to render all of that as one stem.
3. **One-shots.** `node tools/score.js` dumps every cue the sequences schedule. `python3 tools/cuesheet.py out/score.json out/bed.wav audio/cues.json --kit audio/kit` then turns those cues into the sound layer's cue sheet, using the picks in `film/sfx_map.js`. No time is typed by hand. Two story beats are played by the engine at the film's designed level instead (the scream and the kitchen crashes), because the mixer's "just audible" levelling would bury them.
4. **Mix.** Build the library with `uv run sound-layer/scripts/sfx.py kit` (it goes to `audio/kit`, which is not committed). Then run `uv run sound-layer/scripts/mix.py audio/cues.json --out out/audio`. The mixer places each sound's peak on its cue, sets each level against the bed in its own band, adds one shared room, and masters to -18 LUFS with a -2 dBTP limiter.
5. **QA.** Run `uv run sound-layer/scripts/qa.py out/one_too_many.mp4 --audio out/audio`. The reports are in `reports/`.

## Score (Lyria 3.5)

The music is generated by Google's Lyria 3.5 (`lyria-3.5`, plus `lyria-3-clip-preview` for four short cues) through the Gemini API. `SCORE.md` is the score plan, made from `score_plan.json`: the sound world, five motifs, the keys, the 19 cues with their times and jobs, and every prompt. It was spotted from the film's own timing (`node tools/spot.js out/spot`) by six spotters, unified by a lead composer, reviewed by a story critic and a technical critic, and then finalised. The Extra's drone, the final unison, the counting pings, the tinnitus and the heartbeats stay synthesised as story devices. The score sits with the drone on A and never plays the drone's flat G.

1. `python3 tools/lyria.py` generates the cues into `score/`, one request each (about $0.08 a cue). Google's filter blocked two prompts (M03, M10d); both went through after neutral rewording, and the plan records the change.
2. `<earshot venv>/bin/python tools/score_check.py` listens to every take: its description, any singing or drum tags, a match against its brief, and its tuning offset from A440 (`score/check.json`). All 19 matched their brief (86% to over 99%) with no vocal tags. Their tuning ranged from 0 to +36 cents.
3. `<earshot venv>/bin/python tools/score_window.py` picks where each cue starts in its take (`score/windows.json`). Lyria writes about a minute whatever length is asked for and does not keep the prompt's timestamps; M01's piano count, for example, came at 0:40 rather than 0:04.
4. `node tools/stem.js out/bed_s.wav --score` renders the bed stem with every synth bed a generated cue replaces left out whole.
5. `uv run tools/score_place.py out/score.json out/bed_s.wav out/bed_scored.wav` starts each cue at its window, retunes it to A440, high-passes the underscore at 75 Hz (the 55 Hz fundamental is the drone's), levels, filters and fades it, places it on the film's clock, and follows the film's ducks and the Extra's holes.
6. `python3 tools/cuesheet.py out/score.json out/bed_scored.wav audio/cues.json --kit audio/kit`, then mix, mux and QA as above.

## The Dispatcher

The 999 Dispatcher is the film's only spoken voice; the friends speak in type. `python3 tools/dispatcher_tts.py out/spot` voices the nine lines verbatim with `gemini-3.8-flash-tts`, in a British voice from Google's Extended Voice Library (`en-gb-concierge-5`, Winchester, chosen by ear from an audition). Each line carries its own direction in a `speech_metadata` annotation, so the direction is never spoken. `python3 tools/voice_place.py` puts the lines on a phone line (300 Hz to 3.4 kHz, light compression and grit). The cue sheet hands them to the mixer as its voice track, each starting as its line appears on screen, and the music ducks under them. earshot hears speech (AudioSet "Speech" 0.82), but in a forced choice it rates every voice tried nearer "a robotic text-to-speech voice" than "a woman speaking calmly". That is a warning to judge by ear.

## No crowd

The party's crowd walla read as people chanting, so it is out of the film, along with the group laughter (`beds_off` and `"none"` in `film/sfx_map.js`). The record and the score carry the room. earshot's best match for "people chanting" in the mix fell from z +4.6 to +2.5, and those weak matches sit in the kitchen score rather than in any crowd. The gasp, the scream, the sob and the cough stay as story beats.

## The tension layer (Stable Audio)

`tools/tension.py` makes 11 designed sounds with Stable Audio 3.0 Small SFX (Stability AI Community License: free under $1M annual revenue). It runs locally through [Soundbox](https://github.com/HamSanders/soundbox) and an audio.cpp engine built from source for the CPU, at about a minute per sound with no key and no credits.
- **Inhales:** three variants pull the room inward just before each of the film's 8 hard silences.
- **Pressure and swells:** sub pressure into the smash cut, an eerie swell into the second doorbell, and a final swell into the last silence.
- **Hits and textures:** a dark impact at the let-go, a camera-flash whine at the photo, a blade ring at the knife, a gas riser into the explosion, and the house groaning as it collapses.

earshot checked each one (`tension/check.json`). Ten passed first time; the flash read as "an explosion" and was regenerated with a sharper prompt (now strong, over 99%). In the mix, 11 of the 16 placements stand out in their band; the other 5 sit just under, as whispered build-ups.

## Listening with earshot

[earshot](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/earshot) gives an agent ears: it describes a sound in words and says how well it matches a description. `tools/listen.py` runs it on six moments of the final mix, and the result is `reports/earshot_listen.json`. All six match what they should be ("strong"): the cold-open fire, the doorbell, the party, the kitchen crash, the scream and the explosion.

It also changed two decisions:
- **The explosion.** In the mix, the tinnitus ring was taking over the blast. The library blast that earshot ranked best on its own scored worse inside the mix (82% "explosion", 18% "bicycle bell"), so the original layering stayed (97%).
- **The scream.** It is the best of the five recordings for "a woman screaming in terror" (z +6.6). Inside the mix it still reads harsh and tonal: the recording rings at about 1.1 kHz over the drone.

- **The title typing.** Under "A FILM BY OSITA MILES" it read as footsteps (64%): three key hits 0.15 s apart. It is now one 0.85 s burst cut from a real typewriter recording, the one earshot ranked best for "fast typing on an old mechanical typewriter" (z +5.1). It reads 83% "typewriter keys typing".

earshot, on the final mix: the fire, the party, the crash, the scream and the explosion all match "strong". The first doorbell reads 63% "doorbell" (37% "microwave beep"), though the mixer measures it 12.5 dB clear. The score reads clearly in the hand circle (M08a, over 99% "tense chamber strings") and in the kitchen (M10a, 84%). In the cold open (M01) and the phone call (M11b) the score sits pianissimo under the fire, the siren and the voice, and earshot does not hear it as music even after a 4 dB lift. Judge those two by ear.

The cue sheet was revised twice against these checks: one landing per line instead of one per word, card flicks merged into the slap that follows, heartbeats layered with a body thud, and brighter hero hits. That is the limit the sound layer's prompt allows. `LISTEN.md` lists five moments to judge by ear.
