# The Prize That Became a Prison: script, version 3 ("The Held Sentence")

Written 28 November 2026. It replaces version 2 as the working script (design/script/SCRIPT_V2.md stays for reference). Of the three story architectures judged (mystery, wait, device), this one is built on "device": Tim Hunt's real recorded testimony is the spine of the film. It carries the grafts the judges recommended. From "wait" it takes the father thread: the 494 days, spoken once; "no father to spare"; Tim in Gene's seat; the two clock sounds. From "mystery" it takes the statue-to-real match cuts, the plain closed folder and "It won't be the last time". It also makes every must-fix change the judges named. Every fact, date, number and attribution comes from S1-S9 or the S5 hearing transcript, through the production script's fact base. The list at the bottom gives each one with its line.

**Length: about 13:40 at the measured pace.** The narrator reads 1,148 words: 165 words a minute in the cold open, 155 in chapters 01-04, 150 in 05-06, 145 in 07, 135 in 08 and 128 in 09. Tim's real voice adds about 4:20 (2024 at his measured 151 words a minute, 2026 at about 125). The holds, silences and breaths are counted. The scratch narrator of the current cut ran faster than planned (EDL: 12:09 against a 12:55 plan), so the cut should land between 13:00 and 13:40. If it runs over 14:00, the trim list at the bottom takes out 25 s without touching a loop.

## Why this keeps viewers

**The device: the held sentence.**
- **One sentence is held open for eleven minutes.** The cold open ends on Tim's real 2026 voice in front of the commission: "And she keeps asking me the same question." On "question" the frame freezes. The viewer does not hear what she asks until 11:59, when the same frozen frame starts moving again and he finishes: "'When?' She wants a date... 'I can't give you a date.'"
- **His sentences hinge four chapter breaks, and one more inside chapter 07.** At each hinge his voice stops one word short of the next part of the story. On real footage the frame freezes. Over a statue plate, his last words stay alone in the story type. Each hold has 1.2 s of digital silence, and the next shot opens on his next syllable: "started to trust—", "she also won—", "waiting to board her—", "received the—", "because—".
- **Three rules keep it honest without labels.**
  1. *Order.* Within each hearing, his statement plays in the order he spoke it. Sentences may be skipped, but they are never moved and never spliced. Only the cold open flashes forward.
  2. *Picture.* Where a camera was (the hearings, published photographs, published pages), the viewer sees the real thing. Where no camera was (the airport, the trip, detention, the prison, the Hunt home), the viewer sees statues. Tim's statue appears once, at 0:52, and match-cuts into the real man.
  3. *The relay.* Dawn is heard only as her brother reported her: her "yes", her letter, her "When?" and her "false hopes". The film says so at 12:55.
- **Why it holds.**
  - An unfinished sentence in a real man's voice is an itch the viewer stays to scratch.
  - His 2024 voice already knows the ending while the 2014 statues walk toward it ("Once again, she agreed"). Every yes in the first half is a step toward the room the cold open has already shown.
  - Each time his voice returns, it proves again that this is someone's brother, not a story.
  - The attribution the facts demand ("he wrote", "she told me") stops being a hedge and becomes the form.
  - The film ends when his testimony ends: "Thank you for hearing me." No narrator speaks after him, and there is no moral.
