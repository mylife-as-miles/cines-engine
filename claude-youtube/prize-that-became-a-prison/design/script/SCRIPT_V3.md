From "mystery" it takes the statue-to-real match cuts and the plain closed folder.

## Why this keeps viewers

**The device: the held sentence.**
- **One sentence is held open for eleven minutes.** The cold open ends on Tim's real 2026 voice in front of the commission: "And she keeps asking me the same question." The film stops him on the full stop and the frame freezes. The viewer does not hear what she asks until 12:02, when the same frozen frame starts moving again and he goes on: "'When?' She wants a date... 'I can't give you a date.'" (Gate G1: those words are not yet heard on a recording. If they were not spoken, fallback B keeps the question held with a line we do hold; it is written into 0:58 and 12:02.)
- **His statement hinges four chapter breaks, and one more inside chapter 07.** At each hinge the film stops him where his own sentence stops or turns, and the next shot opens on his next sentence or clause. On real footage the frame freezes. Over a statue plate, his last words stay alone in the story type. Each hold has 1.2 s of digital silence. The held words: "treated her well", "some designer purses", "her Australia flight", "a lot of people" (a freeze), "her physical appearance".
- **The rule for every hold.** The film stops him only at a full stop or a clause break, or at a pause the audio shows; never inside a phrase. The held words carry no dash and no dots, and the stop reads as an edit (a dead frame, or his words alone in type over digital silence): it is the film holding him, never Tim faltering.
- **Three rules keep it honest without labels.**
  1. *Order.* Within each hearing, his statement plays in the order he spoke it. Sentences may be skipped, but they are never moved and never spliced, and a hold only pauses a sentence where it already breaks. Only the cold open flashes forward. The two NBC Chicago interviews are separate recordings and sit where their subject belongs.
  2. *Picture.* Where a camera was (the hearings, the two NBC Chicago interviews, published photographs, published pages), the viewer sees the real thing. Where no camera was (the airport, the trip, detention, the prison, the Hunt home), the viewer sees statues. A statue never stands where a camera was, except Tim's at 0:54, which match-cuts into the real man. Tim's and Gene's statues appear only in places no camera saw: the family table and the visiting room.
  3. *The relay.* Dawn is heard only as her family reported her: her "yes", her letter, her "When?" and her "false hopes" through her brother; her "I checked on the internet" through her father. The film says so at 13:02.
- **Why it holds.**
  - An unfinished question in a real man's voice is an itch the viewer stays to scratch.
  - His 2024 voice already knows the ending while the 2014 statues walk toward it ("Once again, she agreed"). Every yes in the first half is a step toward the room the cold open has already shown.
  - Each time his voice returns, it proves again that this is someone's brother, not a story. Her father's voice does the same, twice: once near the start, once just before he dies.
  - The attribution the facts demand ("he wrote", "she told me", "in her account") stops being a hedge and becomes the form.
  - The film ends on his own recorded words: "It has been 12 years. 12 years." No narrator speaks after him, and there is no moral.
