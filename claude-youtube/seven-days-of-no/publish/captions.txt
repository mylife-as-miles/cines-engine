You say yes to everything.
Staying late, lending money,
going to the thing / you did not want to go to.
Yes, yes, yes.
Even when your whole body is saying no.
So here is the question.
What happens if, for seven days, / you say no to everyone?
Not rude, not angry, just no.
Let's see.
Monday, 5:30. Your boss walks over.
"Can you stay a bit late tonight?"
Normally you say, "Of course," / before he finishes the sentence.
Today you say, "No."
He blinks.
Then he says, "Okay."
That's it. "Okay."
You prepared for war and got a shrug.
Tuesday. Your friend calls.
"Can you help me move on Saturday?"
"No."
And you hear it.
That small pause.
The one that says, / "I thought we were close."
Now your stomach drops.
Nobody did anything to you / and you still feel guilty.
Remember that feeling.
It comes back later.
Wednesday, your mom calls.
"Are you coming for Sunday lunch?"
"No."
She does not argue.
She says, "Fine."
In a voice that is not fine.
Then she calls again. And again.
By evening, you have nine missed calls / and one text.
"Are you alive?"
Day three. The world has not ended.
But your phone is exhausted.
Thursday, a coworker pops by.
"Lunch? That new place just opened."
"No."
And the second it leaves your mouth, / you know.
You wanted to go.
You have been wanting to try / that place for weeks.
Here is the problem / with saying no to everything.
You also say no / to the things you actually want.
Friday. A tourist asks, / "Sorry, could you take our photo?"
"No."
They look hurt.
And you feel terrible.
So you run after them.
You take the photo,
and it is the happiest / you have been all week.
Why? / Because this time, you chose it.
Same yes, different reason.
Not fear, choice.
Saturday, your boss texts, / "Need that report tonight."
You do not say no.
You do not panic yes, either.
You say, "I can't tonight. / I can send it first thing tomorrow."
He says, "Perfect."
That is the one that works.
Not a flat no. A real answer.
Sunday. So what happened?
A few people were annoyed.
One went quiet for a day.
And one or two actually / respected you more.
Nobody's life ended.
Here's what I noticed.
Most of our yeses are not kindness.
They are fear.
Fear that people will stop liking us.
That guilty feeling on Tuesday.
It was not proof you did something wrong.
It was just what changing / a habit feels like.
So, do not say no to everyone.
Try this instead.
For one week, when someone asks you / for something, say,
"Let me think and get back to you." / That is all.
It gives you a pause, / and in that pause, you get to choose.
Yes, no, or "yes, but not like that."
You decide on purpose.
So, what if you said no to everyone / for seven days?
You would be tired, a little guilty, / and a lot more honest.
Now tell me in the comments: / what is one thing you said yes to
this week that you really / wanted to say no to?
I'll go first.
I said no to food because / I honestly do not want it.
See you in the next one.
